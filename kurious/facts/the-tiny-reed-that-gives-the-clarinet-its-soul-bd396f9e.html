The Tiny Reed That Gives the Clarinet Its Soul

A sliver of cane thinner than a credit card creates one of music's most expressive voices by splitting air thousands of times per second.

When a clarinetist blows into their instrument, nothing happens inside the mouthpiece that you'd expect. The air doesn't simply flow through like wind in a pipe. Instead, a thin piece of Arundo donax—a Mediterranean grass—flutters against the mouthpiece opening at astonishing speed, chopping the airstream into pulses that become sound waves.
