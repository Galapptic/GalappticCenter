The Great Eskimo Snow Hoax That Fooled Linguists for Decades

The famous claim that Inuit languages have dozens of words for snow started not with anthropologists studying Arctic cultures, but with a casual exaggeration that snowballed through academia and advertising.

You've probably heard it at a dinner party, read it in a magazine, or seen it in an advertisement: the Inuit have fifty words for snow. Or a hundred. Or four hundred, depending on who's telling the story. It's become one of those facts that feels profound—proof that language shapes how we see the world, that cultures closest to nature understand it in ways we've forgotten. There's just one problem. It isn't true. And tracing how this myth spread reveals something far more interesting about language, culture, and the stories we want to believe.
