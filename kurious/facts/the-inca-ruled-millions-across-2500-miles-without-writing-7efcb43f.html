The Inca Ruled Millions Across 2500 Miles Without Writing

While Europeans filled libraries with books, the Inca administered the largest empire in pre-Columbian America using only knotted strings.

By the early 1500s, the Inca Empire stretched from modern-day Colombia to Chile, encompassing roughly 12 million people across some of the world's most challenging terrain. Yet they achieved this without what we'd consider essential for civilization: a written language. No scrolls, no books, no inscriptions on monuments. Instead, they developed an incredibly sophisticated system of governance through oral tradition, standardized roads, and a remarkable invention called the quipu. This network of knotted cords served as their record-keeping system, allowing administrators to track everything from population counts to tribute payments across thousands of miles of Andean mountains.
