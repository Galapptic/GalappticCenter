The Fungus That Made Medieval Europe Go Mad

For centuries, entire villages would suddenly descend into mass hallucinations, convulsions, and gangrene—and nobody knew the rye bread was to blame.

In 994 CE, roughly 40,000 people died in Aquitaine, France. Survivors described burning sensations in their limbs, violent convulsions, and terrifying visions. Some lost fingers, toes, even entire limbs to a creeping black rot. Priests called it "holy fire" or "St. Anthony's Fire," and blamed demonic possession. The real culprit was sitting on their dinner tables: bread made from contaminated rye.
