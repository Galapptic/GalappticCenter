The Eerie Trick That Makes Pure Water Freeze on Command

A bottle of water can stay liquid far below zero degrees—until you tap it, and it turns to ice before your eyes.

In 2011, a YouTube video went viral showing a man pulling a bottle of water from his freezer, giving it a sharp tap, and watching as ice crystals raced through the liquid in seconds. Commenters accused him of camera tricks. But there was no illusion—just physics. The water had been supercooled, held in a precarious liquid state well below its normal freezing point. One small disturbance was all it took to trigger an instantaneous transformation into solid ice.

Supercooling sounds like a laboratory curiosity, but it happens constantly in nature. It shapes the clouds above our heads, the ice storms that down power lines, and the survival strategies of Arctic fish and insects. Understanding why water resists freezing—and what finally tips it over—reveals something profound about how matter changes from one state to another.
