The Paradox That Proves You Cannot Define a Heap

Remove one grain of sand from a pile and it's still a pile—keep going, and philosophy breaks.

A heap of sand sits before you. Remove one grain. Still a heap? Of course. Remove another. Still a heap. Keep going, grain by grain, and at some point you're staring at a single grain of sand. That's not a heap. But when exactly did the heap stop being a heap? If removing one grain never makes the difference, how did you get here? This isn't a trick question or a riddle with a clever answer. It's a genuine logical problem that has tormented philosophers for over two thousand years—and it reaches far beyond piles of sand.
