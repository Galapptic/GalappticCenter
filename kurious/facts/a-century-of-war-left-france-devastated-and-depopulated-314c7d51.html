A Century of War Left France Devastated and Depopulated

For 116 years, English armies repeatedly invaded France, reducing its population by half and turning the most prosperous kingdom in Europe into a wasteland.

The Hundred Years' War wasn't one continuous conflict but a series of devastating campaigns between 1337 and 1453. English kings claimed the French throne, and their armies repeatedly crossed the Channel to press that claim with fire and sword. France bore the terrible cost. English forces used a strategy called chevauchée, essentially organized destruction, burning villages, slaughtering livestock, and terrorizing civilians. Major battles like Crécy and Agincourt saw French knights annihilated by English longbowmen. At its worst, English-controlled territory stretched from Normandy to Bordeaux, with Paris itself falling under English occupation. France faced complete collapse.
