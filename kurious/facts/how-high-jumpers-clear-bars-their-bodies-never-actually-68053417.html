How High Jumpers Clear Bars Their Bodies Never Actually Cross

The Fosbury Flop works because physics allows your body to go over a bar while your center of gravity passes underneath it.

Dick Fosbury looked ridiculous. At the 1968 Mexico City Olympics, while every other high jumper attacked the bar face-first with a scissor kick or straddle, the lanky Oregonian sprinted in on a curve, leaped, and flopped over the bar backward with his spine arched like a bow. Commentators were baffled. Coaches called it dangerous. Then Fosbury cleared 2.24 meters, won gold, and changed the sport forever.

What Fosbury had stumbled onto—he developed the technique in high school by instinct, not calculation—was a loophole in the physics of jumping. The Flop allowed athletes to clear bars that their center of gravity never actually crossed.
