Why the Law Forbids Trying You Twice for the Same Crime

A rule designed to stop medieval kings from harassing enemies now protects millions of defendants worldwide—but it has loopholes that surprise even lawyers.

In 1996, O.J. Simpson walked out of a Los Angeles courtroom acquitted of murdering his ex-wife Nicole Brown Simpson and Ron Goldman. The verdict outraged millions. But no matter how strong the evidence seemed, prosecutors could never charge him again for those deaths. The principle behind that protection—double jeopardy—is one of the oldest in Western law, and it still shapes criminal justice in ways that confuse, frustrate, and sometimes save lives.
