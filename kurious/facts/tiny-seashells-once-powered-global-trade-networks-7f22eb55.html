Tiny Seashells Once Powered Global Trade Networks

Before coins existed, billions of small mollusc shells created one of history's most enduring monetary systems spanning two continents.

Long before paper bills or metal coins dominated commerce, cowrie shells served as legitimate currency across vast stretches of Africa and Asia. These small, glossy shells from the Cypraea moneta species were collected primarily from the Maldives and East African coasts. Their natural durability, uniform size, and impossibility to counterfeit made them ideal money. You couldn't fake a cowrie—nature controlled the mint. From ancient China around 1200 BCE through 20th-century West Africa, these shells purchased everything from everyday goods to enslaved people. They were strung together, counted carefully, and accepted by merchants across thousands of miles of trade routes.
