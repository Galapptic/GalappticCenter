Ancient Legal Magic Words Can Force Any Jailer to Explain Themselves

A Latin phrase from 1215 remains the only thing standing between you and vanishing into a government dungeon forever.

Habeas corpus literally means "you shall have the body" in Latin. It's a court order demanding that whoever holds a prisoner must physically bring them before a judge and explain why they're detained. No vague accusations, no indefinite holding—you get your day in court. This seemingly simple requirement is revolutionary. Without it, governments could simply make people disappear. Secret arrests, hidden prisons, forgotten prisoners—habeas corpus makes all of that illegal. When you file for a "writ of habeas corpus," you're essentially forcing the state to justify itself. The burden shifts to authorities to prove they have legitimate reasons to hold someone, not on the prisoner to prove innocence.
