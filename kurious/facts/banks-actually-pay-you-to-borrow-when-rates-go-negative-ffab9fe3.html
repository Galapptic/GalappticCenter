Banks Actually Pay You to Borrow When Rates Go Negative

In a financial twilight zone, borrowers earn money while savers watch their deposits slowly shrink.

Normally, you pay interest when borrowing money—that's how banks profit. But negative interest rates flip this logic entirely. When central banks push rates below zero, commercial banks essentially get charged for parking their money. This bizarre incentive trickles down: some mortgage holders in Denmark actually received monthly payments from their banks instead of making them. The idea sounds like financial fantasy, but it became reality across Europe and Japan after 2014. Central banks deploy this unconventional weapon when traditional rate cuts to zero aren't enough to stimulate spending. They're essentially penalizing saving and rewarding borrowing to force money into circulation.
