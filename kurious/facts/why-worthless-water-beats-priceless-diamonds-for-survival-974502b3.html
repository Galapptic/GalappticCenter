Why Worthless Water Beats Priceless Diamonds for Survival

A glass of water keeps you alive, yet costs nothing—while a useless sparkly rock can cost more than a house.

Here's a question that tormented philosophers for centuries: water is essential for life, yet it's practically free. Diamonds serve no survival purpose whatsoever, yet people pay fortunes for them. This seems completely backward. Shouldn't the things we need most cost the most? This puzzle, known as the paradox of value, confused thinkers from Plato to Adam Smith. It seemed to prove that economics made no sense. How could markets be so irrational? The answer revolutionized how we understand value itself—and it has nothing to do with how useful something is. It's about something far more psychological: the next sip versus the next sparkle.
