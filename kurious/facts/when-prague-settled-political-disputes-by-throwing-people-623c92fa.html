When Prague Settled Political Disputes by Throwing People Out Windows

The word 'defenestration' exists in multiple languages specifically because Prague officials kept getting hurled from windows—and one incident sparked a war that killed eight million people.

On May 23, 1618, a group of Protestant nobles marched into Prague Castle, seized two Catholic governors and their secretary, and threw all three out of a window seventy feet above the ground. Remarkably, all three survived. Catholics claimed angels had caught them; Protestants pointed out they'd landed in a pile of manure. Either way, this act of political violence—the Third Defenestration of Prague—ignited the Thirty Years' War, one of the deadliest conflicts in European history.

But here's the strange thing: this wasn't even Prague's first defenestration. Or its second. The city had such a habit of resolving political crises by throwing officials out of windows that it gave the world a word. 'Defenestration' comes from the Latin de (out of) and fenestra (window). It exists in Czech, German, French, and English because Prague kept doing it.
