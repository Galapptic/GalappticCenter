The Snow Woman Who Walks Without Leaving a Trace

In Japanese folklore, one spirit is so weightless and otherworldly that she glides barefoot across deep snow without disturbing a single flake.

She appears on the coldest nights of winter, when blizzards howl through mountain villages and travelers lose their way. Her skin is pale as fresh powder, her kimono white as ice. She moves silently across the frozen landscape, her bare feet touching the snow but never breaking its surface. When morning comes, searchers find no trail—just the bodies of those who froze in the night, their faces locked in expressions of terror or, strangely, peace.

This is Yuki-onna, the Snow Woman of Japanese folklore, a spirit whose impossible footprints—or rather, the complete absence of them—have haunted winter tales for at least five centuries.
