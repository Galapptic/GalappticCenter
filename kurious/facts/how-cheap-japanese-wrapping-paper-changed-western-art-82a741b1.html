How Cheap Japanese Wrapping Paper Changed Western Art Forever

The prints that transformed Impressionism and inspired Van Gogh were originally used to wrap fragile cargo shipped from Japan.

In 1856, French artist Félix Bracquemond was rummaging through a shipment of Japanese ceramics when something caught his eye. The porcelain was cushioned with crumpled paper printed with bold images—figures, landscapes, birds. It was packaging material, essentially Japanese newspaper. Bracquemond smoothed out the sheets and realized he was looking at something extraordinary: woodblock prints by Katsushika Hokusai. Within a decade, these disposable wrappers would help dismantle 400 years of European artistic tradition.
