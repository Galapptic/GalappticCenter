Gymnasts Can Start Twisting Mid-Air Without Pushing Off Anything

By exploiting a loophole in physics, gymnasts can begin rotating in a completely new direction while already airborne—something that seems to violate the laws of motion.

Watch a gymnast launch off a vault or floor, and you'll often see them start twisting partway through their flight—even though nothing touched them. This looks like it breaks physics. After all, once you're airborne, you can't change your total angular momentum. Yet gymnasts routinely initiate twists from scratch. The secret? They're not creating rotation from nothing—they're redistributing it. By asymmetrically moving their arms or tilting their body segments, they transfer existing somersault rotation into twist rotation. It's like a falling cat righting itself. The total spin stays constant, but where that spin goes can shift dramatically through clever body manipulation.
