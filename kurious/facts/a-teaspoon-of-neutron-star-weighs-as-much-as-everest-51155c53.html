A Teaspoon of Neutron Star Weighs as Much as Everest

When massive stars die, they compress so violently that a sugar-cube-sized chunk of their corpse would outweigh Earth's tallest mountain.

Imagine scooping up a teaspoon of material that weighs six trillion kilograms—roughly the mass of Mount Everest. That's the reality inside a neutron star, the collapsed core of a massive star that has exploded as a supernova. These stellar remnants pack our Sun's worth of mass into a sphere just 20 kilometers across, about the size of a city. The density is so extreme that atoms themselves cannot survive. Electrons and protons are crushed together to form neutrons, creating matter unlike anything we can produce on Earth. If you could somehow transport a teaspoon to our planet, it would punch straight through the ground like a cosmic bullet.
