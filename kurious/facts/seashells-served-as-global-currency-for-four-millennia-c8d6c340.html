Seashells Served as Global Currency for Four Millennia

Long before gold coins or paper bills, tiny ocean shells from the Maldives became the most widely used currency in human history, spanning Africa, Asia, and Europe.

Cowrie shells weren't just pretty trinkets—they were serious money. These small, glossy shells from the money cowrie species served as currency across Africa, Asia, and parts of Europe for over 4,000 years. They had everything good money needs: they're durable, portable, hard to counterfeit, and naturally limited in supply. Most came from the Maldives Islands, where collectors gathered them from shallow waters. From there, traders shipped them across the Indian Ocean and overland through ancient trade routes. In China, the character for money still contains the symbol for shell. In West Africa, cowries remained legal tender well into the twentieth century. No other currency has ever achieved such geographic reach or temporal longevity.
