Wasabi Attacks Your Nose While Hot Peppers Burn Your Tongue

That sinus-clearing blast from wasabi comes from a completely different chemical weapon than the one chili peppers use.

When you eat a jalapeño, capsaicin binds to heat receptors on your tongue, creating that familiar burning sensation. Wasabi plays by entirely different rules. The green paste releases compounds called isothiocyanates, particularly allyl isothiocyanate, which vaporize and travel up through your nasal passages. That's why wasabi hits your sinuses like a freight train while hot peppers attack your mouth. The sensation is sharp, intense, and mercifully brief—usually fading within seconds. Capsaicin, meanwhile, lingers stubbornly. This fundamental difference explains why drinking water helps with neither: capsaicin is oil-soluble, and isothiocyanates are already airborne and irritating your nasal cavity.
