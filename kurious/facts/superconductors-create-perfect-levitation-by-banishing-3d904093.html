Superconductors Create Perfect Levitation by Banishing Magnetic Fields

When cooled to extreme temperatures, certain materials become so hostile to magnetism that they forcefully eject every magnetic field line, causing magnets to hover in mid-air with zero wobble.

Superconductors are materials that, when chilled to extreme cold, conduct electricity with absolutely zero resistance. But their strangest trick isn't about electricity—it's about magnetism. Place a magnet above a superconductor, and it floats. Not shakily, not briefly, but perfectly still, indefinitely. This happens because superconductors actively expel all magnetic fields from their interior, a phenomenon called the Meissner effect. The magnetic field lines can't penetrate the material, so they bend around it, creating an invisible cushion that suspends the magnet in space. Unlike electromagnetic levitation, which requires constant power adjustments, this levitation is passive and eerily stable.
