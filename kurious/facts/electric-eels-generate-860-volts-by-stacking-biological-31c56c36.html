Electric eels generate 860 volts by stacking biological batteries

A single electric eel can produce enough voltage to power a dozen car batteries simultaneously.

Electric eels are essentially swimming power plants. They can generate shocks of up to 860 volts, which is more than enough to stun a horse or knock a human off their feet. The secret lies in their bodies, which contain thousands of specialized cells called electrocytes. These cells work exactly like tiny batteries. But here's the clever part: the eel doesn't rely on a single super-powered cell. Instead, it stacks thousands of these cellular batteries end to end, connecting them in series. Just like stacking AA batteries in a flashlight to get more voltage, the eel multiplies the small voltage of each cell into a devastating electrical punch.
