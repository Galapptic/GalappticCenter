Jet Engines Don't Need Spark Plugs to Keep Running

Once a jet engine starts, the air inside gets so violently compressed that fuel ignites on contact—no spark required.

Spark plugs in a jet engine are basically unemployed after the first few seconds. They exist only to get things started. Once the engine is running, combustion becomes self-sustaining through a principle so elegant it barely needs moving parts: compress air hard enough, and it gets hot enough to ignite fuel automatically.
