The Fish That Poops Out Paradise Beaches

That pristine white sand between your toes in Hawaii or the Maldives likely passed through the gut of a parrotfish.

Parrotfish are the unlikely architects of tropical coastlines. These rainbow-scaled reef dwellers spend their days chomping coral with fused teeth that form a beak—hence the name—and excreting the calcium carbonate as fine white sand. A single large parrotfish can produce over 380 kilograms of sand per year. Multiply that by the thousands of parrotfish on a healthy reef, and you get entire beaches, one bite at a time.
