Why Prices Know Things That Nobody Knows

The price of orange juice can predict Florida weather better than meteorologists—even though no trader is trying to forecast hurricanes.

In 1945, economist Friedrich Hayek posed a puzzle that still haunts economic theory: how does a society coordinate the actions of millions of people, each knowing only their tiny corner of the world? His answer was prices. Not because traders are geniuses, but because prices aggregate scattered knowledge that exists nowhere else—not in any database, not in any single mind.
