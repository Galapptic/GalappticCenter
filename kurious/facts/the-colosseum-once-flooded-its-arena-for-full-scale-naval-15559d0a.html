The Colosseum Once Flooded Its Arena for Full-Scale Naval Battles

Before gladiators and lions made it famous, Rome's greatest amphitheater could transform into an artificial lake deep enough to stage warship combat.

The Colosseum we see today is a skeleton of stone and brick, its underground chambers exposed to open air. But in its earliest years, those subterranean spaces didn't exist. Instead, the arena floor could be sealed and flooded with water pumped in from nearby aqueducts—turning the world's largest amphitheater into a temporary sea for staged naval battles called naumachiae.
