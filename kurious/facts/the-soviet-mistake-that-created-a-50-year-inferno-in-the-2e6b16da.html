The Soviet Mistake That Created a 50-Year Inferno in the Desert

In 1971, Soviet engineers accidentally punched a hole into a cavern of natural gas—and decided the best solution was to set it on fire.

In the middle of Turkmenistan's Karakum Desert, one of the most inhospitable landscapes on Earth, there is a hole in the ground that glows orange at night. It measures roughly 70 meters across and 30 meters deep—about the size of an American football field if you dropped it into a pit. Locals call it the Door to Hell. Officially, it's the Darvaza gas crater. And it has been burning continuously since the Nixon administration.

The crater sits about 260 kilometers north of Ashgabat, Turkmenistan's capital, in a region so remote that the nearest village has a population measured in dozens. By day, visitors see a collapsed pit ringed with cracked, heat-stressed earth. By night, hundreds of small fires dance across the crater floor like a vision from Dante, casting an amber glow visible for kilometers across the flat desert.
