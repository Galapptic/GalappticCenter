Colorblind Octopuses Still Pull Off Perfect Disguises

The mimic octopus can impersonate over 15 different species on demand, yet its eyes cannot distinguish a single color.

Deep in the Indo-Pacific, the mimic octopus performs one of nature's most impressive magic acts. It can transform into a lionfish, flatfish, sea snake, and dozens of other creatures—all to fool predators or sneak up on prey. But here's the twist: this master of disguise is completely colorblind. Like all octopuses, it has only one type of photoreceptor in its eyes, meaning it sees the world in grayscale. Yet somehow, it matches not just the shapes and movements of other animals, but their colors and patterns too. Scientists have documented at least 15 distinct impersonations, making it the only known animal to mimic multiple species. How does a colorblind creature achieve such chromatic perfection?
