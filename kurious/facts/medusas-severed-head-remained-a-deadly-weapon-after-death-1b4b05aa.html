Medusa's Severed Head Remained a Deadly Weapon After Death

Even decapitated by Perseus, Medusa's head continued turning enemies to stone and spawning monsters from her dripping blood.

When Perseus beheaded Medusa, he didn't just defeat a monster—he acquired the ancient world's most terrifying weapon. The severed head retained all its petrifying power, turning anyone who gazed upon it into stone. Perseus immediately put it to use, wielding it against the sea monster Cetus to save Andromeda, then against King Polydectes who had threatened his mother. Even accidental glimpses proved fatal. The head was so dangerous that Perseus eventually gifted it to Athena, who mounted it on her shield—the aegis—where it continued protecting the goddess by terrifying her enemies. Medusa's death was really just her career change from monster to weapon.
