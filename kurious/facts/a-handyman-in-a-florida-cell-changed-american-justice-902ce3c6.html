A Handyman in a Florida Cell Changed American Justice Forever

Clarence Earl Gideon had no lawyer, no money, and only an eighth-grade education—but his penciled petition from prison convinced the Supreme Court that the Constitution had been wrong for 172 years.

On June 3, 1961, someone broke into the Bay Harbor Pool Room in Panama City, Florida. They smashed a cigarette machine, broke into a jukebox, and stole some beer, wine, and about five dollars in coins. A witness claimed he saw Clarence Earl Gideon leaving the building with a bottle of wine and his pockets bulging with change. Police arrested the 51-year-old drifter, a man with a long criminal record and a troubled past. It was a routine burglary case. But what happened next would reshape the American legal system.
