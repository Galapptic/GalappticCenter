The Inca wove grass bridges that outlasted European stone

Imagine crossing a canyon on a bridge made entirely of grass, rebuilt every year for five centuries.

The Inca Empire stretched across some of the most brutal terrain on Earth. Steep mountains, deep gorges, raging rivers. To connect their vast kingdom, they needed bridges, but they had no iron, no steel, no concrete. What they had was grass. Specifically, a tough local grass called ichu. From this humble plant, they wove suspension bridges that could span chasms hundreds of feet across and support entire herds of llamas. These weren't flimsy rope swings. They were sophisticated pieces of engineering that formed the backbone of an empire.
