The Desert's Tiny Silk Architects Build Funnel Traps in Sand

Buried beneath the Namib Desert, larvae weave elaborate silk funnels that collapse under prey like trapdoors—a hunting strategy so effective it has persisted for millions of years.

Goblet worms aren't worms at all. They're the larvae of wormlion flies, members of the family Vermileonidae, and they've solved a problem that few other desert predators have cracked: how to catch fast-moving insects in loose, shifting sand. Their solution is a silk-lined funnel, delicately woven into the substrate, that acts as both snare and amplifier.
