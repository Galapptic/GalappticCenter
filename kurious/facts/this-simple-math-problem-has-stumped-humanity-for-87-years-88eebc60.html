This Simple Math Problem Has Stumped Humanity for 87 Years

Pick any number, follow two basic rules a child could understand, and you'll encounter a pattern that no mathematician on Earth can explain.

Here's the entire Collatz Conjecture: Pick any positive integer. If it's even, divide by two. If it's odd, multiply by three and add one. Repeat. The conjecture claims that no matter what number you start with, you'll always eventually reach one. Try it with seven: 7 becomes 22, then 11, then 34, then 17, then 52, then 26, then 13, then 40, then 20, then 10, then 5, then 16, then 8, then 4, then 2, then 1. Done. Every number tested—and computers have checked quintillions of them—eventually tumbles down to one. Yet nobody can prove this always happens.
