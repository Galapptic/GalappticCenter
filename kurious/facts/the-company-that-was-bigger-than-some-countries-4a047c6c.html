The Company That Was Bigger Than Some Countries

At its peak, the Dutch East India Company was worth more than Apple, Google, and Facebook combined—and it invented the modern corporation to get there.

In 1602, a group of Dutch merchants did something unprecedented: they pooled their money into a single enterprise, sold shares to the public, and created what we now call a corporation. The Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie—the Dutch East India Company, or VOC—wasn't just a trading business. It was a new kind of organization, one that would reshape global commerce and leave a complicated legacy that still echoes today.
