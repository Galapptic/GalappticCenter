Liquid Helium Defies Gravity by Crawling Up Walls

Cool helium to near absolute zero and it transforms into a ghostly fluid that climbs container walls, escapes through molecule-thin gaps, and flows without any friction whatsoever.

Drop below -271°C and liquid helium undergoes a bizarre transformation. It becomes a superfluid—a state of matter where all friction vanishes completely. In this form, helium develops an unsettling talent: it creeps up the walls of any container holding it, forming an impossibly thin film that defies gravity. The liquid flows up, over the rim, and down the outside until the container empties. It's not magic—it's quantum mechanics operating on a scale we can actually see. The helium atoms lose their individual identities and behave as one collective quantum object. Surface tension and capillary action, normally microscopic effects, become dominant when friction disappears entirely.
