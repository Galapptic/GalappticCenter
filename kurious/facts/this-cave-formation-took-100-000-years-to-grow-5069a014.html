This Cave Formation Took 100,000 Years to Grow

While you've aged a few seconds reading this, a stalagmite in New Mexico has grown less than the width of a human hair.

Deep beneath the Chihuahuan Desert, the Carlsbad Caverns contain formations that make glaciers look speedy. Stalactites hang from ceilings like stone icicles, while stalagmites rise from cave floors—some taking 100,000 years to reach their current size. These mineral sculptures grow at roughly 0.1 to 0.3 millimeters per year under ideal conditions. That's about the thickness of a sheet of paper annually. The massive stalagmites you see in photos, standing meters tall, represent timescales that dwarf human civilization. When some of these formations began growing, Neanderthals still roamed Europe. Every centimeter tells a story spanning millennia, preserved in crystalline layers of calcium carbonate.
