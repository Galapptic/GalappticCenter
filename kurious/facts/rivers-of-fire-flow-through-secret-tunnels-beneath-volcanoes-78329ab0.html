Rivers of Fire Flow Through Secret Tunnels Beneath Volcanoes

The longest lava tube on Earth stretches 40 miles through the Korean countryside, carved by molten rock that flowed underground while the surface above stayed cool enough to walk on.

When Kīlauea erupts on Hawaii's Big Island, tourists watch fountains of orange lava from safe viewing platforms. But the real action happens where no one can see it. Beneath the black crust of hardened rock, rivers of 2,000°F magma race through natural pipelines at speeds up to 20 miles per hour, traveling miles from the volcanic vent without losing more than a few degrees of heat. These are lava tubes—nature's most efficient delivery system for molten rock.
