The Labyrinth Wasn't Just a Maze It Was a Fear Map

The Greeks built their most famous monster prison not to contain a beast, but to give shape to the terror of being hopelessly, permanently lost.

Beneath the palace of King Minos on Crete, the architect Daedalus built something unprecedented: a structure so complex that even its creator barely escaped. Inside waited the Minotaur, a creature half-man, half-bull, fed on Athenian youths sent as tribute. But the labyrinth itself was the true horror—a place where every turn looked identical, where forward and backward lost meaning.
