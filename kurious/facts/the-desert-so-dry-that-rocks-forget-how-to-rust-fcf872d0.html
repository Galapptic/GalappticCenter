The Desert So Dry That Rocks Forget How to Rust

In Chile's Atacama Desert, some weather stations have never recorded a single drop of rain, creating conditions so extreme that even iron refuses to oxidize.

Rust seems inevitable. Leave a nail outside anywhere on Earth, and oxidation will eventually claim it. But in a narrow strip of northern Chile, between the Pacific Ocean and the Andes, this basic chemistry stalls. The Atacama Desert is so profoundly dry that iron objects abandoned decades ago remain pristine, their surfaces untouched by the corrosion that would consume them anywhere else.
