The Vampire Squid's Escape Trick Is Turning Inside Out

When threatened in the pitch-black deep sea, the vampire squid performs one of nature's strangest defensive maneuvers: it flips its webbed arms over its head like an umbrella blown backward, revealing rows of fleshy spines.

The vampire squid isn't actually a squid—or an octopus. It's the sole surviving member of its own ancient order, Vampyromorphida, a living fossil drifting through the oxygen-minimum zone between 600 and 900 meters deep. Down there, in near-total darkness where most predators can't survive, it has evolved defenses unlike anything else in the ocean.
