The Innocent-Looking Equation That Broke Mathematics Forever

In 1931, a 25-year-old Austrian proved that mathematics contains true statements it can never prove—using a formula that talks about itself.

Kurt Gödel was quiet, paranoid, and brilliant. In 1931, while mathematicians were busy trying to put all of mathematics on a perfect logical foundation, Gödel published a short paper that showed their project was impossible. His weapon was a single self-referential formula—an equation that essentially says, "This statement cannot be proven."
