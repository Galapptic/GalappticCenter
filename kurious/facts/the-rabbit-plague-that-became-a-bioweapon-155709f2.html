The Rabbit Plague That Became a Bioweapon

A disease spread by cottontails was once considered so dangerous that Cold War superpowers raced to turn it into an instrument of war.

In 1941, Soviet soldiers defending Stalingrad began falling ill by the thousands. The culprit wasn't German artillery—it was tularemia, a bacterial infection carried by mice and rabbits displaced by the fighting. Some historians suspect the outbreak wasn't entirely natural. Either way, military planners took note: this obscure disease could devastate an army.
