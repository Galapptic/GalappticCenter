Your brain creates false memories when people ask leading questions

A simple question can make you remember things that never happened, and you'll swear they're real.

Ask someone if they saw the broken headlight after an accident, and suddenly they remember one, even if no headlight was broken. This is the misinformation effect, discovered by psychologist Elizabeth Loftus in the 1970s. When someone asks a leading question, your brain doesn't just retrieve a memory. It actually constructs a new one on the spot, weaving together the question's suggestion with whatever fragments you actually recall. The scary part? You can't tell the difference. The false memory feels just as real, just as vivid, as something that truly happened. Your confidence in it might even be stronger.
