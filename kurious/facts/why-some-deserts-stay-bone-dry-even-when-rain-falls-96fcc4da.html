Why Some Deserts Stay Bone-Dry Even When Rain Falls

In certain deserts, salt in the soil is so hungry for moisture that raindrops never reach plant roots.

The Atacama Desert in Chile is one of the driest places on Earth. Some weather stations there have never recorded rain. But when storms do roll through, something strange happens: the ground stays parched. Plants still die. The moisture vanishes almost instantly. The culprit isn't evaporation from heat alone—it's salt.
