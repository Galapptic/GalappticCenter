Every Vanilla Bean Exists Because of Hand Pollination

The world's most popular flavor comes from an orchid so finicky that humans must personally pollinate each flower by hand—a technique discovered by a 12-year-old enslaved boy.

Vanilla comes from an orchid native to Mexico, and it has an extremely awkward reproductive problem. The vanilla flower opens for just a few hours, and its male and female parts are separated by a membrane that prevents self-pollination. In Mexico, a tiny native bee called the Melipona can navigate this obstacle, wriggling into the bloom to transfer pollen. But here's the catch: when Spanish conquistadors brought vanilla cuttings to Europe and other colonies, the bee didn't come along. For centuries, vanilla would only fruit in Mexico, baffling botanists. Today, roughly 99% of all vanilla is pollinated by human hands, making it one of the most labor-intensive crops on Earth.
