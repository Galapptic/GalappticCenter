Glass Threads Trap Light to Send Data Across Oceans

The internet's backbone isn't satellites or radio waves—it's hair-thin glass strands bouncing light signals 99.7% of the way around the world.

Fiber optic cables work like magical light pipes. When light enters a glass fiber thinner than a human hair, it becomes trapped—bouncing off the inner walls thousands of times per second as it races forward. This phenomenon, called total internal reflection, happens because the glass core is surrounded by a layer with a different density that acts like a perfect mirror. Light literally cannot escape sideways, so it has no choice but to travel forward. These trapped light pulses carry your video calls, streaming movies, and messages across entire continents and ocean floors. Over 99% of international internet traffic travels through undersea fiber optic cables—not satellites as many assume.
