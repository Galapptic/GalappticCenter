This Fuzzy Fungus Turns Rice Into Alcohol With Zero Sugar

A microscopic mold performs a biochemical magic trick that took humans centuries to understand, converting plain starch into sugar and then alcohol in one continuous process.

Sake contains no added sugar, yet it's one of the sweetest-tasting alcoholic beverages in the world. The secret? A fuzzy green mold called Aspergillus oryzae, known in Japan as koji. When sprinkled onto steamed rice, this remarkable fungus releases powerful enzymes that break down rice starches into simple sugars. Here's where it gets fascinating: while koji creates sugar, yeast simultaneously converts that sugar into alcohol. This parallel fermentation—happening in the same vessel at the same time—is nearly unique in the brewing world. Beer and wine can't do this; they need their sugars pre-made. Koji essentially runs a microscopic sugar factory that feeds an alcohol factory, all without human intervention.
