Your record player works completely backwards from how you think

The needle on a record player doesn't make sound—it gets pushed around by tiny bumps.

When you watch a record spin, it looks like the needle is reading grooves and somehow creating music. But that's not what happens at all. The needle isn't doing anything active. Those grooves are actually tiny physical hills and valleys carved into the vinyl, and they're literally shoving the needle back and forth. The needle has no choice but to follow. It's being pushed around thousands of times per second by the landscape of the record itself. The needle is passive. The record does all the work. Everything you hear was already there, frozen in plastic.
