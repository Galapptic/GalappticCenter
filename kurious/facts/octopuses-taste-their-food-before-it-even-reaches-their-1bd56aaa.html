Octopuses Taste Their Food Before It Even Reaches Their Mouth

Every one of an octopus's eight arms is covered in hundreds of suckers that can taste prey on contact—and decide whether it's worth eating before the brain even weighs in.

When an octopus reaches a tentacle into a rocky crevice, it's not just feeling around. It's tasting everything it touches. Each of the animal's roughly 1,600 suckers contains chemoreceptors—specialized cells that detect chemical signatures the way our tongues detect salt or sugar. By the time food reaches an octopus's beak, the arms have already conducted a full taste test.
