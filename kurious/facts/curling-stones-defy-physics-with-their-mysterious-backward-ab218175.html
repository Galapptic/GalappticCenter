Curling Stones Defy Physics With Their Mysterious Backward Curve

For over 500 years, curling stones have curved in the opposite direction that physics predicts, and scientists only recently discovered that sweepers create invisible water films just molecules thick.

When you spin a glass across a table, it curves in the direction of rotation—exactly what friction should do. But curling stones do something bizarre: they curve the opposite way. Spin a stone clockwise, and it mysteriously drifts right instead of left. This backward behavior has puzzled physicists for centuries. The secret lies partly in sweeping. When teammates furiously brush the ice ahead of a gliding stone, they're not just clearing debris. They're creating microscopic water films—layers just molecules thick—that fundamentally change how the 44-pound granite stone interacts with the ice surface. These invisible water layers reduce friction unevenly, allowing skilled sweepers to influence exactly where the stone stops.
