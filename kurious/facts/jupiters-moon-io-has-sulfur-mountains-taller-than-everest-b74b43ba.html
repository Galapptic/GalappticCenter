Jupiter's moon Io has sulfur mountains taller than Everest

The most volcanic place in our solar system builds mountains from something you'd find in a chemistry lab.

Io is the most volcanically active body in our solar system, and it shows. Jupiter's innermost large moon looks like a cosmic pizza, splattered with yellows, oranges, and reds. Those aren't just colors. They're different forms of sulfur, erupted from hundreds of active volcanoes and deposited across the surface. Some of those deposits have piled up into mountains that soar over 17 kilometers high. That's nearly twice the height of Mount Everest. Instead of rock or ice, these peaks are built from layer upon layer of sulfur compounds, each eruption adding to their height.
