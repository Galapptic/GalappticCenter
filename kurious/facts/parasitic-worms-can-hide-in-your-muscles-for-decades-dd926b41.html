Parasitic Worms Can Hide in Your Muscles for Decades

Microscopic larvae burrow into your muscle fibers, forming protective capsules where they silently wait for years—sometimes your entire lifetime—without triggering a single symptom.

Trichinella is a parasitic roundworm with a disturbing survival strategy. When you eat undercooked infected meat—typically pork or wild game—larvae travel through your digestive system and burrow directly into your muscle tissue. There, they coil up inside individual muscle cells, transforming them into nurse cells that keep the parasite alive and hidden. Your immune system often fails to detect these intruders because they've essentially hijacked your own cells as camouflage. A single infected meal can deposit thousands of larvae throughout your muscles, where they can remain viable for five to ten years, sometimes longer. Most people never realize they're hosting these silent passengers.
