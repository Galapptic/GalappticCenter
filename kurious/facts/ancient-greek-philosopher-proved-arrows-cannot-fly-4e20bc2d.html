Ancient Greek Philosopher Proved Arrows Cannot Fly

A 2,500-year-old logical argument demonstrates that at any single instant, a speeding arrow is completely motionless.

Here's a brain-twister from ancient Greece: Zeno of Elea argued that a flying arrow is actually motionless. His reasoning? At any single instant of time, the arrow occupies a space exactly equal to itself. It's not moving into new space—it's just there. And if the arrow is still at every single instant, when does it actually move? You can't add up a bunch of zeros and get movement. The arrow should be frozen forever. Obviously arrows do fly, which is precisely what makes this a paradox. Zeno wasn't crazy—he was challenging us to explain how motion works if time consists of individual moments where nothing is happening.
