The Doctors Who Prescribed Hookworms

In the 2000s, researchers in Britain and Australia deliberately infected volunteers with bloodsucking intestinal worms, hoping the parasites could calm overactive immune systems.

In 1989, British epidemiologist David Strachan noticed that children with many older siblings got hay fever less often. He suggested that early exposure to germs might train the immune system to stay calm. His idea became known as the hygiene hypothesis. Later researchers widened it to include "old friends": microbes and parasites, such as worms, that lived alongside humans for most of our history.
