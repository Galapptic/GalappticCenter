Pulsars Spin Hundreds of Times Per Second Without Flying Apart

Some neutron stars rotate so fast they complete 716 full rotations in a single second, making them the universe's most precise natural clocks.

Imagine a star more massive than our Sun, crushed into a ball just 20 kilometers across, spinning faster than a kitchen blender. That's a pulsar. These ultra-dense neutron stars emit beams of radiation from their magnetic poles, and as they rotate, these beams sweep across space like lighthouse beacons. Some pulsars spin so violently they complete full rotations in mere milliseconds. The fastest known pulsar, PSR J1748-2446ad, spins 716 times every second. At its equator, the surface moves at about 24% the speed of light. Despite these extreme speeds, the incredible gravity holds everything together, preventing the star from tearing itself apart.
