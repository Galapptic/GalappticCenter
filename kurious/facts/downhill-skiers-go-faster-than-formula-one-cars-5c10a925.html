Downhill Skiers Go Faster Than Formula One Cars

At 161 mph on nothing but two thin edges and gravity, speed skiers outpace the fastest racing machines on Earth.

When Italian skier Simone Origone hurtles down a mountain, he moves faster than a Formula One car at full throttle. In 2006, he clocked 251.4 km/h (156.2 mph) at Les Arcs, France—a speed that would earn him a speeding ticket on a German autobahn and leave most supercars in his wake. The current world record, set by Ivan Origone (Simone's brother) in 2016, stands at an almost incomprehensible 254.958 km/h (158.4 mph).

Formula One cars, for comparison, typically reach top speeds between 230-240 km/h during races, though they can push past 370 km/h on long straights. But here's the difference: those cars have 1,000-horsepower engines, sophisticated aerodynamics, and teams of engineers. Speed skiers have gravity, a steep slope, and a skintight suit.
