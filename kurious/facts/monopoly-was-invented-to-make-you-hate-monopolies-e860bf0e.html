Monopoly Was Invented to Make You Hate Monopolies

The world's best-selling board game started as a radical protest against capitalism—then got stolen and rebranded as a celebration of it.

Every year, around 275 million people play Monopoly. They buy Boardwalk, crush their friends, and watch gleefully as bankrupt opponents hand over their last crumpled bills. The game feels like a love letter to ruthless capitalism. But Monopoly was invented to teach exactly the opposite lesson: that when one person owns everything, everyone else loses.

The true origin of the game involves a feminist economist, a stolen idea, and a man who rewrote history to make himself the hero. For decades, most people believed the official story. The real one is stranger—and says something uncomfortable about how easy it is to forget inconvenient truths.
