When Pepper Was Worth More Than Gold

In medieval Europe, a sack of pepper could buy a house, and spice merchants routinely outearned the men who pulled precious metals from the earth.

Between the 11th and 15th centuries, black pepper was so valuable that it was counted out peppercorn by peppercorn. Rents could be paid in it. Dowries included it. When Alaric the Visigoth besieged Rome in 408 CE, he demanded 3,000 pounds of pepper as part of the ransom. The spice wasn't just expensive—it was a currency.
