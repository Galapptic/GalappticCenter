The Chocolate Bar That Launched a Kitchen Revolution

A self-taught engineer with a peanut cluster in his pocket stumbled upon a technology that would transform how humanity cooks, all while tinkering with military radar equipment in 1945.

Percy Spencer never graduated from grammar school. Orphaned at eighteen months and raised by a destitute aunt, he went to work in a spool mill at age twelve. By his mid-twenties, he had taught himself calculus, chemistry, physics, and metallurgy—mostly by reading textbooks during his night shifts. By 1945, he held 120 patents and was one of the world's foremost experts on radar tube design. What he discovered that year, quite literally by accident, would eventually sit in 90 percent of American kitchens.
