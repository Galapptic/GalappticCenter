Dead Stars Spin Faster Than Your Blender Every Second

Some collapsed stars rotate 716 times per second—so fast that their surfaces move at nearly a quarter the speed of light.

When massive stars die, they don't always go quietly. Some collapse into neutron stars—city-sized balls of matter so dense that a teaspoon would weigh billions of tons. But here's the wild part: these stellar corpses spin at mind-boggling speeds, some rotating hundreds of times every second. As they spin, they shoot beams of radiation from their magnetic poles. Like a lighthouse beam sweeping across the ocean, these beams flash past Earth with clockwork precision. We call these objects pulsars, and their rhythmic pulses are so regular that when astronomers first detected them in 1967, they briefly wondered if they'd found alien signals.
