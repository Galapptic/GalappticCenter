Medieval Monk's Simple Rule Still Guides Modern Science

A 14th-century friar's philosophical shortcut became the foundational principle that scientists, doctors, and detectives still use to solve problems today.

When you hear hoofbeats, think horses, not zebras. That's Occam's Razor in action. This principle states that among competing explanations, the one requiring the fewest assumptions is usually correct. Named after William of Ockham, a 14th-century English friar, this mental tool helps us avoid overcomplicating problems. If your car won't start, it's more likely a dead battery than a stolen engine. The razor isn't about being lazy—it's about intellectual efficiency. Scientists use it constantly when developing theories. Why invent invisible forces when known physics explains observations? Occam's Razor doesn't guarantee truth, but it's remarkably effective at cutting through confusion to find the most probable answer.
