The Dead Sea Is Sinking Into Oblivion

Earth's lowest point on land drops by over a meter every year, and the shoreline has retreated so dramatically that hotels once on the beach now sit a mile from the water.

The Dead Sea sits roughly 430 meters below sea level, making its shores the lowest dry land on our planet. But here's the unsettling part: that number keeps changing. The water level drops by more than a meter annually, meaning Earth's lowest point is literally getting lower every year. Straddling the border between Israel, Jordan, and the West Bank, this hypersaline lake has lost about a third of its surface area since the 1960s. The famous buoyant waters where tourists float effortlessly reading newspapers are receding at an alarming rate. Resorts that once offered beachfront access now require shuttle rides to reach the retreating shoreline.
