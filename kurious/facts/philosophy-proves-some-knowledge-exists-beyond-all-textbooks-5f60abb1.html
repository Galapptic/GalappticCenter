Philosophy Proves Some Knowledge Exists Beyond All Textbooks

A brilliant scientist who knows everything about color has never actually seen it—and that changes everything about what knowledge means.

Imagine a scientist named Mary who lives her entire life in a black-and-white room. She studies color through colorless monitors and books, learning absolutely everything physics can teach about wavelengths, retinas, and neural processing. Mary becomes the world's foremost expert on color vision. Then one day, she steps outside and sees a red tomato for the first time. Does she learn something new? Philosopher Frank Jackson argued in 1982 that she obviously does—she learns what red actually looks like. This seemingly simple thought experiment, called the Knowledge Argument, suggests that conscious experience contains knowledge that no amount of physical information can capture. Some things must be lived to be known.
