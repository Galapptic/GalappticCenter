Vinyl Grooves Are Narrower Than a Human Hair

The music on your favorite record lives inside microscopic valleys thinner than the hair on your head.

Pick up a vinyl record and you'll see what looks like smooth black plastic with faint lines. But zoom in and you'll discover an engineering marvel. Those grooves spiral from the outer edge to the center, and each one is typically just 40-50 micrometers wide—less than half the width of a human hair. A stylus, or needle, rides through this microscopic canyon, bouncing off the walls as it travels. Those walls aren't smooth; they're covered in tiny undulations that correspond to sound waves. As the needle vibrates against these microscopic patterns, it converts physical movement back into electrical signals, then into the music you hear. It's analog audio at its most elegant.
