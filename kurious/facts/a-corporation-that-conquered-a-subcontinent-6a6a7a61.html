A Corporation That Conquered a Subcontinent

At its peak, a London trading company commanded a private army twice the size of Britain's, ruled 200 million people, and generated revenues that dwarfed entire nations.

On December 31, 1600, Queen Elizabeth I signed a royal charter granting a small group of London merchants exclusive rights to trade with the East Indies. The document created the Governor and Company of Merchants of London Trading into the East-Indies—later shortened to the East India Company. What began as 218 investors pooling £68,373 would become the most powerful corporation in human history, a business that didn't just trade with a subcontinent but ruled it outright for nearly a century.
