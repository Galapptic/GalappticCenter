A Riverside Stroll Puzzle Accidentally Invented Modern Mathematics

Leonhard Euler proved you couldn't walk across seven bridges exactly once each, and accidentally created an entirely new branch of mathematics.

In 1736, the Prussian city of Königsberg had a quirky obsession. Seven bridges connected two islands to the mainland, and residents wondered: could you walk across each bridge exactly once without retracing your steps? Mathematician Leonhard Euler tackled this recreational puzzle but approached it differently than anyone expected. Instead of trying endless walking routes, he stripped the problem down to its essence. He ignored distances, bridge lengths, and scenic views. All that mattered were the landmasses and their connections. By doing this, Euler proved the walk was mathematically impossible—and unknowingly created graph theory, the mathematics of connections that now underpins everything from social networks to GPS navigation.
