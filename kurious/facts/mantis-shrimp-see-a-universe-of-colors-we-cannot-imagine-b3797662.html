Mantis Shrimp See a Universe of Colors We Cannot Imagine

With 16 types of color receptors compared to our measly three, mantis shrimp perceive wavelengths of light that literally don't exist in human experience—yet their brains process color in a way scientists never expected.

Somewhere on a coral reef in the Indo-Pacific, a four-inch crustacean is looking at you with eyes that make human vision seem like watching television through a keyhole. The mantis shrimp—neither mantis nor shrimp, but a stomatopod crustacean—possesses the most complex visual system ever discovered in any animal. It can see ultraviolet light, polarized light, and colors so far outside our visual spectrum that no human word exists to describe them.

For decades, scientists assumed this meant mantis shrimp experienced color with impossible richness—a psychedelic world of millions more hues than we could ever perceive. Then researchers actually tested this assumption. What they found was stranger than anyone imagined: these creatures had evolved a completely alien way of seeing, one that sacrifices nuance for speed.
