Black Holes Aren't Black—They're Slowly Evaporating Into Nothing

Stephen Hawking discovered that the darkest objects in the universe are actually glowing, leaking particles until they vanish entirely—a finding so strange it took physicists decades to accept.

For most of the twentieth century, black holes seemed like perfect prisons. Once something crossed the event horizon—the boundary beyond which escape becomes impossible—it was gone forever. Not even light could climb back out. The universe's most extreme objects were thought to be eternal, growing larger with every star they swallowed but never shrinking, never changing, never giving anything back.

Then in 1974, a 32-year-old physicist at Cambridge showed that everyone had missed something. Black holes weren't just swallowing matter. They were radiating it away, particle by particle, in a process so slow it would take longer than the current age of the universe to notice—but unstoppable nonetheless. Stephen Hawking had discovered that black holes die.
