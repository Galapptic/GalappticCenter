Zombie fungi turn ants into puppets before bursting through their heads

A fungus can hijack an ant's brain, march it to the perfect dying spot, and explode.

Deep in tropical forests, certain ants wander away from their colonies in a daze. They climb plants to a very specific height, clamp their jaws onto a leaf vein with unusual force, and die. Within days, a fungal stalk erupts from their heads like something from a horror film. This is Ophiocordyceps unilateralis, a parasitic fungus that turns ants into zombies. The fungus needs the ant to die in exactly the right spot, at the right height and humidity, so its spores can rain down on unsuspecting ants below. The precision is unsettling. Every infected ant climbs to roughly the same height and bites down at solar noon.
