The Island Where Trees Look Like They Belong on Another Planet

A third of Socotra's plant species exist nowhere else on Earth, making this Yemeni archipelago one of the most alien landscapes you can visit without leaving the planet.

Four hundred kilometers off the coast of Yemen, in the turquoise waters where the Arabian Sea meets the Gulf of Aden, sits an island that evolution forgot to tell about the rest of the world. Socotra spent millions of years in isolation, and the result is a landscape so strange that the first European naturalists to catalog its species struggled to classify what they were seeing. Trees that look like giant mushrooms. Succulents that resemble elephant legs. A forest canopy shaped like inside-out umbrellas, dripping red sap that ancient civilizations believed was dragon's blood.
