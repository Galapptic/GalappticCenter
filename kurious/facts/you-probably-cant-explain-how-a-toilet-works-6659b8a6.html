You Probably Can't Explain How a Toilet Works

Most people are convinced they understand everyday objects and systems—until you ask them to actually explain one.

In 2002, Yale psychologists Leonid Rozenblit and Frank Keil asked people to rate how well they understood things like zippers, helicopters, and piano keys. Confidence ran high. Then they asked participants to write step-by-step explanations. Confidence collapsed. People discovered, mid-sentence, that they had no idea what they were talking about.
