The Hidden Code in Your Vocabulary That Reveals Your Class

Researchers can predict your parents' income within minutes of hearing you speak, and the words you choose betray far more than your accent ever could.

In 1912, George Bernard Shaw wrote a play about a phonetics professor who bets he can pass off a Cockney flower seller as a duchess simply by changing how she speaks. Audiences laughed because they recognized the truth: in Britain, and everywhere else, your voice is your autobiography. But Shaw focused on accent. A century later, linguists have discovered something more insidious. The words themselves—not how you pronounce them, but which ones you reach for—form an invisible class marker that shapes how others judge your intelligence, trustworthiness, and potential.
