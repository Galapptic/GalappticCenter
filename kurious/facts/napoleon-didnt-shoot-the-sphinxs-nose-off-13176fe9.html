Napoleon Didn't Shoot the Sphinx's Nose Off

One of history's most persistent myths blames Napoleon for disfiguring the Great Sphinx, but the real culprit struck centuries earlier—and we know his name.

The story is irresistible: Napoleon Bonaparte, fresh from conquering Egypt in 1798, orders his artillery to use the 4,500-year-old Sphinx for target practice. A cannonball strikes the ancient face, and the nose crumbles into the desert sand. It's dramatic, it feels plausible, and it's been repeated in classrooms and tour guides for generations. There's just one problem. It never happened.

The Sphinx was already noseless when Napoleon arrived. We have proof—not just circumstantial evidence, but actual drawings made before the French expedition ever set foot in Egypt. The real story of who destroyed the nose is stranger, more deliberate, and far older than the Napoleon legend.
