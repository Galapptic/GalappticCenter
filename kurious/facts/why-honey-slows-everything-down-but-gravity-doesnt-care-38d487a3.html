Why Honey Slows Everything Down But Gravity Doesn't Care

Gravity pulls a marble through honey with exactly the same force it uses in air, yet the marble takes 10,000 times longer to reach the bottom.

Drop a marble into a jar of honey and watch it sink. Slowly. Agonizingly slowly. The same marble dropped through air hits the ground in a fraction of a second. Your intuition might suggest that honey somehow weakens gravity, cushioning the fall. But gravity hasn't changed at all. The marble experiences precisely the same downward pull—9.8 meters per second squared—whether it's falling through air, water, or the stickiest honey on Earth. What changes is everything the marble has to push through to get where gravity wants it to go.
