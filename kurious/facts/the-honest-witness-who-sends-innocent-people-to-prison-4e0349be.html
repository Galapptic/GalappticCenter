The Honest Witness Who Sends Innocent People to Prison

The most compelling evidence in criminal trials—a victim pointing at the defendant and saying 'That's him'—is wrong roughly 70% of the time in proven wrongful convictions.

Jennifer Thompson was certain. In 1984, she picked Ronald Cotton out of a police lineup, testifying that he had broken into her apartment in Burlington, North Carolina, and raped her at knifepoint. She had studied his face during the attack, determined to remember every detail. The jury believed her. Cotton went to prison for life.

Eleven years later, DNA evidence proved Cotton was innocent. The actual rapist was Bobby Poole, a man Thompson had never seen before the trial—though Poole had been presented in a later lineup. By then, her memory had already cemented Cotton's face as her attacker. She was sincere, confident, and completely wrong.
