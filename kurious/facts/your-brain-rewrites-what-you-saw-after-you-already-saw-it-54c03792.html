Your brain rewrites what you saw after you already saw it

The moment you think you're seeing is actually a fraction of a second in the past, edited by your brain.

You might think you see the world in real time, like a live video feed. You don't. Your brain needs about a tenth of a second to process visual information before you become conscious of it. But here's the wild part: your brain doesn't just delay the image. It actually goes back and changes what you think you saw based on what happened afterward. The experience you call seeing is your mind's best guess about what probably happened, constructed after the fact. Reality reaches your consciousness on a tape delay, and the tape gets edited before you watch it.
