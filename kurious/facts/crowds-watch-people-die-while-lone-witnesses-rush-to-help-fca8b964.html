Crowds Watch People Die While Lone Witnesses Rush to Help

The more people who witness your emergency, the less likely anyone will save you.

You'd think collapsing in a crowded street would be safer than in an empty alley. You'd be wrong. The bystander effect describes a disturbing psychological phenomenon: as the number of witnesses increases, the probability that any single person will help dramatically decreases. When you're alone and spot someone in trouble, responsibility falls squarely on your shoulders. But in a crowd? Everyone assumes someone else will call for help, someone else will step forward, someone else will take charge. This diffusion of responsibility creates a dangerous paralysis. People look around, see others doing nothing, and interpret their inaction as a signal that intervention isn't needed. The crowd becomes its own trap.
