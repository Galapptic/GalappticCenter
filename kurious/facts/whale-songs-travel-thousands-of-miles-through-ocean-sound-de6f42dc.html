Whale songs travel thousands of miles through ocean sound highways

A whale singing near Bermuda can be heard by another whale near the coast of Africa.

When whales sing, they're not just performing for nearby neighbors. Their songs can travel across entire ocean basins, sometimes crossing distances of over 10,000 miles. This extraordinary reach happens because of a special layer deep in the ocean called the SOFAR channel. It stands for Sound Fixing and Ranging, and it acts like an acoustic highway. Water temperature and pressure create a zone, usually around 600 to 1200 meters deep, where sound gets trapped and channeled horizontally. Instead of dissipating in all directions, whale songs can ride this channel across vast distances with remarkably little loss of volume.
