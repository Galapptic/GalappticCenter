A Colorblind Scientist Proves Knowledge Cannot Capture Experience

Imagine knowing every physical fact about color while never having seen red—would you learn something new the moment you finally did?

Meet Mary, a brilliant scientist who knows absolutely everything about color vision. She understands wavelengths, retinal cones, neural processing—every physical fact about seeing red. But there's a catch: Mary has lived her entire life in a black-and-white room, studying color through grayscale monitors. One day, she steps outside and sees a red rose for the first time. Does she learn something new? Most people instinctively say yes—she finally knows what red looks like. But if she already knew all the physical facts, what exactly did she gain? This puzzle, created by philosopher Frank Jackson in 1982, challenges whether physical science can fully explain conscious experience.
