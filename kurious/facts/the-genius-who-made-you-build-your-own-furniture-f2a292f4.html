The Genius Who Made You Build Your Own Furniture

Ingvar Kamprad built a $58 billion empire by making customers do the work themselves—and somehow making them feel smart for doing it.

In 1956, a young Swedish furniture seller named Ingvar Kamprad watched a worker struggling to fit a table into a customer's car. The legs wouldn't clear the trunk. So the worker grabbed a screwdriver and removed them. That moment of frustration sparked an idea that would reshape global retail: What if furniture came apart on purpose?

Kamprad didn't invent flat-pack furniture, but he turned it into a philosophy. By the time he died in 2018, IKEA had 422 stores in 50 countries, annual revenues exceeding $40 billion, and had sold enough Billy bookcases to circle the Earth—twice. The company's secret wasn't just cheap prices. It was convincing hundreds of millions of people that assembling particle board with an Allen wrench was actually a good time.
