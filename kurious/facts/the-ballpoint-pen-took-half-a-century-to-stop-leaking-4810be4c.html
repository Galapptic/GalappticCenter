The Ballpoint Pen Took Half a Century to Stop Leaking

The simple pen in your desk drawer defeated engineers for fifty years because ink refused to behave.

The ballpoint pen concept seems brilliantly simple: a tiny rotating ball dispenses ink as you write. But that elegance masked a nightmare of engineering problems. Invented in 1888 by John Loud, early ballpoint pens were disasters. The ink either leaked everywhere, creating blotchy messes, or dried up inside the pen, making it useless. Temperature changes caused the ink to expand and ooze out. Writing at certain angles made the ball skip. For decades, inventors filed patent after patent, each claiming to solve the leaking problem. None truly succeeded. The humble fountain pen remained king while engineers struggled with a concept that looked so simple on paper but proved maddeningly difficult in practice.
