How One Man Conquered an Empire the Size of Africa in 25 Years

By the time Genghis Khan died in 1227, his horsemen had seized more territory than the Romans took in four centuries.

In 1206, a 44-year-old nomad named Temüjin united the warring Mongol tribes and took a new title: Genghis Khan, "Universal Ruler." Over the next two decades, he and his sons would conquer 24 million square kilometers—roughly the size of the entire African continent. No empire before or since has grown so fast or stretched so far across unbroken land.
