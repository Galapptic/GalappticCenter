Banks Actually Pay You to Borrow When Interest Rates Go Negative

In a bizarre economic twist, some homeowners have received monthly checks from their banks instead of mortgage bills.

Imagine taking out a loan and having the bank pay you for the privilege. That's exactly what happened in countries like Denmark, Switzerland, and Japan when central banks pushed interest rates below zero. Instead of earning interest on savings, depositors might pay banks to hold their money. Meanwhile, some lucky mortgage holders watched their loan balances shrink automatically as negative rates kicked in. This economic twilight zone emerged after the 2008 financial crisis when traditional tools stopped working. Central banks needed new ways to stimulate spending, so they ventured into territory economists once thought impossible—charging people to save and rewarding them to borrow.
