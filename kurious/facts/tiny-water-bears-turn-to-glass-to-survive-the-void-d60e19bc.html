Tiny Water Bears Turn to Glass to Survive the Void

When exposed to the vacuum of space, tardigrades transform their entire cellular structure into a glass-like solid, pausing life itself until conditions improve.

Tardigrades, affectionately called water bears, are microscopic eight-legged creatures barely visible to the naked eye. In 2007, European scientists sent them into the vacuum of space aboard the FOTON-M3 mission—and they came back alive. Their secret? A process called cryptobiosis, where they expel nearly all water from their bodies and enter a state called a "tun." In this dried-out form, their proteins and cell membranes transition into a glass-like matrix, essentially freezing all biological processes. No metabolism, no aging, no life—yet not quite death. When water returns, they simply wake up and continue living as if nothing happened.
