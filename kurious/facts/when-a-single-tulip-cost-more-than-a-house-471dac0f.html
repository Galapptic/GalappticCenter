When a Single Tulip Cost More Than a House

At the peak of 1637's tulip mania, one bulb sold for enough money to buy a grand Amsterdam canal house, hire servants, and still have change left over.

In the winter of 1637, the Dutch Republic was the richest nation in Europe. Its ships dominated global trade, its banks pioneered modern finance, and its merchants had developed an unlikely obsession: tulip bulbs. What began as aristocratic flower collecting had spiraled into history's first recorded speculative bubble, one that would collapse so suddenly it became a warning for every market crash that followed.
