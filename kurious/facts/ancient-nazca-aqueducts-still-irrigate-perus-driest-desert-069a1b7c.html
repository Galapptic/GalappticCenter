Ancient Nazca aqueducts still irrigate Peru's driest desert today

In one of Earth's driest places, pre-Columbian engineers built a water system we still can't fully replicate.

The Nazca people vanished over a thousand years ago, but their water system still works. In the Peruvian desert where rainfall measures barely half an inch per year, these ancient aqueducts called puquios deliver fresh water to farms and towns. Farmers today open the same stone channels their ancestors carved fifteen centuries ago. The water flows as reliably now as it did then. While the famous Nazca Lines baffle us from above, beneath the surface lies an even more astonishing achievement. These weren't simple ditches. They were sophisticated hydraulic systems that turned an uninhabitable wasteland into agricultural paradise.
