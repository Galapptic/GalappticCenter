Your brain erases most dreams within 10 minutes of waking

You experience vivid stories every night, but your brain treats them like classified documents that must be destroyed.

You wake up with fragments of a dream still clinging to your consciousness. A strange house. Someone you knew in college. Flying, maybe. But as you reach for your phone or stumble toward the bathroom, it dissolves like sugar in water. Within ten minutes, most of it is gone. Within an hour, nearly all of it has vanished. This isn't a flaw in your memory. Your brain is actively choosing to let dreams slip away, prioritizing the waking world over the night's bizarre theater. We forget dreams faster than we forget almost anything else we experience.
