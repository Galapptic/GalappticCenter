The Day Wall Street Lost a Quarter of Its Value in Hours

On October 19, 1987, the stock market suffered its worst single-day percentage drop in history—and to this day, no one fully agrees on why.

The morning of October 19, 1987, began with a sense of unease on Wall Street. Markets in Asia and Europe had already fallen sharply overnight. But no one—not the traders arriving at the New York Stock Exchange, not the economists at the Federal Reserve, not the portfolio managers handling billions in pension funds—had any idea they were about to witness the single worst day in stock market history.

By the closing bell, the Dow Jones Industrial Average had plunged 508 points, a staggering 22.6 percent of its total value. In today's terms, adjusted for the size of modern markets, the equivalent loss would exceed six trillion dollars. The date became known simply as Black Monday.
