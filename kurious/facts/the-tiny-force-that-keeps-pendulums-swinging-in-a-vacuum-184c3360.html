The Tiny Force That Keeps Pendulums Swinging in a Vacuum

Even in a perfect vacuum with no air resistance, pendulums lose energy slower than physics predicts—and for decades, nobody knew why.

In 1851, Léon Foucault hung a 28-kilogram brass bob from the dome of the Panthéon in Paris and let it swing. The pendulum proved Earth's rotation, but it also revealed something odd: even accounting for air drag, pendulums seemed to hold onto their energy stubbornly. In a vacuum, the effect became more pronounced. The swing decayed, but not as fast as friction alone could explain.
