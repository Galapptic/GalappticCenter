Your Brain Makes Decisions Before You Know It

That feeling of choosing what to do next might be your brain's way of explaining a decision it already made hundreds of milliseconds ago.

Every time you decide to move your finger, grab a coffee, or change the channel, your brain has already made that decision before you consciously experience choosing it. This isn't philosophy—it's measurable neuroscience. Brain activity associated with a decision can be detected up to several seconds before a person reports being aware of deciding. Your conscious mind, that voice in your head that feels like 'you,' appears to be receiving news of decisions rather than making them. This challenges our deepest intuitions about who's really in control. We experience ourselves as the authors of our actions, but the timeline suggests consciousness might be more like a narrator describing events that have already been set in motion.
