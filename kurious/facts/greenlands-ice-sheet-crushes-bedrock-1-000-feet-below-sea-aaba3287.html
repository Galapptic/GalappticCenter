Greenland's ice sheet crushes bedrock 1,000 feet below sea level

Two miles of ice are so impossibly heavy they've pressed an entire landmass deep underwater.

Greenland sits beneath an ice sheet that covers 660,000 square miles. That's roughly three times the size of Texas, piled up to two miles thick in places. All that frozen water weighs an almost incomprehensible amount. The sheer mass of ice has pressed down on the bedrock underneath for thousands of years, deforming the Earth's crust like a thumb pushing into bread dough. Much of the ground beneath central Greenland now sits more than 1,000 feet below sea level. If you could somehow instantly remove all the ice, you'd find a giant bowl shaped depression filled with compressed rock where mountains once stood.
