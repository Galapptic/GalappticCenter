Male Anglerfish Permanently Melt Into Females to Become Parasites

In the crushing darkness of the deep sea, some male fish survive by literally fusing their bodies into females, dissolving away until only their testicles remain.

When a tiny male anglerfish finds a female in the pitch-black abyss, he bites into her flesh and never lets go. His mouth fuses with her skin, their blood vessels merge, and his body begins to waste away. Eyes, fins, and most organs dissolve as he becomes a permanent appendage. What remains is essentially a pair of testicles attached to the female, ready to release sperm whenever she produces eggs. Some females carry multiple males—up to eight parasitic partners have been found on a single fish. The male trades his independence for guaranteed reproduction in an ocean where finding a mate twice would be nearly impossible.
