A Moldy Accident Launched the Age of Antibiotics

Alexander Fleming's messy lab habits accidentally saved more lives than any other medical discovery in history.

In September 1928, Scottish bacteriologist Alexander Fleming returned from vacation to find something strange in his London laboratory. A petri dish containing Staphylococcus bacteria had been contaminated by mold. Rather than simply discarding it, Fleming noticed something remarkable: the bacteria surrounding the mold had died. The fuzzy intruder was Penicillium notatum, and it was secreting a substance that killed bacteria. Fleming named this bacteria-killing compound penicillin. Though he published his findings, Fleming lacked the resources to purify the substance for medical use. His accidental discovery would sit largely dormant for over a decade before others recognized its life-saving potential.
