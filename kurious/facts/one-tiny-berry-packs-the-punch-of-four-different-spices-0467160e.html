One Tiny Berry Packs the Punch of Four Different Spices

Allspice isn't a blend at all—it's a single berry that evolved to taste like cinnamon, nutmeg, cloves, and black pepper combined.

Despite what its name suggests, allspice is not a mixture of spices. It's the dried, unripe berry of the Pimenta dioica tree, native to the Caribbean and Central America. When European explorers first encountered it in Jamaica, they were baffled—this single seed somehow delivered the warming notes of cinnamon, the earthiness of nutmeg, the pungency of cloves, and hints of black pepper. English traders named it 'allspice' because it seemed to contain everything at once. The Mayans had been using it for centuries to embalm their leaders and flavor chocolate drinks. Today, it remains essential in Caribbean jerk seasoning, Middle Eastern cuisine, and holiday baking around the world.
