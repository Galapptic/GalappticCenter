Why Cooling Lava Builds Perfect Hexagons

The same geometry that makes honeycomb efficient also emerges spontaneously from molten rock—no bees required.

In 1693, a fellow of the Royal Society named Sir Richard Bulkeley sent a letter to his colleagues in London describing something he'd seen on the northeastern coast of Ireland: a vast pavement of stone columns, fitted together so precisely they looked machine-cut. Each pillar had five, six, or seven sides, and they rose from the sea in steps like a ruined staircase built for giants. The locals called it exactly that—the Giant's Causeway—and credited its construction to the mythical warrior Fionn mac Cumhaill.

Bulkeley suspected a more earthly origin. He was right, but it would take another century before geologists understood what they were looking at: the frozen signature of a very specific kind of cooling.
