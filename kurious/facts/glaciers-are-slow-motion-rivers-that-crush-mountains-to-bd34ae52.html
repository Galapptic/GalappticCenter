Glaciers Are Slow-Motion Rivers That Crush Mountains to Powder

The fine silt that turns glacial lakes milky blue is pulverized mountain, ground down grain by grain over thousands of years.

A glacier looks frozen solid, but it's actually moving—sometimes several meters a day. The immense weight of accumulated snow and ice, often hundreds of meters thick, creates enough pressure at the base to make ice behave like an extremely slow liquid. It deforms, slides, and flows downhill under its own mass, following valleys just as rivers do.
