Tiny Fungi Spent 1300 Years Perfecting Your Soup

A single spoonful of miso contains the living descendants of microbes that Japanese craftsmen first cultivated before the Vikings reached America.

That tub of miso in your fridge isn't just a condiment—it's a time capsule of microbial evolution. Miso paste begins as simple soybeans, but through the magic of koji mold (Aspergillus oryzae), it transforms into something extraordinary. This fuzzy fungus breaks down proteins and starches over months or even years, creating hundreds of flavor compounds that didn't exist before. The result? That deeply savory, almost meaty taste we call umami. Japanese brewers have passed down their koji starter cultures for generations, meaning the microbes making your miso today are direct descendants of those working in medieval Japan. Each batch carries forward centuries of flavor evolution.
