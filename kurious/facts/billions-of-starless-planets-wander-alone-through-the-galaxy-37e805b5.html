Billions of Starless Planets Wander Alone Through the Galaxy

For every star in the Milky Way, there may be more rogue planets drifting through eternal darkness, untethered to any solar system.

Somewhere in the vast darkness between stars, billions of planets drift alone. These rogue planets, also called free-floating planets, travel through interstellar space without orbiting any star. Unlike Earth, which enjoys the warmth of our Sun, these worlds exist in perpetual night, their surfaces frozen in temperatures near absolute zero. Scientists believe rogue planets form in two ways: they're either ejected from their home solar systems through gravitational chaos, or they form independently in interstellar gas clouds, never belonging to any star. Recent surveys suggest our galaxy alone may contain hundreds of billions of these cosmic wanderers, potentially outnumbering stars themselves. They're invisible in the conventional sense, making them among the most mysterious objects in the universe.
