The Dinosaur That Swam Like a Crocodile

For a century, paleontologists missed the obvious signs that the largest predatory dinosaur ever discovered spent most of its life underwater.

In 1912, a German paleontologist named Ernst Stromer dug up something extraordinary in the Egyptian Sahara: bones from a predator unlike any dinosaur seen before. It had a massive sail on its back, a long crocodile-like snout, and conical teeth built for gripping fish. Stromer named it Spinosaurus aegyptiacus and shipped the fossils to a museum in Munich. Then, in April 1944, an Allied bombing raid destroyed them completely.

For decades, Spinosaurus existed only in Stromer's drawings and descriptions. Scientists assumed it was just another large theropod, a land predator like Tyrannosaurus rex. They were spectacularly wrong.
