This jellyfish cheats death by turning its adult cells into babies

One species has cracked biological immortality, transforming its aging body back into a newborn whenever it faces death.

Most living things march in one direction: birth to death. But Turritopsis dohrnii, a jellyfish smaller than your pinky nail, can reverse the journey entirely. When injured, starved, or simply old, this transparent blob doesn't die. Instead, it sinks to the ocean floor and transforms itself back into a polyp, the juvenile stage that jellyfish start as before becoming free-swimming adults. It's as if a butterfly could turn back into a caterpillar, or a frog could become a tadpole again. Then the process starts over. The polyp grows, buds off new jellyfish, and those clones swim away to live another life.
