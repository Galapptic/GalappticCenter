Empty Space Pushes Metal Plates Together

The vacuum between two metal plates isn't actually empty—it's buzzing with enough quantum activity to create a measurable force.

Bring two smooth metal plates close enough together in a vacuum, and they'll nudge toward each other with no apparent cause. No magnetism, no static, no air pressure. Just the plates and the void between them. This is the Casimir effect, predicted by Dutch physicist Hendrik Casimir in 1948 and confirmed experimentally decades later.
