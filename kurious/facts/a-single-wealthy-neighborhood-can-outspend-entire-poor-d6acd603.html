A Single Wealthy Neighborhood Can Outspend Entire Poor Districts

Some PTAs raise more money annually than entire school districts receive in state funding for thousands of students.

In America, your address can determine your education quality more than any test score. Wealthy neighborhoods routinely spend over $20,000 per student annually, while poor districts scrape by on less than $8,000. This isn't just about private donations—it's baked into the system. Since schools are largely funded by local property taxes, expensive homes mean expensive educations. A single affluent suburb might generate more school revenue than an entire urban district serving ten times as many students. Parent-teacher associations in wealthy areas sometimes raise millions for extras like science labs, art programs, and smaller class sizes—luxuries that poorer schools consider impossible dreams.
