Stop-Motion Animation Is a Beautiful Trick on Your Brain

Every time you watch Wallace and Gromit, your visual system is being fooled by a glitch that scientists still argue about.

A stop-motion animator might spend an entire day creating just a few seconds of footage. They move a puppet a fraction of a millimeter, snap a photograph, adjust it again, and repeat—sometimes hundreds of times for a single scene. Yet when played back at 24 frames per second, the jerky snapshots transform into fluid motion. The secret lies in how your brain processes visual information.
