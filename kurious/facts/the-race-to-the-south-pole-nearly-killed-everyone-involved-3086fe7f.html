The Race to the South Pole Nearly Killed Everyone Involved

Roald Amundsen's triumphant Antarctic expedition came within days of disaster, while Robert Scott's team perished just eleven miles from salvation.

In the summer of 1911, two teams of men hauled themselves across the most hostile landscape on Earth, racing toward an invisible point that no human had ever reached. The Norwegian explorer Roald Amundsen and British naval officer Robert Falcon Scott both wanted to be first to the South Pole. Amundsen won. Scott died trying. But the story usually told—of Norwegian triumph and British tragedy—obscures how close both expeditions came to total catastrophe.
