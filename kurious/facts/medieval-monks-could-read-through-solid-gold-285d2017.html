Medieval Monks Could Read Through Solid Gold

The gold leaf used in illuminated manuscripts was hammered so thin that light passed through it, letting scribes see their guidelines beneath the glittering surface.

A single ounce of gold can be beaten into a sheet covering 300 square feet. In medieval scriptoria, craftsmen pushed this even further, pounding gold into leaves just 0.1 micrometers thick—about 500 times thinner than a human hair. At that thickness, gold stops looking golden. It turns translucent, glowing faintly green when held to the light.
