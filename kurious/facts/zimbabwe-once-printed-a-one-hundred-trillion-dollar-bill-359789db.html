Zimbabwe Once Printed a One Hundred Trillion Dollar Bill

At hyperinflation's peak, prices doubled every 24 hours, making your morning coffee unaffordable by lunchtime.

Imagine needing a wheelbarrow of cash to buy bread. In Zimbabwe during 2008, this nightmare became reality. The government printed money so frantically that they eventually issued a one hundred trillion dollar banknote—the highest denomination ever printed. Workers demanded wages twice daily because waiting until evening meant their pay had already lost half its value. Shop owners changed prices multiple times per day, sometimes per hour. People stopped using Zimbabwean dollars entirely, switching to US dollars, South African rand, or simple barter. The currency became so worthless that people used stacks of bills as wallpaper or toilet paper—the paper itself was worth more than the printed amount.
