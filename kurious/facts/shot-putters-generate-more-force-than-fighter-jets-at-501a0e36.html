Shot Putters Generate More Force Than Fighter Jets at Takeoff

In the fraction of a second before a shot put leaves an athlete's hand, their body produces forces that exceed what keeps an F-16 airborne.

The shot put looks deceptively simple: pick up a heavy ball, throw it as far as possible. But the physics happening in that final explosive moment—when the athlete's arm snaps forward and the shot leaves their fingers—involves forces that rival aerospace engineering. Biomechanical studies have measured elite shot putters generating peak forces above 600 pounds during release, concentrated into a push lasting barely 0.1 seconds.
