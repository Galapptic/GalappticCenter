This Material Is Stronger Than Steel Yet Nearly Invisible

A single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a honeycomb pattern creates a material 200 times stronger than steel that you could see through like glass.

Graphene sounds like science fiction: a material just one atom thick that's stronger than any steel ever forged. It's made entirely of carbon atoms arranged in a perfect hexagonal lattice, like an impossibly thin honeycomb. Despite being so thin that millions of layers stacked together would barely match a human hair, graphene is remarkably tough. You'd need an elephant balanced on a pencil point to puncture a sheet the thickness of plastic wrap. Yet light passes right through it—graphene absorbs only about 2.3% of visible light, making it nearly transparent. This paradox of extreme strength and ethereal thinness has made graphene one of the most exciting materials discovered in the 21st century.
