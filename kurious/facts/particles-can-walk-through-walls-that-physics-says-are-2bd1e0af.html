Particles Can Walk Through Walls That Physics Says Are Impossible

Every second, particles inside your body are performing impossible escapes through barriers they shouldn't be able to cross.

Imagine throwing a ball at a wall and watching it appear on the other side—not breaking through, just suddenly being there. That's quantum tunneling, and it happens constantly at the subatomic level. In classical physics, a particle lacking enough energy to overcome a barrier simply bounces back. End of story. But quantum mechanics plays by different rules. Particles don't have precise positions; they exist as probability waves spread through space. When this wave encounters a barrier, it doesn't completely stop. Instead, it decays exponentially through the obstacle, and if the barrier is thin enough, some probability leaks out the other side. The particle can literally materialize beyond a wall it never had the energy to climb.
