Why Shogi Players Obsess Over the Direction of Wood Grain

In Japanese chess championships, players sometimes burn precious minutes of clock time simply rotating a wooden tile until its grain lines up perfectly—and the rules allow it.

Shogi, often called Japanese chess, shares an ancestor with the Western game but evolved into something stranger. Both sides play with identical wedge-shaped wooden pieces distinguished only by which direction they point. Capture an enemy piece and it becomes yours—flip it around, drop it anywhere, and it fights for your army. This recycling mechanic means games rarely end in draws. But it also means every piece must clearly show whose side it's on.
