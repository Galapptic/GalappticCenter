Sperm whales sleep vertically, floating like giant motionless submarines

The world's biggest toothed predators drift in the deep, standing completely still in formation like an underwater forest.

Sperm whales don't sleep like other animals. They don't curl up on the seafloor or swim slowly near the surface. Instead, they hang perfectly vertical in the water column, heads pointing up toward the distant surface, tails dangling below. They form small groups of five or six individuals, all bobbing motionless together like enormous floating logs. The whales remain completely still during these moments, suspended between the sunlit world above and the crushing darkness below. Scientists only discovered this behavior in 2008, when researchers accidentally drifted into a group of vertically sleeping whales off the coast of Chile.
