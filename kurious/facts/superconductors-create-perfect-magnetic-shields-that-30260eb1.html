Superconductors Create Perfect Magnetic Shields That Levitate Objects

When cooled to extreme temperatures, certain materials become so magnetically hostile that they physically push magnets away, creating seemingly impossible floating objects.

When you cool certain materials to incredibly low temperatures, something almost magical happens. They become superconductors, meaning electricity flows through them with zero resistance. But that's not the only trick. These materials completely expel magnetic fields from their interior, a phenomenon called the Meissner effect. Place a magnet above a superconductor, and it floats in midair. The superconductor creates perfect opposing magnetic fields that push back against the magnet with precise force. It's not just repulsion—it's total magnetic exclusion. The magnet becomes locked in space, stable and hovering, as if gravity itself has been partially cancelled. This isn't science fiction; it's physics at work.
