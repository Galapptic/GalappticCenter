Antarctica's saltiest pond stays liquid at negative fifty degrees Celsius

In Antarctica's driest valley lies a shallow pond so salty that winter cannot touch it.

Don Juan Pond sits in Antarctica's McMurdo Dry Valleys, one of the most hostile environments on Earth. While everything around it is locked in ice, this shallow pond remains stubbornly liquid even when temperatures plunge to negative fifty degrees Celsius. The secret is salt. This isn't your average seawater though. Don Juan Pond is nearly ten times saltier than the ocean, making it the saltiest body of water on our planet. It's so salty that ice crystals simply cannot form, no matter how brutal the cold becomes.
