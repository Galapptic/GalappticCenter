Western Scientists Dismissed Umami Taste for Nearly 100 Years

A Japanese chemist discovered the fifth basic taste in 1908, but Western science refused to accept it until 2002.

You know sweet, salty, sour, and bitter. But there's a fifth taste that makes parmesan cheese addictive, ripe tomatoes irresistible, and soy sauce savory perfection. It's called umami, Japanese for 'pleasant savory taste.' In 1908, chemist Kikunae Ikeda was sipping dashi broth when he noticed a distinct flavor that didn't fit the four accepted tastes. He isolated the compound responsible: glutamate, an amino acid. Ikeda published his findings and even invented MSG to replicate the taste. But Western scientists dismissed his discovery as merely a combination of other tastes or an Asian cultural concept. It would take almost a century for them to admit he was right all along.
