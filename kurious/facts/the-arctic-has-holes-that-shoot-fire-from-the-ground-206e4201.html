The Arctic Has Holes That Shoot Fire from the Ground

Across Siberia, craters the size of city blocks are blasting open as ancient methane, trapped for tens of thousands of years, erupts from thawing permafrost and ignites on contact with air.

In July 2014, helicopter pilots spotted something impossible on Russia's Yamal Peninsula: a nearly perfect cylindrical hole, 30 meters wide and at least 70 meters deep, punched into the frozen tundra. Blackened scorch marks ringed its edges. Local Nenets reindeer herders reported hearing an explosion. Scientists who rappelled into the crater found methane concentrations 50,000 times higher than normal atmospheric levels. The permafrost had burped—violently.
