Sovereign Citizens Insist Traffic Laws Simply Do Not Apply to Them

Thousands of Americans genuinely believe that by saying magic words during traffic stops, they can exempt themselves from all laws.

Sovereign citizens form a loosely connected movement of people who believe the government has no authority over them. They argue that by declaring themselves 'sovereign,' they become exempt from taxes, traffic laws, and most legal obligations. During police encounters, they often recite pseudo-legal phrases like 'I do not consent' or 'I'm traveling, not driving,' believing these incantations carry legal weight. They distinguish between being a 'person' (subject to law) and a 'free man' (exempt from everything). The FBI estimates hundreds of thousands of Americans hold these beliefs to some degree. Courts universally reject sovereign citizen arguments, but adherents interpret losses as proof the system is corrupt rather than evidence their theories are wrong.
