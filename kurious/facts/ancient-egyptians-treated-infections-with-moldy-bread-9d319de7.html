Ancient Egyptians treated infections with moldy bread before penicillin

The same fuzzy green stuff you scrape off old bread saved lives in ancient Egypt long before modern antibiotics.

Ancient Egyptian physicians pressed moldy bread directly onto infected wounds. This wasn't superstition or placebo. Medical papyri from over 3,000 years ago specifically describe using spoiled bread as treatment for infections and inflammation. The Ebers Papyrus, one of the oldest medical texts ever discovered, details the practice explicitly. What the Egyptians didn't know was why it worked. They observed that certain moldy substances helped wounds heal and infections clear. They were accidentally harnessing the power of naturally occurring antibiotics, the same basic principle Alexander Fleming would famously rediscover in 1928 when a contaminated petri dish led to penicillin.
