Medieval Dancers Could Not Stop Until They Collapsed in Streets

In the summer of 1518, a woman began dancing in a Strasbourg street and within a month, 400 people had joined her uncontrollable marathon.

In July 1518, a woman named Frau Troffea stepped into a narrow Strasbourg street and began dancing. She couldn't stop. For days she twisted and gyrated without music, rest, or apparent joy. Within a week, thirty others had joined her involuntary performance. By August, the number swelled to around 400 afflicted dancers. These weren't celebrations—witnesses described contorted faces, exhausted bodies, and people begging for the compulsion to end. Many danced until they collapsed from exhaustion, strokes, or heart attacks. City officials, baffled and desperate, initially encouraged more dancing, even building a stage and hiring musicians, believing sufferers needed to dance the fever out of their systems.
