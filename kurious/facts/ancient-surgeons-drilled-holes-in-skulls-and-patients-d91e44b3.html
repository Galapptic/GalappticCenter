Ancient Surgeons Drilled Holes in Skulls and Patients Survived

Thousands of prehistoric skulls show evidence of deliberate drilling—and bone regrowth proves many patients lived for years afterward.

Trepanation is humanity's oldest known surgical procedure—deliberately drilling, cutting, or scraping a hole through the skull while the patient is still alive. Archaeological evidence shows this was practiced as far back as 7,000 years ago, across every inhabited continent. What's truly remarkable? Many patients survived. We know this because their skulls show clear signs of bone healing around the holes, indicating they lived months or even decades after the operation. Some skulls have multiple healed trepanation sites, meaning people underwent this terrifying procedure more than once. Stone Age surgeons achieved survival rates that wouldn't be matched again until the antiseptic era of modern medicine.
