Scientists Tried to Drill Through Earth's Crust in the 1960s

Before the Moon landing captured the world's attention, American scientists launched an equally audacious mission—straight down.

In 1957, a group of geologists gathered for drinks at a San Diego wine bar and hatched one of the most ambitious scientific projects of the twentieth century. Their goal: punch a hole through Earth's crust to reach the mantle, the hot, semi-solid layer that makes up 84 percent of our planet's volume. No one had ever sampled it directly. Everything scientists knew about it came from seismic waves and mathematical guesswork.
