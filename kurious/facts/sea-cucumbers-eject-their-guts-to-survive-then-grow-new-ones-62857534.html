Sea cucumbers eject their guts to survive, then grow new ones

Imagine escaping danger by hurling your internal organs at your attacker, then casually growing them back over a few weeks.

When a sea cucumber faces a hungry predator, it does something almost unthinkable. It violently contracts its body and ejects a tangled mass of its internal organs out through its rear end. This isn't a death throe. It's a calculated defense strategy called evisceration. The predator gets distracted by the sudden offering of fresh tissue, giving the sea cucumber time to crawl away to safety. Over the next few weeks, the sea cucumber regenerates everything it lost. New intestines, new respiratory organs, sometimes even new gonads. It's like hitting a biological reset button that would kill almost any other animal.
