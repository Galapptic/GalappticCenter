One Syllable Can Mean Four Completely Different Things

In Mandarin Chinese, saying 'ma' with a rising tone means 'hemp,' but say it with a falling tone and you've just called someone's mother a horse.

In tonal languages, the pitch of your voice isn't just emotional expression—it's the entire meaning. Take Mandarin's famous example: 'mā' with a high flat tone means 'mother,' 'má' with a rising tone means 'hemp,' 'mǎ' with a dipping tone means 'horse,' and 'mà' with a falling tone means 'to scold.' Same consonant, same vowel, completely different words. About 70% of the world's languages use tone to distinguish meaning, including Chinese, Vietnamese, Thai, and many African languages like Yoruba and Igbo. For speakers of non-tonal languages like English, this feels like learning to sing while speaking—because essentially, that's exactly what it is.
