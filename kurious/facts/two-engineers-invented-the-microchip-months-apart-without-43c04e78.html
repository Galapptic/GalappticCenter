Two Engineers Invented the Microchip Months Apart Without Knowing

In 1959, two men working in different states filed nearly identical patents for the integrated circuit within six months of each other, neither aware the other existed.

In the late 1950s, Jack Kilby at Texas Instruments and Robert Noyce at Fairchild Semiconductor were both wrestling with the same problem: electronics were getting too complex. Devices needed thousands of transistors, each hand-wired together, creating unreliable tangles of connections. Working completely independently, both men arrived at the same revolutionary solution—put multiple transistors on a single piece of semiconductor material. Kilby filed his patent in February 1959. Noyce filed his in July. Neither had any idea the other was working on identical technology. The patent office was stunned. Here were two completely separate inventions of arguably the most important technology of the 20th century, developed in parallel.
