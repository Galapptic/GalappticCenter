The Parthenon Is Crooked So Your Eyes See It Straight

Ancient Greek architects deliberately built one of history's most famous structures with almost no straight lines—because perfectly straight lines would have looked wrong.

Stand in front of the Parthenon and everything looks flawlessly straight: the columns, the platform, the horizontal beams stretching across the top. But pull out a level and you'd find almost nothing is actually level. The floor bows upward. The columns lean inward. The corner pillars are thicker than the rest. Every one of these "mistakes" was calculated with mathematical precision—and without them, the temple would look subtly wrong to the human eye.
