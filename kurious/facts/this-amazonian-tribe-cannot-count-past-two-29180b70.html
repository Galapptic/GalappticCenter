This Amazonian Tribe Cannot Count Past Two

The Pirahã language has no words for exact numbers, only terms meaning 'small amount' and 'larger amount,' challenging everything linguists thought they knew about human cognition.

Deep in the Brazilian Amazon, roughly 800 Pirahã people live along the Maici River with a language unlike any other on Earth. They have no words for specific numbers. Instead, they use approximate terms: 'hói' for small quantities and 'hoí' for larger ones. When researchers asked them to match groups of objects, they succeeded with small amounts but struggled once quantities exceeded three or four. They don't count on their fingers. They have no system for tracking exact quantities. Yet they thrive as hunter-gatherers, trading successfully and managing their resources through estimation and relative comparison rather than precise enumeration.
