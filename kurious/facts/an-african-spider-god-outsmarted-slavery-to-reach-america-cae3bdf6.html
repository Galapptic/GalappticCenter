An African Spider God Outsmarted Slavery to Reach America

Enslaved Africans preserved their trickster deity Anansi through oral storytelling, transforming him into a symbol of resistance that still appears in Caribbean and American folklore today.

Anansi is a spider god from the Akan people of Ghana who uses cunning and wit rather than strength to defeat opponents. In West African mythology, he's credited with bringing stories to humanity after tricking the sky god Nyame. When millions of Africans were forcibly transported across the Atlantic during the slave trade, they couldn't bring physical possessions—but they brought Anansi in their memories. The spider became a secret hero on plantations, representing the power of intelligence over brute force. His stories spread through whispered tales at night, preserving African identity when everything else was stripped away. Anansi proved that some things simply cannot be enslaved.
