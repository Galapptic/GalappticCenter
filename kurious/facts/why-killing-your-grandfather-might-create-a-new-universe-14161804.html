Why Killing Your Grandfather Might Create a New Universe

A thought experiment designed to prove time travel impossible may have accidentally shown us how it could work.

In 1943, science fiction writer René Barjavel posed a deceptively simple question in his novel Le Voyageur Imprudent: What happens if a time traveler kills his own grandfather before his father is born? The traveler would never exist. But if he never existed, he couldn't travel back to commit the murder. So his grandfather survives, and the traveler is born after all—free to go back and kill him again. The logic spirals forever, each conclusion canceling itself out.
