How Hitchcock Taught the Camera to Feel Fear

Before Alfred Hitchcock, cameras simply watched characters—he made them become characters, trapping audiences inside minds they couldn't escape.

In 1958, audiences watching Vertigo experienced something unprecedented. As detective Scottie Ferguson peers down a stairwell, the camera performs an impossible movement: it zooms in while simultaneously pulling back, warping the architecture into a nauseating spiral. Viewers didn't just see his fear of heights—they felt their own stomachs lurch. This wasn't a gimmick. It was Alfred Hitchcock's declaration that cinema could do more than show you a story. It could trap you inside someone else's terror.
