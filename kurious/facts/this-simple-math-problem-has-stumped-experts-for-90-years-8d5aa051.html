This Simple Math Problem Has Stumped Experts for 90 Years

Pick any number, apply an elementary school rule repeatedly, and you'll create a mystery that has defeated the world's greatest mathematicians.

Here's a game anyone can play: pick any positive whole number. If it's even, divide by two. If it's odd, multiply by three and add one. Repeat with your new number. Try it with 7: you get 22, 11, 34, 17, 52, 26, 13, 40, 20, 10, 5, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1. Every number mathematicians have tested eventually reaches 1. The Collatz conjecture claims this happens for every starting number, no matter how large. Sounds easy to prove, right? Yet since German mathematician Lothar Collatz proposed it in 1937, nobody has managed to prove—or disprove—this seemingly innocent claim. Some numbers take wild rides before reaching 1, creating unpredictable sequences that defy analysis.
