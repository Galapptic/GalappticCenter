Prime Numbers Hide Inside Pi in Completely Unpredictable Ways

The most famous number in mathematics contains every prime number ever discovered, yet no one can predict where any of them will appear.

Pi's decimal expansion goes on forever without repeating: 3.14159265358979... and it never stops. Buried within those endless digits, you'll find prime numbers everywhere. The sequence 2, then 3, then 5, then 7—they're all there. But here's the mystery: mathematicians believe pi is what they call a "normal" number, meaning every possible sequence of digits appears with equal frequency. That would include every prime number, every phone number, even your birthday. The catch? Despite centuries of study, nobody can prove this about pi, and nobody can predict where any particular prime will show up next. It's mathematical chaos hiding in plain sight.
