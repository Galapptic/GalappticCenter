The Dead Sea Sits at Earth's Lowest Point on Land

At 430 meters below sea level, you could stack the Empire State Building here and still be underground.

The Dead Sea shoreline sits approximately 430 meters below sea level, making it the lowest exposed land surface on our planet. This isn't just slightly below sea level—it's dramatically lower than anywhere else you could stand without diving underwater. The lake itself plunges even deeper, with its floor reaching about 730 meters below sea level at certain points. Located between Jordan and Israel, this hypersaline lake earned its name because almost nothing can survive in water nearly ten times saltier than the ocean. The extreme depth creates fascinating effects: the air pressure here is measurably higher than at sea level, and the extra atmosphere actually filters more UV radiation, reducing sunburn risk.
