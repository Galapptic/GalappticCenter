Tiny Sea Shells Powered Global Trade for Four Thousand Years

Before gold coins existed, billions of small mollusc shells became the world's first truly international currency, accepted from Beijing to Benin.

Long before credit cards or paper bills, cowrie shells circulated as money across Africa, Asia, and Oceania. These small, glossy shells from the Maldive Islands became humanity's most widespread currency, used continuously for over four millennia. What made them ideal? They were durable, portable, impossible to counterfeit, and naturally beautiful. You couldn't forge them since they came only from specific tropical waters. Merchants from China to West Africa recognized their value instantly. A handful of shells could buy food in markets separated by thousands of miles. The cowrie system worked because everyone agreed these shells held worth, the same psychological contract underlying all money throughout history.
