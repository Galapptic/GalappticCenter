The Printing Press Sparked Europe's First Intellectual Property Wars

Within decades of Gutenberg's revolutionary invention, pirate printers were stealing manuscripts, forging famous authors' names, and driving the original publishers into bankruptcy—creating legal battles that would shape copyright law for centuries.

Johannes Gutenberg probably thought his biggest problem was money. The German goldsmith had spent years perfecting his movable type system in Mainz, borrowing heavily to fund his experiments. When he finally printed his famous Bible around 1455, his creditor Johann Fust sued him, seized his equipment, and went into the printing business himself. But Gutenberg's personal tragedy was just the opening act. His invention would unleash chaos across Europe—not just a revolution in knowledge, but an explosion of theft, forgery, and legal warfare that governments scrambled to contain.
