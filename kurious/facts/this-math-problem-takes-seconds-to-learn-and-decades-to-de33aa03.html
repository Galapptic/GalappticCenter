This Math Problem Takes Seconds to Learn and Decades to Solve

Pick any number, follow two simple rules, and you'll always reach 1—except nobody can prove why.

Here's a game any eight-year-old can play: pick any positive number. If it's even, divide by two. If it's odd, multiply by three and add one. Repeat. Mathematicians have tried every number up to billions of billions, and they all eventually spiral down to 1. The Collatz Conjecture claims this always happens—no matter which number you start with. Sounds obvious, right? Yet since Lothar Collatz proposed it in 1937, no one has proven it true. Paul Erdős, one of history's greatest mathematicians, said mathematics is not yet ready for such problems. The rules fit on a napkin, but the proof has eluded humanity for nearly a century.
