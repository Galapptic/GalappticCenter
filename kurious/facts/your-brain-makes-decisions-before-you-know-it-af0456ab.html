Your Brain Makes Decisions Before You Know It

In 1983, a neuroscientist discovered that your brain starts preparing to act a full half-second before you feel like you've decided anything.

Benjamin Libet was measuring something nobody had tried to time before: the gap between a brain signal and a conscious choice. His subjects sat in a lab at the University of California, San Francisco, watching a clock face while electrodes monitored their brain activity. Their task was simple—flex your wrist whenever you feel like it, and note exactly when you decided to move.

What Libet found unsettled philosophers and neuroscientists for decades. The brain's motor cortex began firing about 550 milliseconds before the wrist moved. But subjects reported feeling the urge to act only about 200 milliseconds before movement. The brain, it seemed, had already made the decision 350 milliseconds before the person felt they had chosen anything at all.
