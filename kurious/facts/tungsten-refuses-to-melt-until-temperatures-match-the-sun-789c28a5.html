Tungsten Refuses to Melt Until Temperatures Match the Sun

With a melting point of 3,422°C, tungsten stays solid at temperatures that would vaporize nearly every other metal on Earth.

Tungsten holds an extraordinary record: the highest melting point of any pure metal. At 3,422°C (6,192°F), it stays solid at temperatures where steel becomes liquid soup and even titanium surrenders. For comparison, iron melts at just 1,538°C. The Sun's surface burns at about 5,500°C, meaning tungsten survives conditions roughly two-thirds as hot as a star's outer layer. This incredible heat resistance comes from tungsten's tightly packed atomic structure and exceptionally strong metallic bonds. The electrons holding tungsten atoms together require tremendous energy to break apart. This makes tungsten indispensable when engineers need materials that absolutely cannot fail under extreme thermal stress.
