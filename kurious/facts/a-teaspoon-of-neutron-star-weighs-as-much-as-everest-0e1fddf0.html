A Teaspoon of Neutron Star Weighs as Much as Everest

The densest objects in the universe pack more mass into a sugar cube than every human who ever lived combined.

Imagine scooping up a teaspoon of material that weighs six trillion kilograms—roughly the mass of Mount Everest. That's the reality of neutron star matter. These stellar remnants are so incredibly dense that their gravity crushes atoms beyond recognition. When massive stars die in supernova explosions, their cores collapse catastrophically. Protons and electrons are forced together to form neutrons, which pack together with almost no empty space between them. A neutron star typically crams about 1.4 times our Sun's mass into a sphere just 20 kilometers across—smaller than most cities. The gravitational pull at the surface is about 200 billion times stronger than Earth's, making these objects the densest things in the universe besides black holes.
