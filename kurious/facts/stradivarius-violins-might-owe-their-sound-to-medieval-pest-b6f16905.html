Stradivarius violins might owe their sound to medieval pest control

The world's most valuable violins may sound magical because their wood was treated with common household borax.

Antonio Stradivari crafted violins in Cremona, Italy during the late 1600s and early 1700s that musicians still consider the finest ever made. These instruments sell for millions of dollars today. For centuries, people have wondered what makes them sound so extraordinary. Some credited the varnish. Others pointed to the wood from alpine forests during a mini ice age. But recent chemical analysis has revealed something unexpected. The wood in Stradivarius violins contains traces of borax, aluminum, and other minerals that don't occur naturally in maple or spruce. Someone deliberately treated this wood before it became music.
