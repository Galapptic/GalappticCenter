Mudpots bubble with acid hot enough to dissolve bone

These pools of boiling mud aren't just hot water and dirt mixing together on the surface.

Walk up to a mudpot and you'll hear it before you see it. The air fills with wet, rhythmic glopping sounds as thick gray or brown mud churns and spits globs into the air. These aren't just hot springs that happen to be muddy. The pools contain water so acidic it literally dissolves the surrounding rock into clay. Steam rises from the surface while bubbles swell and pop with surprising force. Some mudpots bubble gently like simmering oatmeal. Others explode violently, hurling mud several feet into the air. The temperature often reaches boiling point, powered by volcanic heat radiating up from deep underground chambers of magma.
