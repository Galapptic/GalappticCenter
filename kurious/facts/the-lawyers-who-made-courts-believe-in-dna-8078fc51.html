The Lawyers Who Made Courts Believe in DNA

In 1989, American courts considered DNA evidence junk science—today it has freed over 375 wrongfully convicted people, many from death row, thanks to two lawyers who refused to accept that prisons held only the guilty.

Gary Dotson spent eight years in an Illinois prison for a rape that never happened. In 1989, DNA testing proved the semen on the victim's clothing wasn't his. The judge didn't care. He ruled the technology too new, too unproven, and sent Dotson back to his cell. It took another three years and a governor's pardon before Dotson finally walked free—the first American exonerated by DNA evidence. The courts that should have welcomed scientific truth had slammed their doors shut.

Two young lawyers in New York watched cases like Dotson's pile up and saw something others missed: the criminal justice system wasn't just making occasional mistakes. It was structurally incapable of correcting them. In 1992, they decided to force the issue.
