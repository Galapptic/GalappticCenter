More Witnesses Actually Means Less Help for Victims

In emergencies, a crowd of 50 people can be less helpful than a single passerby walking alone.

You'd think collapsing on a busy street would be safer than on an empty one. More people means more potential helpers, right? Psychologists discovered the opposite is true. When multiple witnesses observe an emergency, each individual becomes significantly less likely to intervene. This counterintuitive phenomenon, called the bystander effect, happens because responsibility gets mentally divided among everyone present. If you're the only witness, it's entirely on you. But with fifty others around, your brain calculates that surely someone else will step in. Everyone makes this same calculation simultaneously, often resulting in a victim surrounded by passive observers, each waiting for another person to act first.
