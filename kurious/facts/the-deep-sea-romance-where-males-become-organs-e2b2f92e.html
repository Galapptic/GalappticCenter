The Deep-Sea Romance Where Males Become Organs

In the pitch-black abyss, female anglerfish don't just mate with males—they absorb them into their bodies permanently, two fish becoming one flesh.

When scientists first examined deep-sea anglerfish in the early 1900s, they noticed something odd: females often had strange lumps attached to their bodies. For years, researchers assumed these were parasites or tumors. Then, in 1922, Icelandic biologist Bjarni Sæmundsson made a startling discovery. The lumps had eyes. They had fins. They were males—or what remained of them.
