The Instrument You Play Without Touching

In 1920, a Soviet physicist accidentally invented an instrument played entirely by waving your hands through invisible electromagnetic fields—and it became the voice of science fiction.

Most instruments require physical contact. You press piano keys, pluck guitar strings, blow into a saxophone. But the theremin demands something stranger: you stand before two metal antennas and sculpt sound from thin air. Move your right hand closer to the vertical antenna and the pitch rises. Pull it away and the pitch drops. Your left hand controls volume near a horizontal loop. The instrument responds to the position of your body within its electromagnetic field, detecting changes in capacitance caused by the proximity of human flesh. It sounds like science fiction because, in many ways, it helped create science fiction.
