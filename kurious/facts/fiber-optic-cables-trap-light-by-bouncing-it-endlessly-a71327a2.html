Fiber Optic Cables Trap Light by Bouncing It Endlessly Inside

The internet travels at the speed of light through glass threads thinner than human hair, bouncing thousands of times per meter without ever escaping.

When light hits a boundary between two materials at a steep enough angle, something remarkable happens: it reflects completely, with zero light escaping. This is total internal reflection, and it's the secret behind fiber optic cables. Glass fibers are engineered with a core surrounded by cladding with a slightly lower refractive index. Light entering the core bounces off the boundary at angles too steep to escape, zigzagging down the fiber for kilometers. It's like rolling a ball inside a perfectly smooth pipe—except the ball is traveling at 186,000 miles per second. This trapped light carries phone calls, streaming video, and internet data across oceans and continents.
