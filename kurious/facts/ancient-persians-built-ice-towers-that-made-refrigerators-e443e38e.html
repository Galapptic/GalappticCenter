Ancient Persians built ice towers that made refrigerators in deserts

In the scorching Iranian desert, massive mud structures created ice overnight when temperatures soared past 100°F during the day.

Around 400 BCE, Persian engineers constructed enormous dome structures called yakhchals that stored ice throughout brutal summer months. These weren't simple ice houses. They were sophisticated refrigeration systems built entirely from mud brick, standing up to 60 feet tall. During winter nights, water froze in shallow pools beside the towers. Workers collected the ice before dawn and stored it inside the domes, where it stayed frozen for months. Even when outside temperatures climbed past 100°F, people could access ice cream, chilled food, and cold drinks. The technology spread across Iran and beyond, with some yakhchals remaining functional into the 20th century.
