The Technology That Learned to See with Sound

Engineers spent decades trying to perfect underwater navigation before realizing bats had solved the problem 50 million years ago.

In 1912, the Titanic sank after striking an iceberg no one saw coming. Within weeks, engineers began racing to build devices that could detect objects in darkness and murky water. The answer came not from physics labs but from nature: bats and dolphins had been navigating by sound for millions of years.
