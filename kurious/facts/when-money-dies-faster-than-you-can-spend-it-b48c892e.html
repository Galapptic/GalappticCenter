When Money Dies Faster Than You Can Spend It

In 2008 Zimbabwe, prices doubled every 24 hours, making millionaires and paupers indistinguishable.

Imagine buying coffee in the morning for $10, then watching that same coffee cost $20 by evening. That's hyperinflation—when currency loses value so rapidly that prices spiral out of control. Unlike normal inflation, which might creep up a few percent yearly, hyperinflation explodes exponentially. People rush to spend money the instant they receive it because waiting even hours means losing purchasing power. Savings accounts become worthless. Wheelbarrows of cash can't buy groceries. Zimbabwe's 2008 crisis saw inflation reach 89.7 sextillion percent—a number so absurd it defies comprehension. Venezuela's recent collapse left citizens unable to afford basic food despite holding millions in local currency.
