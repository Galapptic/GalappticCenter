A wave of molasses killed 21 people in Boston

On a mild January day, a tank explosion sent 2.3 million gallons of molasses through Boston's streets at 35 mph.

January 15, 1919 started as an unusually warm day in Boston's North End. The temperature had jumped from freezing to nearly 40 degrees. Then at 12:30 pm, a massive storage tank containing 2.3 million gallons of molasses exploded without warning. A wave of sticky syrup, standing 25 feet high, roared through the streets at 35 miles per hour. The flood destroyed buildings, crushed train cars, and killed 21 people. Another 150 were injured. Cleanup took weeks, and the harbor ran brown for months. Boston residents claimed they could smell molasses on hot summer days for decades afterward.
