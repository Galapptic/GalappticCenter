The Desert Mirror That Makes Pilots Forget Which Way Is Up

In northern Chile, a salt flat reflects the sky so perfectly that aircraft have crashed because pilots couldn't tell where the ground began.

The Atacama Desert is the driest place on Earth outside Antarctica. In some weather stations, no rain has ever been recorded. But scattered across this moonscape are vast salt flats—remnants of ancient lakes that evaporated millions of years ago. After rare rains, a thin layer of water transforms these flats into the world's largest natural mirrors.
