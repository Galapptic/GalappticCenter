Dead Stars Become Cosmic Lighthouses Spinning Hundreds of Times Per Second

When massive stars die, their cores collapse into objects so dense that a teaspoon weighs billions of tons—and they spin so fast they sweep beams of radiation across the universe like lighthouse beacons.

When a massive star explodes as a supernova, its core doesn't just disappear—it collapses into one of the universe's strangest objects: a neutron star. Imagine cramming the Sun's mass into a city-sized ball. These stellar remnants spin incredibly fast, some rotating hundreds of times per second. As they spin, they blast powerful beams of radiation from their magnetic poles. Because these poles aren't perfectly aligned with the rotation axis, the beams sweep through space like a cosmic lighthouse. When one of these beams happens to point toward Earth with each rotation, we detect it as a pulsar—a rhythmic flash of radio waves so precise it rivals atomic clocks.
