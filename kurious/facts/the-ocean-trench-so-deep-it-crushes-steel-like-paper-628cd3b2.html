The Ocean Trench So Deep It Crushes Steel Like Paper

Off the coast of Chile, a crack in the Earth's crust plunges so deep that if you dropped Mount Everest into it, the peak would still be underwater.

The Atacama Trench—also called the Peru-Chile Trench—runs for 5,900 kilometers along South America's western edge, a dark scar in the Pacific seafloor. At its deepest point, it reaches 8,065 meters below the waves. Mount Everest, by comparison, rises 8,849 meters above sea level. Drop the mountain into this abyss and you'd still have nearly 800 meters of water above its summit.

Down there, the pressure reaches roughly 800 atmospheres—800 times what you feel standing at sea level. At those depths, the ocean doesn't just press against objects. It infiltrates them, finds every weakness, and collapses them from the inside out.
