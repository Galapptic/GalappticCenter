Your brain treats imaginary threats like they're about to kill you

That racing heart before a presentation? Your brain thinks you're facing a literal predator right now.

Your heart pounds before a job interview exactly the same way it would if a bear were charging at you. That's because your amygdala, the brain's ancient alarm system, can't distinguish between an actual physical threat and something you're merely thinking about. When you imagine giving a terrible presentation or picture your partner leaving you, your amygdala triggers the same cascade of stress hormones as if those disasters were happening right now. Evolution designed this system to keep you alive in a world of immediate physical dangers. It never got the memo about mortgages, deadlines, and social media.
