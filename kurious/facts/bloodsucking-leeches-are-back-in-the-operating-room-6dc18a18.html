Bloodsucking Leeches Are Back in the Operating Room

The same slimy parasites medieval doctors used to balance humors are now FDA-approved medical devices saving severed fingers and reattached ears.

In 2004, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration did something that would have delighted a 15th-century physician: it approved leeches as medical devices. Not a leech-derived drug or a leech-inspired tool, but the actual animals—Hirudo medicinalis, the European medicinal leech, cleared for sale alongside scalpels and sutures. The approval wasn't nostalgia. It was an acknowledgment that modern surgeons had discovered something their medieval predecessors never understood: leeches solve a very specific problem that no machine can.
