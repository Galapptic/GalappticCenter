This Material Is One Atom Thick Yet Stronger Than Steel

A sheet of carbon just one atom thick could support the weight of an elephant balanced on a pencil point without breaking.

Graphene is essentially a single layer of graphite—the stuff in your pencil lead—but its properties are almost magical. This honeycomb lattice of carbon atoms is just one atom thick, making it the thinnest material ever created. Despite this impossibly slim profile, graphene is about 200 times stronger than steel by weight. It's also remarkably transparent, absorbing only 2.3% of light that passes through it. You could stack three million sheets of graphene and still have a stack only one millimeter thick. Yet this gossamer-thin material conducts electricity better than copper and heat better than any known substance. Scientists call it a true two-dimensional material.
