Arrows Deliberately Bend Around Bows to Hit Their Targets

Every arrow shot from a traditional bow performs an invisible snake-like dance that physicists once thought should make accurate archery impossible.

Here's a puzzle that baffled scientists for decades: when you nock an arrow, it points slightly away from your target. The bowstring sits to one side of the bow's centerline. Logic says the arrow should fly off at an angle, crashing into the bow itself. Yet archers hit bullseyes with remarkable precision. The secret? Archer's paradox. When released, the arrow doesn't fly straight—it flexes dramatically, bending around the bow handle like a fish swimming through water. This oscillating wiggle allows the arrow to clear the bow entirely and then straighten out mid-flight, heading exactly where the archer intended. Your eyes just can't see it happening.
