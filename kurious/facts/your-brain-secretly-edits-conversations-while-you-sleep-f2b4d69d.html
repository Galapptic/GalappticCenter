Your Brain Secretly Edits Conversations While You Sleep

That argument you remember so clearly from last night has already been rewritten by your brain to make you sound smarter and more justified.

Think you remember exactly what you said in that heated discussion? You don't. Within hours of any conversation, your brain begins reconstructing the exchange, filling gaps, smoothing rough edges, and subtly adjusting who said what. This isn't lying—it's how memory fundamentally works. Your hippocampus doesn't record conversations like a voice memo. Instead, it stores fragments: emotional highlights, key phrases, general impressions. When you recall the conversation later, your brain reassembles these pieces into something that feels complete. But each reconstruction introduces small changes. You might remember making a clever point you actually thought of afterward, or recall the other person being more aggressive than they were. Your brain is less historian, more novelist.
