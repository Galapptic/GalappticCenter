Diamonds and Coal Share the Same Building Blocks

The glittering gemstone on an engagement ring and the black lump heating a barbecue are both made of pure carbon atoms.

Here's a mind-bending truth: diamonds and coal are both made entirely of carbon. The difference? Arrangement and pressure. Coal forms from ancient plant matter compressed over millions of years near Earth's surface. Diamonds, however, require extreme conditions found roughly 150 kilometers underground, where temperatures exceed 1,000°C and pressure reaches 50,000 times atmospheric levels. Under these brutal conditions, carbon atoms lock into a rigid three-dimensional lattice, creating the hardest natural material known. The popular saying that diamonds are 'pressurized coal' isn't technically accurate—most diamonds formed before plants even existed—but it captures the essential truth that extraordinary pressure transforms ordinary carbon into something spectacular.
