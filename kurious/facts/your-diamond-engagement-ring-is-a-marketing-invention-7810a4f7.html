Your Diamond Engagement Ring Is a Marketing Invention

Diamonds aren't rare at all—one company just convinced the world they were through the most successful advertising campaign in history.

That diamond on your finger? It's not particularly rare. De Beers, a company founded in 1888, once controlled roughly 90% of the world's diamond supply. Rather than flood the market and crash prices, they did something clever: they stockpiled diamonds and released them slowly, creating artificial scarcity. Then came the genius stroke. In 1938, De Beers hired advertising agency N.W. Ayer to convince Americans that diamonds equaled love. Before this campaign, diamond engagement rings weren't traditional at all. The company essentially invented the tradition, making a common carbon crystal into an emotional necessity. They didn't sell rocks—they sold romance itself.
