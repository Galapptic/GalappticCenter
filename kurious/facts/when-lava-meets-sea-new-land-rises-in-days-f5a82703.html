When Lava Meets Sea, New Land Rises in Days

In 1963, fishermen off Iceland watched the ocean start to boil—and within four years, a brand-new island stood 174 meters tall.

On November 14, 1963, the crew of a fishing boat named Ísleifur II noticed something strange southwest of Iceland. Dark smoke rose from the water. They radioed shore, thinking a ship was on fire. But there was no ship. The Atlantic Ocean itself was burning, or so it seemed. By the next morning, a ridge of black rock had broken the surface. Within a week, it stood 70 meters high. The Icelandic government named the newborn island Surtsey, after the Norse fire giant Surtr.
