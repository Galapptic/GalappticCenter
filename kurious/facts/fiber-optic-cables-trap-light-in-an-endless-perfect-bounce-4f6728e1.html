Fiber optic cables trap light in an endless perfect bounce

The internet travels through hair-thin glass threads where light bounces millions of times without ever escaping.

The cables carrying the internet across ocean floors are surprisingly simple at their core. They're made of glass threads thinner than a human hair, and they work by trapping light inside. When you shine light into one end of these fibers, it doesn't just travel straight through. Instead, it bounces off the walls again and again, zigzagging its way forward like a marble rattling down a tube. The remarkable part is that almost none of the light escapes. It stays trapped, bouncing perfectly, carrying your video calls and messages across continents at nearly the speed of light itself.
