Medieval Churches Used Glass as Comic Books for the Masses

Before literacy spread, cathedral windows served as vivid illustrated Bibles that even peasants could understand.

In medieval Europe, most people couldn't read. Books were rare, expensive, and handwritten in Latin that common folk didn't speak. So how did the Church teach biblical stories to farmers and craftspeople? They turned sunlight into storytelling. Stained glass windows transformed churches into illuminated picture books. Each panel depicted familiar scenes: Adam and Eve, Noah's Ark, the life of Christ. Congregations could follow narratives simply by looking up. The brilliant colors grabbed attention in an era without electric light or vivid imagery. A peasant who couldn't write their own name could still understand salvation, sacrifice, and redemption through these glowing visual narratives that came alive with each sunrise.
