Hagfish Weaponize Slime That Suffocates Attackers in Seconds

When a shark bites a hagfish, it's the shark that ends up fighting for its life as its gills become clogged with a gallon of expanding goo.

Hagfish look like swimming sausages, but they pack one of nature's most disgusting defenses. When threatened, these primitive fish release a small amount of mucus and thread-like proteins from glands along their body. Within milliseconds, this mixture explodes into a massive cloud of slime—up to five gallons from a single hagfish. The gel is so thick and sticky that it clogs the gills of any predator foolish enough to attack. Sharks, seals, and large fish have all been observed gagging and retreating after biting a hagfish. The predator literally can't breathe and must flee to clear its gills. Meanwhile, the hagfish ties itself into a knot and scrapes the slime off its own body, swimming away unharmed.
