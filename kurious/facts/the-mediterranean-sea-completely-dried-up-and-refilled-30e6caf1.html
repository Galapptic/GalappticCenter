The Mediterranean Sea completely dried up and refilled catastrophically

Six million years ago, the entire Mediterranean was a scorching salt desert two miles deep.

Around six million years ago, the Mediterranean Sea simply vanished. The entire basin, which today holds enough water to cover Texas 50 feet deep, became a giant desert of salt flats and baking canyons. This wasn't gradual decline. Geological evidence shows the Mediterranean evaporated almost completely, possibly multiple times, leaving behind salt deposits over a mile thick in places. Then it came roaring back. When the Atlantic finally broke through at Gibraltar, it created what may have been the largest waterfall Earth has ever seen, refilling the entire basin in anywhere from months to a few thousand years.
