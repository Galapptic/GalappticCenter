Every Olympic Curling Stone Comes from One Scottish Island

The world's entire supply of elite curling stones depends on a single volcanic island off Scotland's coast that produces rock found nowhere else on Earth.

Here's a wild sporting secret: virtually every competitive curling stone in the world comes from Ailsa Craig, a tiny uninhabited island in Scotland's Firth of Clyde. This 240-acre volcanic plug produces two types of exceptionally rare granite—Blue Hone and Ailsa Craig Common Green—that have unique properties essential for the sport. The island's rock is incredibly dense with almost zero water absorption, meaning it won't pit or crack when sliding across ice thousands of times. Kays of Scotland holds exclusive quarrying rights and harvests granite only every decade or so, stockpiling enough to supply the world. Each 42-pound stone costs around $600, and Olympic sets can exceed $10,000.
