Particles Can Pass Through Walls Without Breaking Them

Every second, particles inside your body are teleporting through barriers that should be completely impenetrable according to classical physics.

Imagine throwing a tennis ball at a brick wall and watching it appear on the other side—without leaving a hole. Absurd, right? Yet at the quantum scale, particles do exactly this constantly. Quantum tunneling occurs when particles like electrons pass through energy barriers they shouldn't have enough energy to overcome. It's not that they break through; they simply have a probability of existing on the other side. This isn't science fiction—it's how the Sun generates energy, how computer chips work, and why radioactive decay happens. The particle doesn't travel through the barrier in any classical sense. Instead, its wave-like nature means there's always a small chance it's already on the other side.
