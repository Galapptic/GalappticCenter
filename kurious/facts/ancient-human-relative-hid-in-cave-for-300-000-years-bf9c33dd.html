Ancient Human Relative Hid in Cave for 300,000 Years

A species with an orange-sized brain deliberately buried its dead in pitch-black caves so narrow that only the smallest researchers could squeeze through to find them.

In 2013, cavers exploring South Africa's Rising Star Cave system stumbled upon something extraordinary. Deep within a chamber accessible only through a crack just eighteen centimeters wide, they found bones—lots of them. What emerged was Homo naledi, a previously unknown human relative. Over 1,500 fossil fragments from at least fifteen individuals were recovered, making it one of the largest single discoveries of early human remains in Africa. The species had a strange mix of features: human-like feet and hands capable of tool use, but a brain barely larger than a chimpanzee's. How did these bones end up in such an inaccessible place? That mystery would spark fierce debate.
