The Poisonous Fruit That Built Caribbean Cuisine

A toxic West African fruit, stuffed into slave ships as cheap provisions, would accidentally become the national dish of Jamaica and reshape how an entire region eats.

The ackee fruit looks like a mistake. When ripe, its red shell cracks open to reveal three glossy black seeds cushioned by pale yellow flesh that resembles scrambled eggs. Eat it at the wrong moment—before the fruit opens naturally—and you'll consume hypoglycin, a toxin that causes what Jamaicans call "vomiting sickness" and what doctors call fatal hypoglycemia. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration banned fresh ackee imports for decades. Yet this dangerous, finicky fruit became Jamaica's national dish and one of the defining flavors of Caribbean cooking. The story of how it got there is the story of the slave trade itself.
