Zimbabwe Printed Money So Fast It Was Worthless Before the Ink Dried

At the peak of Zimbabwe's hyperinflation, prices doubled every 24 hours—meaning a loaf of bread cost twice as much by dinnertime as it had at breakfast.

In November 2008, Zimbabwe's annual inflation rate hit 89.7 sextillion percent—a number with 22 zeros. The country's central bank was printing hundred-trillion-dollar notes, yet workers who received their wages in the morning would sprint to spend them before lunch. By afternoon, the same stack of bills might not cover a bus fare home.
