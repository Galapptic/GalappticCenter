The Devil's Breath Drug Creates Zombie-Like Obedience

Criminals in Colombia blow a powder in victims' faces, turning them into compliant puppets who willingly empty their own bank accounts—then remember nothing.

Scopolamine, nicknamed 'Devil's Breath,' is extracted from borrachero trees native to South America. When administered—often blown as powder into a victim's face—it creates a terrifying state of chemical submission. Victims remain conscious and functional, able to walk, talk, and follow complex instructions, yet they become eerily compliant to any command. They've been documented withdrawing their own savings and handing it to criminals, inviting robbers into their homes, even assisting in their own kidnappings. The drug simultaneously blocks memory formation, so victims wake hours later with zero recollection of what happened. This combination of obedience and amnesia makes it a perfect tool for criminals seeking untraceable crimes.
