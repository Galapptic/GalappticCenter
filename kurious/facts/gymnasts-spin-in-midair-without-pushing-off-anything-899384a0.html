Gymnasts Spin in Midair Without Pushing Off Anything

Once airborne, gymnasts can start twisting and spinning using nothing but their own body movements, defying what seems physically possible.

Watch a gymnast launch off a vault and suddenly start twisting—it looks impossible. They're not pushing off air, and nothing's touching them. Yet they spin. The secret is conservation of angular momentum, a fundamental physics principle. When gymnasts leave the ground, they carry a certain amount of rotational energy. By changing their body shape—tucking arms in, extending legs, tilting their torso—they redistribute this energy. Pull your arms tight to your chest, and you spin faster. Extend them wide, and you slow down. It's the same physics that lets figure skaters accelerate during spins. Gymnasts become masters of manipulating their own rotating bodies through pure technique.
