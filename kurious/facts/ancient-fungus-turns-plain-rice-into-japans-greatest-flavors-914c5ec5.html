Ancient Fungus Turns Plain Rice Into Japan's Greatest Flavors

A fuzzy green mold that would make most people throw out their food is actually Japan's national fungus and the secret behind miso, sake, and soy sauce.

Koji is Aspergillus oryzae, a domesticated mold that Japanese craftspeople have cultivated for over a thousand years. When sprinkled onto steamed rice, barley, or soybeans, this fuzzy fungus gets to work producing powerful enzymes. These enzymes break down starches into sugars and proteins into amino acids, creating the building blocks for fermentation. Without koji, there would be no miso paste, no sake, no soy sauce, and no mirin. The Japanese government officially designated it the national fungus in 2006, recognizing its irreplaceable role in Japanese cuisine. Every time you taste that deep, savory complexity in Japanese food, you're tasting koji's handiwork.
