De Beers Invented the Idea That Diamonds Are Rare

The sparkly gem on engagement rings everywhere isn't actually scarce—one company just convinced the world it was for nearly a century.

Diamonds aren't rare. They're actually one of the most common gemstones on Earth. But for most of the 20th century, De Beers—a South African mining company—controlled roughly 80-90% of the world's diamond supply. Instead of flooding the market, they locked stones away in vaults, releasing only enough to maintain astronomical prices. They created artificial scarcity for a naturally abundant resource. The company also pioneered demand-side control, spending millions convincing consumers that diamonds were precious symbols of eternal love. Before De Beers' marketing campaigns, diamond engagement rings weren't even traditional. The company essentially manufactured both the supply shortage and the cultural desire simultaneously.
