Arabic Letters Shapeshift Depending on Where They Appear

Unlike English's static alphabet, Arabic letters transform into completely different shapes depending on whether they start, middle, or end a word.

The Arabic alphabet contains 28 letters, but here's the twist: most of them have up to four different forms. A letter looks one way when it starts a word, another way in the middle, yet another at the end, and still different when standing alone. Take the letter 'ayn'—its four forms barely resemble each other to untrained eyes. This isn't random artistic flair. Arabic is a cursive script where letters connect within words, flowing right to left like water. Each positional form evolved to make these connections smooth and beautiful. English letters occasionally do this too—think of how we might connect cursive letters—but Arabic made it fundamental to the writing system itself.
