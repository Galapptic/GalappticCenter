Your Brain Is Wired to Share Rage Before Reading

A single angry tweet travels six times faster than factual corrections, and your neurochemistry is exactly why platforms exploit this.

Here's an uncomfortable truth: when you see something infuriating online, you're more likely to share it immediately than pause to verify it. Studies from MIT found that false news stories reach people six times faster than accurate ones. Why? Anger triggers a fight-or-flight response that bypasses careful thinking. Social media algorithms noticed this pattern and leaned into it hard. Content that provokes strong negative emotions generates more engagement—more comments, shares, and time on platform. This creates a feedback loop where the most inflammatory content rises to the top, while nuanced truth struggles to compete. Your outrage isn't a bug; it's the business model.
