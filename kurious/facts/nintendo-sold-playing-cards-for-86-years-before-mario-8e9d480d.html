Nintendo Sold Playing Cards for 86 Years Before Mario Existed

The company behind the world's most famous video game characters spent nearly a century making hand-painted cards for Japanese gambling parlors.

On September 23, 1889, a craftsman named Fusajiro Yamauchi opened a small shop in Kyoto, Japan. He called it Nintendo Koppai, and his product was hanafuda—"flower cards" used in traditional Japanese gambling games. Each deck of 48 cards was hand-painted with seasonal flowers, and Yamauchi's were good enough to attract the attention of the yakuza, who ran the country's gambling parlors.
