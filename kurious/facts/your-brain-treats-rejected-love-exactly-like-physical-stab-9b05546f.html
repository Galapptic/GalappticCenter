Your brain treats rejected love exactly like physical stab wounds

The agony of rejection isn't metaphorical. Your brain processes social pain using the exact same neural circuits as physical pain.

When someone breaks your heart, you might say it physically hurts. Turns out you're not being dramatic. Brain imaging studies reveal that social rejection activates the same neural regions that light up when you stub your toe or burn your hand. The anterior cingulate cortex and the insula, both critical for processing physical pain, respond identically whether you're looking at a photo of your ex or touching a hot stove. Your brain literally cannot tell the difference between emotional wounds and physical ones. Evolution wired social pain into our most primitive alarm systems.
