Your brain invents a delay so your senses arrive synchronized

Everything you experience right now actually happened at different times, but your brain hides this from you.

Touch a table while looking at your hand. It feels simultaneous, doesn't it? But here's the thing: light travels faster than pressure signals through your nerves. Your visual system processes information faster than your touch system. By all rights, you should see your finger contact the table before you feel it. Yet somehow everything arrives perfectly synchronized in your conscious experience. Your brain is doing something remarkable behind the scenes. It's actively holding back the faster signals and waiting for the slower ones to catch up, creating an artificial delay so everything feels like it happens at once.
