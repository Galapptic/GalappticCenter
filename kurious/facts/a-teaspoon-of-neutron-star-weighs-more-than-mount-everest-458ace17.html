A teaspoon of neutron star weighs more than Mount Everest

Six billion tons crushed into a space smaller than your fingertip—that's the mind-breaking reality of the densest objects in the visible universe.

When massive stars die, they don't go quietly. After exhausting their nuclear fuel, stars between 10 and 25 times our Sun's mass collapse in spectacular supernova explosions. What remains is almost incomprehensible: a neutron star. These stellar corpses pack roughly 1.4 solar masses into a sphere just 20 kilometers across—about the size of a city. The density is staggering. A single teaspoon of neutron star material weighs around six billion tons. That's roughly the weight of every car, truck, and vehicle on Earth combined, squeezed into a space you could balance on your finger. Gravity has won so completely that atoms themselves have surrendered their structure.
