The Locked Room Where a Man Speaks Perfect Chinese Without Understanding a Word

In 1980, a philosopher who knew no Chinese proposed a thought experiment so unsettling that artificial intelligence researchers are still arguing about it four decades later.

John Searle was fed up. The Berkeley philosopher watched as artificial intelligence researchers in the late 1970s made bold claims about their programs: that computers could genuinely understand stories, that machines were beginning to think. To Searle, something was deeply wrong with this picture. In 1980, he published a three-page thought experiment in the journal Behavioral and Brain Sciences that detonated like a bomb in the AI community. He called it the Chinese Room.
