Ancient Roman Soldiers Got Paid in Salt

The word 'salary' literally comes from the Latin 'salarium,' meaning salt money, because Roman legionnaires received part of their wages in this precious mineral.

Before refrigeration existed, salt was the only reliable way to preserve meat, fish, and vegetables. This made it extraordinarily valuable—sometimes worth more than gold by weight in certain regions. Roman soldiers stationed across the empire needed salt not just for their food, but as a crucial survival resource. Their 'salarium' became so standard that we still use its descendant word today. When someone wasn't doing their job well, Romans would say they weren't 'worth their salt'—a phrase we still use two thousand years later. This simple white crystal literally shaped how we talk about earning a living.
