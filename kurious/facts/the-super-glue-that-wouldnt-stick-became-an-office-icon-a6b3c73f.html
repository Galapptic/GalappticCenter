The Super-Glue That Wouldn't Stick Became an Office Icon

Spencer Silver spent five years trying to find a use for his failed adhesive before a frustrated choir singer finally saw its potential.

In 1968, a chemist at 3M was trying to develop a super-strong adhesive for the aerospace industry. Instead, Spencer Silver created something bewilderingly useless: a glue that stuck to things but peeled right off, leaving no residue. It bonded weakly to surfaces but strongly to itself, forming tiny indestructible spheres. His bosses weren't impressed. What good was an adhesive that didn't permanently adhere?
