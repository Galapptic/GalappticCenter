Glowing Mushrooms Light Up Forest Floors at Night

Over 80 species of fungi produce their own ghostly green light, turning dark forests into scenes from a fairy tale.

Walk through certain forests at night and you might witness something magical—mushrooms that glow in the dark. These bioluminescent fungi emit an eerie greenish light from their caps, stems, or even underground networks. Found across tropical and temperate regions worldwide, from Brazil's Atlantic Forest to Japan's mountainous woods, these living lanterns have captivated humans for centuries. Ancient Greeks called them 'foxfire,' while soldiers in World War I reportedly used glowing wood infected with luminescent fungi to mark paths in trenches. The glow is continuous, requiring no trigger like some other bioluminescent organisms. On moonless nights, the effect transforms the forest floor into a constellation of tiny green stars scattered among the leaf litter.
