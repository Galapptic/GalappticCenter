Archerfish Outsmart Physics to Snipe Insects From Water

These small fish mentally calculate how light bends at the water's surface, then adjust their aim to nail targets they technically shouldn't be able to see accurately.

Archerfish have mastered one of nature's most impressive hunting techniques. From beneath the water's surface, they spot insects resting on overhanging branches, then fire precise jets of water to knock them down for dinner. But here's the mind-bending part: light bends when it passes between air and water, a phenomenon called refraction. This makes objects above the surface appear in different positions than where they actually are. It's the same effect that makes a straw look broken in a glass of water. Somehow, archerfish have evolved to automatically correct for this optical illusion, calculating exactly where their prey really is despite the visual trickery. Their accuracy rate? Up to 95 percent from distances of over a meter.
