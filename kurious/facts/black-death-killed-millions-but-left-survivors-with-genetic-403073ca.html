Black Death Killed Millions But Left Survivors With Genetic Superpowers

The deadliest pandemic in human history accidentally created populations with built-in resistance to certain diseases.

Between 1347 and 1351, the Black Death swept across Europe with terrifying speed, killing an estimated 75 to 200 million people—roughly half the continent's population. Entire villages vanished. Bodies piled in streets faster than they could be buried. The bacterium Yersinia pestis, spread primarily by flea-infested rats, caused agonizing deaths within days. But here's the twist: not everyone died. Some people exposed to plague simply survived, and scientists now know this wasn't just luck. These survivors carried genetic variants that helped their immune systems fight off the infection, and they passed these protective genes to their descendants.
