A Stone Slab Unlocked Three Thousand Years of Lost History

For over a millennium, the magnificent temples and tombs of ancient Egypt stood covered in mysterious symbols that no living person could read—until a chance military discovery changed everything.

In 1799, French soldiers rebuilding a fort near the Egyptian town of Rashid (Rosetta) stumbled upon a dark granite slab covered in inscriptions. What made this stone extraordinary wasn't its size or beauty—it was that the same decree appeared in three different scripts: ancient Egyptian hieroglyphics, Demotic script, and ancient Greek. Since scholars could still read Greek, they finally had a translation key. The stone dated to 196 BCE, created during Ptolemy V's reign, but its true power lay dormant for another two decades. After centuries of silence, hieroglyphics—those enigmatic pictures adorning pyramids and papyri—were about to speak again.
