Owls hunt silently because feathers comb turbulence into whispers

A mouse can hear a twig snap fifty feet away but never hears the owl diving straight toward it.

Owls are ghostly quiet when they fly. While a pigeon creates a whoosh you can hear from across a room, an owl gliding inches from your head makes almost no sound at all. This stealth is a hunting superpower. Mice, voles, and rabbits have excellent hearing that alerts them to danger, but owls have evolved flight so silent that prey never knows death is coming. The secret lies not in how owls fly differently, but in the extraordinary structure of their feathers. Each feather is an acoustic engineering marvel that transforms what should be noisy turbulence into near silence.
