The Dead Sea Drops a Meter Every Year and Cannot Drain

A lake with no exit is vanishing faster than any body of water on Earth, dropping over a meter annually while becoming ever saltier.

The Dead Sea sits at the lowest point on Earth's surface, roughly 430 meters below sea level. Unlike most lakes, it has no river flowing out—water enters from the Jordan River but has nowhere to go except up. Evaporation is the only escape route, and in the scorching Middle Eastern heat, it's relentless. Every year, the water level drops by more than a meter. Since the 1960s, the surface has fallen over 40 meters. The sea that ancient civilizations believed was eternal is quite literally shrinking before our eyes. What remains grows increasingly concentrated with minerals, pushing salinity levels to nearly ten times that of ordinary seawater.
