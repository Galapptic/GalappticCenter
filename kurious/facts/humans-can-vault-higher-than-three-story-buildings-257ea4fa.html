Humans Can Vault Higher Than Three-Story Buildings

Using nothing but a bendy stick and raw athleticism, pole vaulters launch themselves over six meters into the air—higher than a giraffe standing on another giraffe.

The current men's pole vault world record stands at 6.26 meters, set by Armand Duplantis in 2024. That's roughly the height of a three-story building—or about 20 feet for those counting. Women's records hover around 5.06 meters, held by Yelena Isinbayeva. What makes this feat mind-bending is the simplicity of the equipment: a flexible pole, a runway, and human determination. Vaulters sprint at nearly 10 meters per second, plant a four-meter pole into a box, and somehow convert that horizontal speed into vertical flight. The whole vault takes about one second from plant to peak, yet the biomechanics involved rival the complexity of any engineered system.
