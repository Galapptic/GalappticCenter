This Math Problem Takes Seconds to Explain But Decades to Solve

Pick any positive integer, apply two simple rules repeatedly, and mathematicians have spent 90 years trying to prove where you'll always end up.

Here's the Collatz Conjecture: Pick any positive whole number. If it's even, divide by two. If it's odd, multiply by three and add one. Repeat. The conjecture claims that no matter what number you start with, you'll eventually reach one. Try it with seven: 7 becomes 22, then 11, then 34, then 17, then 52, then 26, then 13, then 40, then 20, then 10, then 5, then 16, then 8, then 4, then 2, then 1. It works for every number ever tested—and computers have checked numbers up to roughly 2^68. Yet nobody can prove it always works. Mathematician Paul Erdős famously said mathematics is not yet ready for such problems.
