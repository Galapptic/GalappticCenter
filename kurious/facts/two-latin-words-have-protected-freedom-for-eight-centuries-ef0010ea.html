Two Latin Words Have Protected Freedom for Eight Centuries

A 13th-century legal phrase invented to challenge a king's power remains the single most important protection against wrongful imprisonment worldwide.

Habeas corpus literally means 'you have the body' in Latin. It's a legal command that forces authorities to bring a prisoner before a court and justify why they're being held. No explanation? The prisoner goes free. This simple mechanism prevents governments from making people disappear into dungeons without charges. Before habeas corpus, kings and officials could imprison anyone indefinitely without reason. The writ fundamentally shifts power: instead of citizens proving innocence, the state must prove it has legitimate grounds for detention. It's why you can't be thrown in jail and forgotten. Every democracy considers it essential, and suspending it is universally recognized as a warning sign of authoritarianism taking hold.
