Mars dust storms could zap astronauts with dangerous static electricity

Future explorers might get shocked by something far stranger than Earth's static cling on laundry day.

When dust storms sweep across Mars, they don't just obscure the sun and coat everything in red powder. They generate massive amounts of static electricity. Picture rubbing a balloon on your hair, then scale that up to a planetary weather system. Martian dust particles collide with each other during storms, transferring electrons and building up electric charges. The storms themselves become giant batteries. NASA scientists worry that future astronauts walking through these conditions could receive shocks powerful enough to damage equipment or even harm human bodies. The Red Planet has turned weather into a weapon.
