Why drying mud cracks into nearly perfect hexagons

The same mathematical rule that shapes honeycombs and soap bubbles also controls how dirt dries in your backyard.

Walk past a dried puddle and you'll notice something strange. The cracks don't just zigzag randomly. They meet at angles that look oddly deliberate, forming shapes that are almost always hexagons. This isn't coincidence. When wet mud dries, it's actually solving an ancient mathematical problem without knowing it. The same pattern appears in everything from cooling lava to old paint to the surface of distant planets. Nature keeps reaching for hexagons because they're the most efficient way to cover a flat surface when things pull apart. The cracks know something we're still figuring out.
