Ancient City Had No Doors Because Everyone Used the Roof

For over a thousand years, residents of one of humanity's oldest cities climbed ladders to enter their homes through holes in the ceiling.

Imagine a city where houses are packed so tightly together that you walk across your neighbors' rooftops to get anywhere. That was daily life in Çatalhöyük, a Neolithic settlement in central Turkey that thrived between 7500 and 5700 BCE. With no ground-level doors, windows, or streets, residents descended into their mud-brick homes via wooden ladders through openings in the roof. These roof holes doubled as chimneys, venting smoke from interior hearths. Up to 8,000 people lived in this honeycomb-like arrangement at its peak, making it one of the earliest known urban settlements. The rooftop served as the real living space—a communal highway where neighbors socialized, worked, and moved freely above the private dwellings below.
