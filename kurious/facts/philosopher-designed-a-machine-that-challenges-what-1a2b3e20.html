Philosopher Designed a Machine That Challenges What Happiness Means

A thought experiment from 1974 has convinced most people that feeling happy isn't the same as actually being happy.

Imagine a machine that could give you any experience you desire—falling in love, climbing Everest, winning the Nobel Prize. Neuroscientists program it perfectly, and your brain can't tell the difference from reality. You'd feel genuinely happy, accomplished, and loved. Here's the catch: once plugged in, you'd forget it was fake. You'd float in a tank somewhere, living an entire simulated life while your real body ages. Philosopher Robert Nozick posed this scenario in 1974, asking a simple question: would you plug in forever? Most people instinctively say no—revealing something profound about human values that goes beyond just wanting to feel good.
