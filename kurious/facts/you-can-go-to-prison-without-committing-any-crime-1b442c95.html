You Can Go to Prison Without Committing Any Crime

A judge's single word—"guilty"—can send you to jail indefinitely, without a jury, without charges, and without any law you actually broke.

In 2014, Brooklyn journalist H. Beatty Chadwick had just been released from prison. He had served fourteen years—longer than the average sentence for second-degree murder in the United States. His crime? There wasn't one. No robbery, no assault, no fraud conviction. Chadwick had simply refused to comply with a judge's order to transfer $2.5 million to his ex-wife during divorce proceedings. He claimed the money was gone. The judge didn't believe him. So Chadwick sat in a Pennsylvania cell for over a decade, held not for punishment, but for "coercion"—a legal tool called civil contempt of court.
