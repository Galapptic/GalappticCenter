Philosopher Invented a Machine That Makes You Question Everything

Most people say they'd reject a lifetime of perfect artificial happiness to experience imperfect reality—but can they explain why?

Imagine a machine that could give you any experience you desire. Want to write a symphony, climb Everest, or fall deeply in love? Just plug in, and your brain will believe it's all real. You'll feel genuine joy, accomplishment, and connection. The catch? You'd never know you're floating in a tank, living an illusion. Philosopher Robert Nozick proposed this scenario in 1974 to challenge utilitarianism—the idea that maximizing pleasure is life's ultimate goal. Surprisingly, most people instinctively refuse the machine. They want real achievements, authentic relationships, and actual experiences, even if these come with pain and disappointment. This gut reaction suggests happiness alone isn't everything we value.
