Archerfish spit water bullets to snipe insects out of mid-air

A fish that can't fly has mastered the art of shooting down airborne targets with deadly precision.

Archerfish lurk just beneath the surface of rivers and mangroves in Southeast Asia and Australia, eyeing the world above. When they spot an insect on an overhanging leaf or branch, they do something extraordinary. They shoot it down. The fish forms a tight groove with its tongue against the roof of its mouth, creating a barrel. Then it snaps its gill covers shut, forcing water through this channel at high speed. The result is a jet stream that can knock a beetle off a branch from nearly two meters away. The insect tumbles into the water, and dinner is served.
