Male Anglerfish Fuse to Females and Dissolve Into Nothing

In the crushing darkness of the deep ocean, some male fish permanently melt into their mates, losing their brains, hearts, and independence—becoming little more than living sperm sacs attached to a female's body.

The deep sea is so vast and so dark that finding a mate is nearly impossible. For some species of anglerfish, evolution solved this problem with a solution that sounds like body horror: when a tiny male finds a female, he bites into her flesh and never lets go. His mouth fuses to her skin. His blood vessels merge with hers. And then, piece by piece, he dissolves—losing his eyes, his fins, his brain, his heart—until all that remains is a pair of gonads hanging from her body, ready to fertilize her eggs on demand.
