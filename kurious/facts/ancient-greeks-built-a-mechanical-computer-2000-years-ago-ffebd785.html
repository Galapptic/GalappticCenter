Ancient Greeks Built a Mechanical Computer 2000 Years Ago

A corroded lump pulled from a shipwreck contained the most sophisticated technology humanity would create for another 1,400 years.

In 1901, sponge divers exploring a Roman-era shipwreck near the Greek island of Antikythera pulled up a calcified bronze lump. Inside were over 30 precision-cut gears, smaller than coins, meshed together in a mechanism that wouldn't look out of place in a Swiss watch. Dating to around 100 BCE, the Antikythera Mechanism could predict solar and lunar eclipses, track the positions of the five known planets, and even calculate the dates of the ancient Olympic Games. Users simply turned a hand crank, and the interlocking gears computed astronomical cycles with stunning accuracy. It's essentially an analog computer, built two millennia before the digital age.
