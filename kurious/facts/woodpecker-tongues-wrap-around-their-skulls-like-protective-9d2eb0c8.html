Woodpecker tongues wrap around their skulls like protective airbags

A woodpecker's tongue is so absurdly long it has to store the extra length inside its own skull.

Woodpeckers slam their heads into trees up to 20 times per second. That kind of impact would give us a serious concussion. But here's something even stranger than their reinforced skulls: their tongues are so long that they can't fit in their mouths. We're not talking a little too long. A woodpecker's tongue can be up to three times the length of its beak. So where does all that extra tongue go? It wraps around the back of the skull, between the bone and the skin, sometimes even extending around the eye socket before finally anchoring near the nostril.
