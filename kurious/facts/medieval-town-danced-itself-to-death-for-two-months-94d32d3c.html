Medieval Town Danced Itself to Death for Two Months

In 1518, a woman started dancing in the streets of Strasbourg and couldn't stop—within a month, 400 people had joined her, dancing until their hearts gave out.

It started with one woman. In July 1518, Frau Troffea stepped into a narrow street in Strasbourg and began to dance. She had no music, no partner, no apparent reason. She danced for hours. Then days. Within a week, thirty-four others had joined her, writhing and leaping uncontrollably. By August, the number swelled to around 400 people. City officials, baffled, initially encouraged more dancing—they built a stage and hired musicians, believing the afflicted needed to dance it out. This backfired spectacularly. People collapsed from exhaustion, strokes, and heart attacks. Some reportedly danced until they died. The plague lasted nearly two months before mysteriously fading away.
