The Library of Alexandria Burned Multiple Times Over 600 Years

History's greatest library didn't die in one dramatic fire—it suffered a slow, agonizing death spanning six centuries of repeated destruction.

Forget what you learned about a single catastrophic fire destroying the Library of Alexandria. The truth is messier and more tragic. Founded around 300 BCE under Ptolemy I, this ancient wonder housed hundreds of thousands of scrolls containing humanity's accumulated wisdom. But its destruction wasn't a single event—it was death by a thousand cuts. Julius Caesar accidentally burned part of it in 48 BCE during urban warfare. Later, Roman Emperor Aurelian's troops damaged it around 270 CE while suppressing a revolt. Christian mobs destroyed the nearby Serapeum in 391 CE. The final blow likely came during the Muslim conquest in 642 CE. Each generation lost more irreplaceable knowledge.
