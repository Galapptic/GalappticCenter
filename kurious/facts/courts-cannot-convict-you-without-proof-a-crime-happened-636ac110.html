Courts Cannot Convict You Without Proof a Crime Happened

Before proving who committed a crime, prosecutors must first prove that a crime actually occurred—a safeguard born from centuries of wrongful executions.

Corpus delicti—Latin for 'body of the crime'—is a fundamental legal principle requiring prosecutors to prove that a crime actually occurred before anyone can be convicted. This doesn't literally mean producing a body in murder cases, despite the common misconception. Instead, it demands evidence showing two things: that harm or loss happened, and that criminal conduct caused it. For example, in an arson case, prosecutors must prove a fire occurred and that someone deliberately set it. A confession alone isn't enough. This protection exists because throughout history, innocent people confessed to crimes that never happened—due to coercion, mental illness, or false memory. Corpus delicti stands as a crucial barrier against wrongful conviction.
