Rivers of molten sulfur flow beneath the Earth's crust

Deep underground, there are rivers that don't flow with water at all.

Forget water. In certain parts of the world, actual rivers of liquid sulfur wind through cracks and cavities deep beneath your feet. These molten streams flow at temperatures around 240°F, hot enough to scald but cool enough to remain liquid. Scientists discovered evidence of these underground sulfur rivers while studying volcanic regions and analyzing rock samples. The sulfur isn't just sitting there in pools. It genuinely flows like a subterranean river system, carving channels through rock and carrying dissolved minerals along for the ride. It's an entirely parallel water cycle happening in the darkness below, except the star of the show is bright yellow sulfur instead of clear water.
