The Dead Sea Keeps Dropping to Record Lows

Earth's lowest land point is sinking by over a meter every year, creating sinkholes that swallow roads and buildings.

The Dead Sea's surface sits at roughly 430 meters below sea level, making it the lowest exposed land point on Earth. But here's the thing—it's not staying put. Every year, the water level drops by more than a meter, setting new records for how low land can go. This hypersaline lake, nestled between Israel, Jordan, and the West Bank, has lost about a third of its surface area since the 1960s. The dramatic shrinkage results from human water diversion and natural evaporation in one of the hottest places on the planet. What was once a vast ancient sea is becoming increasingly concentrated and compact.
