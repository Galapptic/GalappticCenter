The Oldest Unsolved Problem That Looks Ridiculously Simple

Every even number greater than 2 can be written as the sum of two primes—a statement so simple a child can test it, yet so stubborn that nearly 300 years of mathematics has failed to prove it.

Pick any even number. Try 8. That's 3 + 5. Try 20. That's 7 + 13. Try 100. That's 47 + 53. Try 4,000,000,000,000,000,000. Computers have checked: it works. In fact, every even number anyone has ever tested breaks down into two primes. The pattern holds without a single exception across trillions upon trillions of cases. And yet mathematicians cannot prove it will always work.

This is the Goldbach Conjecture, and it has been tormenting number theorists since 1742. It sits in a peculiar category of mathematical problems: statements that are almost certainly true, supported by overwhelming computational evidence, but which lack the ironclad logical proof that mathematics demands. In a discipline where certainty is everything, Goldbach's simple claim remains maddeningly out of reach.
