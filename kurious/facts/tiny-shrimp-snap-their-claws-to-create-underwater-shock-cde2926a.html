Tiny Shrimp Snap Their Claws to Create Underwater Shock Waves

A creature smaller than your thumb generates a bubble so powerful it briefly reaches the temperature of the sun's surface.

Pistol shrimp possess one of nature's most extraordinary weapons—an oversized snapping claw that creates underwater shock waves powerful enough to stun or kill prey instantly. These small crustaceans, typically just a few centimeters long, snap their specialized claw shut so rapidly that it creates a cavitation bubble. When this bubble collapses, it produces a shock wave traveling at speeds up to 100 kilometers per hour, accompanied by a sharp crack reaching 218 decibels—louder than an actual gunshot. The snap happens in less than a millisecond, making it one of the fastest movements in the animal kingdom. Small fish, crabs, and worms caught in the blast zone are immediately stunned or killed, making the pistol shrimp a remarkably efficient predator despite its tiny size.
