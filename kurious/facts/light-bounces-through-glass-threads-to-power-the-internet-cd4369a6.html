Light Bounces Through Glass Threads to Power the Internet

Every time you stream a video, photons are zigzagging through hair-thin glass tubes at 70% the speed of light, bouncing millions of times per second without escaping.

Fiber optic cables are essentially light prisons. Each strand, thinner than a human hair, traps photons using a clever physics trick called total internal reflection. When light hits the boundary between the glass core and its outer cladding at a shallow angle, it bounces back entirely instead of escaping. The light zigzags down the fiber, sometimes traveling thousands of kilometers while bouncing billions of times. Despite moving at 70% light speed—slower than in vacuum because glass is denser—signals cross the Atlantic Ocean in about 40 milliseconds. This trapped light carries your emails, video calls, and streaming content as pulses representing ones and zeros.
