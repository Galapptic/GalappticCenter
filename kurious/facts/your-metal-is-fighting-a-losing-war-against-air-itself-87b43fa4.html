Your Metal Is Fighting a Losing War Against Air Itself

Every piece of iron on Earth is slowly being devoured by the very air we breathe, atom by atom, in a relentless chemical siege that never stops.

That orange-brown crust spreading across your car, bicycle, or garden tools isn't just ugly—it's evidence of a molecular war. Rust forms when iron atoms meet oxygen and water, triggering oxidation. During this reaction, iron surrenders electrons to oxygen, transforming solid metal into iron oxide—a crumbly, flaky substance we call rust. Unlike protective coatings on some metals, rust doesn't shield the underlying iron. Instead, it's porous and keeps absorbing moisture, allowing the attack to penetrate deeper. This is why a tiny scratch in paint can eventually consume an entire car panel. The process is relentless, occurring anywhere iron meets air and moisture, silently eating through bridges, ships, and buildings worldwide.
