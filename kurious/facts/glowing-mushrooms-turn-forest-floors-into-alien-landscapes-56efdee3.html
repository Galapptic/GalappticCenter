Glowing Mushrooms Turn Forest Floors Into Alien Landscapes

Over 80 species of fungi produce their own ghostly green light, creating natural nightlights that have mystified humans for millennia.

Deep in forests around the world, certain mushrooms do something extraordinary—they glow in the dark. These bioluminescent fungi emit an eerie green light that's visible to the naked eye on moonless nights, transforming forest floors into something resembling an alien planet. The glow comes from a chemical reaction involving a compound called luciferin, similar to what makes fireflies flash. Ancient Greeks called these mysterious lights 'foxfire,' and Victorian-era miners sometimes used glowing wood infected with luminescent fungi to illuminate tunnels without risking explosive sparks. Today, scientists have identified over 80 species of glowing mushrooms scattered across tropical and temperate forests, from Brazil to Japan.
