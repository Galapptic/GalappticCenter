The Silk Road had postal stations every 25 miles across 5,000 miles

Long before the internet, the Mongols created a communication network that could transmit messages faster than medieval armies could march.

The Mongol Empire didn't just conquer the largest land empire in history. They wired it together. Starting in the 1200s, they built a system called the Yam, a network of postal relay stations stretching from Beijing to the Black Sea. Every 25 miles, riders could swap exhausted horses for fresh ones. The system covered over 5,000 miles of routes crisscrossing Asia. Imagine riding your bicycle across your city, then multiplying that distance two hundred times, and placing a rest stop at every interval. That's the scale of what the Mongols achieved with medieval technology.
