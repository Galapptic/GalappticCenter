How Fire Became Motion and Built the Modern World

Before the steam engine, the only way to get more power was to find more muscles—human, horse, or ox—but a Scottish instrument repairman changed everything.

In 1763, James Watt was handed a broken model of a Newcomen engine to fix. The device, invented fifty years earlier to pump water from coal mines, was woefully inefficient—it wasted about 99% of the coal's energy. Watt didn't just repair it. He became obsessed with why it worked so badly, and his tinkering would launch the Industrial Revolution.
