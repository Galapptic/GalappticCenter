Ancient Greeks built a computer from bronze gears 2,000 years ago

The world's oldest known computer lay hidden in a shipwreck, predicting the cosmos with stunning precision.

In 1901, divers exploring a Roman shipwreck off the Greek island of Antikythera found a corroded lump of bronze. When researchers cleaned it up, they discovered something that shouldn't exist. Inside were dozens of precision gears, interlocking like a mechanical Swiss watch. The device was over 2,000 years old. Nothing remotely this sophisticated would appear again until medieval cathedral clocks, more than a millennium later. The Greeks had built an analog computer to model the heavens, and then the knowledge vanished. For decades, scientists couldn't believe what they were seeing.
