A Messy Lab Accident Gave Us the First Antibiotic

Alexander Fleming's most important discovery happened because he left dirty petri dishes sitting out for weeks while on vacation.

In September 1928, Scottish bacteriologist Alexander Fleming returned to his laboratory at St Mary's Hospital in London after a month-long holiday. He'd left the place a mess. Stacks of petri dishes containing Staphylococcus bacteria sat unwashed by the window—a clear violation of standard lab hygiene.

As Fleming sorted through the dishes to clean them, he noticed something strange. A blue-green mold had contaminated one plate, and around it, the deadly staph bacteria had dissolved. Instead of tossing it out, Fleming investigated.
