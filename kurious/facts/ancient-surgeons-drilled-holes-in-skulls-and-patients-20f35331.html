Ancient Surgeons Drilled Holes in Skulls and Patients Survived

Thousands of years before anesthesia or antibiotics, Stone Age doctors performed brain surgery—and the bone regrowth on ancient skulls proves their patients often lived.

In 1865, an American diplomat named Ephraim George Squier brought a curious artifact back from Peru: a human skull with a perfectly square hole cut into it. Scientists examined the bone and noticed something remarkable. The edges had healed. Whoever underwent this procedure had survived it—and lived for weeks or months afterward. The skull was roughly 400 years old, but the practice it revealed was far more ancient.
