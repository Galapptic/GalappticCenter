Ancient Frozen Forests in Permafrost Can Spontaneously Catch Fire

Trees that died millions of years ago are now thawing out and burning, releasing carbon that predates human civilization.

Beneath the Arctic surface lies permafrost—ground that has stayed frozen for thousands to millions of years. But it's not just ice and dirt down there. Permafrost contains entire ancient ecosystems: frozen forests, plant matter, and organic material that never fully decomposed. When climate change thaws this frozen ground, something remarkable happens. The preserved organic material, still rich in carbon, becomes exposed to oxygen and can actually ignite. These aren't ordinary forest fires—they're burning fuel that's been locked away since before humans existed. Scientists have discovered that thawing permafrost releases not just greenhouse gases but can fuel underground fires that smolder for months, even through winter.
