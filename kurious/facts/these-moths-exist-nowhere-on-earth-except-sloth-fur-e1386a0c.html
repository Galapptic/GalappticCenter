These Moths Exist Nowhere on Earth Except Sloth Fur

A tiny moth species has evolved to live exclusively in the coarse, algae-covered hair of three-toed sloths, completing its entire life cycle on a single animal.

Three-toed sloths aren't just slow-moving tree dwellers—they're mobile habitats. Living deep within their coarse, grooved fur are sloth moths, species that exist nowhere else on the planet. These small, cryptic insects spend their entire adult lives nestled in sloth hair, feeding on algae and skin secretions. A single sloth can host over 100 moths at once. The relationship is so specialized that different sloth species have their own moth species. When researchers examine sloth fur under magnification, they discover a bustling micro-world: moths, beetles, algae, and fungi all thriving in this unlikely ecosystem. The sloth carries an entire community wherever it climbs.
