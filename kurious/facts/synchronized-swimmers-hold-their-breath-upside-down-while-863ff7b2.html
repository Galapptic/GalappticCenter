Synchronized Swimmers Hold Their Breath Upside Down While Sprinting

The popular claim that synchronized swimmers hold their breath for eight minutes is a myth. The real number is far shorter, but they hold it while working as hard as middle-distance runners, upside down, and smiling.

A popular claim says synchronized swimmers hold their breath for eight minutes. They don't. A whole routine lasts only about three to four minutes. Individual underwater sequences typically run 30 seconds to about a minute. What makes those seconds brutal is everything happening during them. The swimmers are inverted, lifting teammates and kicking hard enough to raise their legs high out of a pool at least 10 feet (3 m) deep. They are never allowed to touch the bottom.
