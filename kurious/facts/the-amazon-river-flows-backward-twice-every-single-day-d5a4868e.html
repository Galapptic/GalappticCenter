The Amazon River flows backward twice every single day

The ocean is so powerful it literally shoves the Amazon River into reverse for hours at a time.

The Amazon dumps more water into the Atlantic than any other river on Earth. But twice a day, the ocean pushes back. When the tide comes in, seawater surges up the river mouth with such force that it overwhelms the downstream flow. The river stops. Then it reverses. Water that was heading to the ocean moments ago is now flowing inland, sometimes for hundreds of kilometers. This tidal pulse travels up tributaries and floods forests that were dry hours before. The mightiest river on the planet surrenders to the rhythm of the moon.
