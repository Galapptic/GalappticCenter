Ancient Bloodsuckers Now Work in Modern Operating Rooms

The FDA officially classified leeches as medical devices in 2004, making them the first living creatures to earn that designation.

Those slimy creatures your great-grandparents feared are now surgical superstars. When surgeons reattach severed fingers, ears, or other body parts, they face a tricky problem: arteries reconnect relatively easily, but tiny veins are nearly impossible to suture. Blood flows in but can't flow out, causing dangerous congestion that kills the reattached tissue. Enter the medicinal leech. These creatures attach to the surgical site and drain excess blood while their saliva delivers natural anticoagulants. This keeps blood moving until the body regrows its own venous connections, usually within five days. Hospitals now keep leech pharmacies stocked for exactly these emergencies.
