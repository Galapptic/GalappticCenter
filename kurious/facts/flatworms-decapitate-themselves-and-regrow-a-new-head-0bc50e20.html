Flatworms Decapitate Themselves and Regrow a New Head

Some flatworms deliberately sever their own heads from their bodies—and both pieces survive to become complete, independent animals.

Slice a planarian flatworm in half, and you won't kill it. You'll make two of them. These soft-bodied creatures, common in freshwater ponds and streams, possess regenerative abilities so extreme that a fragment just 1/279th of the original animal can regrow into a fully functional worm. But here's what's stranger: sometimes they do this to themselves on purpose.
