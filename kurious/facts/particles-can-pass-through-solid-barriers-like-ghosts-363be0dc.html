Particles Can Pass Through Solid Barriers Like Ghosts

Electrons routinely appear on the wrong side of walls they lack the energy to climb over, violating everything classical physics says should happen.

Imagine throwing a ball at a wall and watching it appear on the other side—not breaking through, just materializing there. That's quantum tunneling. Electrons and other particles can pass through barriers they absolutely shouldn't have enough energy to cross. In classical physics, if you don't have enough energy to climb a hill, you're stuck. But quantum mechanics plays by different rules. Particles exist as probability waves, not tiny billiard balls. When that wave encounters a barrier, most of it bounces back, but a small portion leaks through to the other side. If the barrier is thin enough, the particle has a real chance of appearing beyond it. No breaking, no climbing—just quantum weirdness.
