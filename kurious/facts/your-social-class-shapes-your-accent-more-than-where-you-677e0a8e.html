Your Social Class Shapes Your Accent More Than Where You Live

A linguist can guess your family's income from how you pronounce a single vowel, often more accurately than pinpointing your hometown.

Here's something uncomfortable: the way you speak reveals your social class almost instantly. Linguists have found that socioeconomic background shapes pronunciation, vocabulary, and grammar more powerfully than geography does. Two people from the same city but different class backgrounds often sound more different from each other than two upper-middle-class speakers from opposite ends of a country. This phenomenon exists across languages and cultures. In England, the distinction between working-class and upper-class accents within London is far greater than regional differences among elites nationwide. Your accent isn't just about where you grew up—it's a sonic fingerprint of your family's education, occupation, and social standing.
