A Drifter's Handwritten Letter Changed American Justice Forever

Clarence Earl Gideon couldn't afford a lawyer, so he taught himself law in prison and won a Supreme Court case that transformed the rights of every American accused of a crime.

On June 3, 1961, someone broke into the Bay Harbor Pool Room in Panama City, Florida. They smashed a cigarette machine, broke into a jukebox, and stole about five dollars in coins along with some beer and wine. It was a small-time burglary in a small Florida town. But what happened next would reshape the American legal system and enshrine a right that most people today assume has always existed.

A witness claimed he saw Clarence Earl Gideon leaving the pool hall that morning with a bottle of wine and his pockets full of coins. Gideon, a 51-year-old drifter with an eighth-grade education and a string of petty crimes behind him, was arrested and charged with breaking and entering with intent to commit petty larceny—a felony under Florida law.
