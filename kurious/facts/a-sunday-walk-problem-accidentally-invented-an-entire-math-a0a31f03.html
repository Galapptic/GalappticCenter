A Sunday Walk Problem Accidentally Invented an Entire Math Field

A simple question about crossing seven bridges without repeating any sparked a mathematical revolution that now powers everything from GPS to social networks.

In 1736, the Prussian city of Königsberg had a peculiar geography. The Pregel River flowed through it, creating two islands connected to the mainland and each other by exactly seven bridges. Citizens puzzled over a seemingly simple question: could you walk through the city crossing each bridge exactly once, without backtracking? Locals tried countless routes but always failed. The problem reached mathematician Leonhard Euler, who proved something remarkable—it wasn't just difficult, it was mathematically impossible. His proof didn't require testing every path. Instead, he showed the arrangement itself made success impossible, creating an entirely new way of thinking about space and connection.
