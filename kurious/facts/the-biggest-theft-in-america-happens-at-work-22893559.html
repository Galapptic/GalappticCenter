The Biggest Theft in America Happens at Work

Every year, American employers steal more from their workers' paychecks than all the nation's burglars, muggers, and car thieves steal from everyone combined.

In 2012, all the robberies, burglaries, larcenies, and motor vehicle thefts in the United States added up to about $14 billion in losses. That same year, a study by the Economic Policy Institute found that wage theft—employers illegally underpaying workers—cost American workers between $40 billion and $60 billion. The crime that affects the most people, costs the most money, and happens the most often barely registers as a crime at all.

Wage theft isn't one thing. It's a category that includes dozens of ways employers can shortchange workers: paying less than minimum wage, refusing overtime, forcing off-the-clock work, stealing tips, misclassifying employees as independent contractors, or simply not paying at all. Unlike a mugging, it rarely happens all at once. It accumulates in missing minutes, shaved hours, and paychecks that don't quite add up.
