Magna Carta Forced Kings to Follow Their Own Laws First Time

A group of rebellious barons cornered an unpopular king in a meadow and accidentally invented the foundation of modern democracy.

In June 1215, England's King John faced a crisis. His barons were furious over heavy taxes, arbitrary imprisonment, and royal abuses. They marched on London and forced the king to meet them at Runnymede, a meadow by the Thames. There, John pressed his seal onto a revolutionary document: Magna Carta. For the first time in European history, a monarch formally agreed that even he wasn't above the law. The charter declared that no free man could be imprisoned or punished except through lawful judgment. It established that the king couldn't levy taxes without baronial consent. Though John immediately tried to annul it, the principle had been planted: rulers must follow rules too.
