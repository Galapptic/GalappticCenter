Fold paper 103 times and it exceeds the observable universe

A sheet of paper folded just over a hundred times would stretch beyond the edge of everything we can see in the cosmos.

Start with a regular sheet of paper, about 0.1 millimeters thick. Fold it once, and you have 0.2 millimeters. Fold again: 0.4 millimeters. Seems harmless, right? But here's where math gets wild. Each fold doubles the previous thickness, and doubling is deceptively explosive. By fold 10, your paper is thicker than your hand. By fold 20, it's taller than a building. By fold 42, it reaches the moon. And at fold 103? You've created something approximately 93 billion light-years thick—the diameter of the observable universe. The math is simple: 2^103 × 0.1 millimeters. The result is incomprehensible.
