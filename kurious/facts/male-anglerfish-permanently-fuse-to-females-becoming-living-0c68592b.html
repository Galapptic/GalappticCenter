Male Anglerfish Permanently Fuse to Females Becoming Living Sperm Banks

In the darkest ocean depths, male anglerfish bite into females and literally dissolve into their bodies, losing their eyes, organs, and independence forever.

Deep in the pitch-black ocean, finding a mate is nearly impossible. So male anglerfish evolved a disturbing solution: when a tiny male encounters a female up to sixty times his size, he bites into her flesh and never lets go. His mouth fuses with her skin, their blood vessels merge, and his body begins to atrophy. Eyes? Gone. Digestive system? Unnecessary. Brain? Mostly dissolved. The male becomes a permanent parasitic attachment, essentially a pair of testes dangling from her body. When the female is ready to spawn, she has a built-in sperm supply ready to go. Some females carry multiple fused males, collecting partners like living trophies.
