Pulsars Spin Hundreds of Times Per Second Without Flying Apart

Some dead stars rotate 716 times every second—faster than a kitchen blender—yet their incredible gravity keeps them perfectly intact.

Imagine a star more massive than our Sun, crushed into a ball just 20 kilometers wide, spinning hundreds of times every single second. That's a pulsar—and by all intuition, it should tear itself apart. These ultra-dense neutron stars are the collapsed cores of massive stars that exploded as supernovae. When they spin, they emit beams of radiation from their magnetic poles, sweeping across space like cosmic lighthouses. The fastest known pulsar, PSR J1748-2446ad, rotates 716 times per second. That means its surface moves at about 24% the speed of light. Yet it holds together perfectly, defying what seems physically possible.
