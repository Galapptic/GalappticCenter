Dead Stars Spin Hundreds of Times Per Second Firing Cosmic Lighthouses

When massive stars die, their crushed cores can spin up to 716 times per second—so fast that their surfaces move at a quarter the speed of light.

When a massive star explodes as a supernova, its core doesn't simply vanish. Instead, it collapses into something extraordinary: a neutron star. Pack the mass of our Sun into a sphere just 20 kilometers across, and you get matter so dense that a teaspoon weighs billions of tons. But here's where it gets wild. As the star collapses, it spins faster—like a figure skater pulling in their arms. Combined with incredibly powerful magnetic fields, these spinning neutron stars generate focused beams of gamma rays and other radiation that sweep across space like lighthouse beams. When these beams happen to cross Earth, we detect regular pulses and call them pulsars.
