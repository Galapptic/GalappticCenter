Your fridge door traps invisible magnetic fields inside flexible rubber

Every time you close your refrigerator, you're bending magnetic fields into a flexible prison made of rubber.

The soft rubbery strip around your refrigerator door isn't just a seal. It's packed with millions of tiny magnetic particles, frozen in place inside flexible rubber. This is a ferrite magnet, and it performs a quiet miracle every day. Unlike the hard magnets stuck to your fridge's surface, this one bends and twists with the door. It clings to the metal frame with just enough force to keep cold air in but weak enough that you can pull it open without a struggle. The magnetism is real, but it's been tamed and made flexible.
