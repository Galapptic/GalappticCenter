Figure Skaters Spin Faster by Breaking No Laws of Physics

When a spinning skater pulls their arms in, they can triple their rotation speed without pushing off anything—a perfect demonstration of one of nature's most fundamental conservation laws.

Watch any figure skater perform a spin, and you'll notice something remarkable. They start rotating with arms extended wide, then pull them tight against their body—and suddenly they're spinning dramatically faster. No one pushed them. No motor kicked in. Yet they accelerate like magic. This is angular momentum conservation in action. Angular momentum depends on both how fast you're spinning and how far your mass is from your rotation axis. When skaters pull their arms inward, they reduce their moment of inertia—essentially making themselves 'smaller' around the spin axis. Since angular momentum must stay constant without external torque, their rotation speed automatically increases to compensate.
