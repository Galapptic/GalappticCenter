Ancient Humans Drilled Holes in Skulls and Patients Lived

Thousands of ancient skulls show deliberate surgical holes with clear evidence of bone healing, proving patients survived these brain-adjacent procedures for years afterward.

Trepanation is exactly what it sounds like: drilling, scraping, or cutting holes into living human skulls. And here's the shocking part—archaeological evidence proves people survived it. We've found thousands of trepanned skulls across every inhabited continent, some dating back 10,000 years. The proof of survival? Bone regrowth around the holes. When someone dies during or shortly after such trauma, the bone edges remain sharp. But many ancient skulls show smooth, healed edges, indicating the patient lived months or even decades afterward. Some skulls have multiple healed holes, suggesting repeat procedures. Without anesthesia, antibiotics, or sterile environments, our ancestors performed what we'd consider neurosurgery today.
