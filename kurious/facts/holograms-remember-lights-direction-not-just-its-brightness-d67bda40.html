Holograms remember light's direction, not just its brightness

A photograph captures what light looks like, but a hologram captures what light actually does.

When you take a photograph, you're capturing how bright each point of light is. The camera records intensity, turning a three-dimensional scene into a flat image. But light carries more information than just brightness. It also has phase, which describes where each light wave is in its cycle of oscillation. Think of it like freezing an ocean: a photo captures the height of each wave, but a hologram captures both the height and the exact position of every ripple. This hidden information is what makes holograms genuinely three-dimensional, not just pictures that look 3D.
