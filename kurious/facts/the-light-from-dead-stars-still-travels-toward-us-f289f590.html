The Light From Dead Stars Still Travels Toward Us

Some of the stars you see tonight exploded before Earth existed, yet their light is only now arriving.

On any clear night, you can look up and see stars that no longer exist. They burned through their fuel, collapsed, and detonated in explosions bright enough to outshine entire galaxies. But light travels at a finite speed—299,792 kilometers per second—and space is unimaginably vast. The photons that hit your retina tonight may have begun their journey when dinosaurs roamed Earth, or before the planet itself formed.

This isn't poetic metaphor. It's physics. And it means that every telescope pointed at the sky is also a time machine, capturing moments from a universe that has long since changed.
