The Letter Z Vanished From Latin for 200 Years

Around 300 BCE, Roman officials kicked Z out of the alphabet—then sheepishly invited it back centuries later when they realized they needed it.

The Latin alphabet had 21 letters when Rome was still a young republic. One of them was Z, sitting right there in the sixth position, borrowed from the Greeks along with most of the other letters. Then something strange happened. Sometime around 300 BCE, the Romans simply dropped it. For roughly two centuries, Z didn't exist in Latin. When it finally returned, it had to take the last seat in the alphabet—the linguistic equivalent of arriving late to a dinner party and finding your chair given away.
