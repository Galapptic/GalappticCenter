A Stone Slab Cracked a Code Silent for 1400 Years

For over a millennium, ancient Egypt's written wisdom was completely unreadable—until Napoleon's soldiers accidentally dug up the key.

In 1799, French soldiers rebuilding a fort near the Egyptian town of Rashid (Rosetta) unearthed a dark granite slab covered in writing. What made this stone extraordinary was that it contained the same decree written in three scripts: ancient Greek, Demotic (everyday Egyptian), and hieroglyphics. Since scholars could read Greek, they finally had a translation key. The stone was a royal decree from 196 BCE, praising King Ptolemy V. For centuries, hieroglyphics had been a complete mystery—people thought they were mystical symbols or primitive pictures. This accidental find would eventually prove that hieroglyphics represented an actual spoken language with grammar and phonetics.
