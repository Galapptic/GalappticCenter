Tube worms farm bacteria in their blood instead of eating

Deep sea worms have no mouth, no stomach, and survive by hosting trillions of bacteria inside their bodies.

At hydrothermal vents on the ocean floor, water shoots out at temperatures hot enough to melt lead. The water is thick with toxic chemicals and so acidic it could dissolve metal. Yet giant tube worms thrive here in massive colonies, some growing over eight feet long. They have no mouth and no digestive system at all. Instead, they survive on a remarkable partnership. Inside specialized organs that fill most of their body cavity, they house trillions of bacteria. These microbes are their food source, their farm, and their only way to extract energy from one of Earth's most hostile environments.
