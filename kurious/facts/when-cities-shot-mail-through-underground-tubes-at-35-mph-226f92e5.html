When Cities Shot Mail Through Underground Tubes at 35 MPH

Before email, before fax machines, Victorian engineers built pressurized networks that rocketed letters beneath city streets in metal canisters.

In 1853, London became the first city to move mail by air pressure. Engineers laid cast-iron tubes beneath the streets and used steam-powered pumps to create vacuums that sucked cylindrical carriers through the pipes. A letter could cross the city in minutes rather than hours. By the 1890s, pneumatic tube networks hummed beneath Berlin, Paris, Vienna, Philadelphia, and New York.
