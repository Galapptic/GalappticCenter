Australia Once Declared War on Emus and Lost Spectacularly

In 1932, Australian soldiers armed with machine guns faced 20,000 flightless birds and suffered a humiliating defeat.

After World War One, Australian veterans were given farmland in Western Australia to cultivate wheat. Then the Great Depression hit, wheat prices collapsed, and roughly 20,000 emus migrated inland after their breeding season. These massive flightless birds trampled crops and destroyed fences with gleeful abandon. Desperate farmers lobbied the government for help. The military's solution? Send in soldiers with Lewis machine guns. What followed was one of history's most bizarre military operations, where trained gunners struggled to hit fast-moving birds across open terrain. The emus, it turned out, were tactical geniuses.
