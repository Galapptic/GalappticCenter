One Criminal's Arrest Created Rights for Every American

A kidnapper's confession was thrown out by the Supreme Court, and suddenly every cop in America had to memorize a script.

You have the right to remain silent. Anything you say can and will be used against you. These words exist because of Ernesto Miranda, a Phoenix laborer arrested in 1963 for kidnapping and assault. During two hours of police questioning, Miranda confessed—but nobody told him he could stay quiet or have a lawyer present. His written confession helped convict him. But Miranda's attorney appealed, arguing the confession was coerced. The case climbed to the Supreme Court, which ruled 5-4 in 1966 that police must inform suspects of their constitutional rights before questioning. Today, this warning is standard procedure in every arrest across America.
