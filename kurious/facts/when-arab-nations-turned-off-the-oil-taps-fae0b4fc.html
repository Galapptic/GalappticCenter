When Arab Nations Turned Off the Oil Taps

In October 1973, a handful of oil ministers in Kuwait City made a decision that would leave Americans shivering in fuel lines and reshape global power forever.

On October 17, 1973, representatives from eleven Arab oil-producing nations gathered in Kuwait and agreed to something unprecedented: they would slash oil production by 5% each month and completely cut off shipments to the United States and the Netherlands. The weapon wasn't missiles or troops. It was crude oil—and it worked with devastating efficiency.
