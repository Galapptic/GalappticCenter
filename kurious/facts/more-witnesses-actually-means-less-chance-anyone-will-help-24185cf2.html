More Witnesses Actually Means Less Chance Anyone Will Help

In 1964, thirty-eight people reportedly watched a woman being murdered over thirty-five minutes, yet not one called police.

Here's a disturbing truth about human nature: the more people witness an emergency, the less likely anyone is to help. This is the bystander effect, and it flips our assumptions about safety in numbers completely upside down. When you're alone and see someone collapse, your brain screams 'Act now!' But surrounded by others, a strange paralysis takes hold. Each person assumes someone else will step in, or they look to others for cues on how to react. If everyone's standing still, the emergency must not be that serious, right? Wrong. This diffusion of responsibility has cost lives, and understanding it might just save one.
