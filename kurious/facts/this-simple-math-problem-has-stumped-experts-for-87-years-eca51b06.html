This Simple Math Problem Has Stumped Experts for 87 Years

Pick any number, apply two basic rules, and watch it inevitably spiral down to one — but nobody can prove this always happens.

Here's a game anyone can play: pick any positive number. If it's even, divide by two. If it's odd, multiply by three and add one. Repeat forever. Mathematicians have tested this on numbers up to billions of trillions, and every single one eventually tumbles down to one. Take 7: it becomes 22, then 11, 34, 17, 52, 26, 13, 40, 20, 10, 5, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1. The sequence dances wildly — soaring to unexpected heights before collapsing. German mathematician Lothar Collatz proposed this conjecture in 1937, and despite its kindergarten-simple rules, the greatest mathematical minds have failed to prove it always works. It's been called 'mathematical quicksand' because it traps anyone who tries.
