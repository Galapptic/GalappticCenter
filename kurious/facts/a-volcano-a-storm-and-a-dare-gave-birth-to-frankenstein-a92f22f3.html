A Volcano, a Storm and a Dare Gave Birth to Frankenstein

The first great science-fiction novel began as a party game, played by a teenager stuck indoors during a summer that never came because a volcano on the far side of the world had darkened the sky.

In June 1816, an 18-year-old named Mary Godwin sat in a rented villa above Lake Geneva, listening to thunder roll off the Alps. She was not yet married to the poet Percy Bysshe Shelley, though she had run away with him two years earlier. Within days she would begin a story about a scientist who builds a man from corpses. Published in 1818, Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus would outlive nearly everything written by the famous men in the room with her.
