Diamond Has an Evil Twin Made of Boron and Nitrogen

One form is soft enough to use as makeup, the other can scratch diamond itself—and they're the exact same compound.

In chemistry, there's a strange phenomenon called isoelectronicity: two completely different substances can have the same number of electrons in the same arrangement, making them structural twins despite having nothing in common chemically. Carbon and boron nitride are the textbook example. A carbon atom has six electrons. A boron atom has five, nitrogen has seven—average them together and you get six. This quirk of electron math means boron nitride can exist in almost the exact same crystal structures as carbon, with wildly different results.
