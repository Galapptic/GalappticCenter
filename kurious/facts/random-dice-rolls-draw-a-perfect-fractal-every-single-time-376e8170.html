Random Dice Rolls Draw a Perfect Fractal, Every Single Time

Roll a die and move halfway to a randomly chosen corner, over and over, and pure chance produces an exact geometric masterpiece that no two runs ever fail to reproduce.

Start with three dots on a sheet of paper, the corners of a triangle. Put a pencil anywhere inside. Roll a die: on 1 or 2, move halfway toward the first corner; on 3 or 4, halfway toward the second; on 5 or 6, halfway toward the third. Make a dot. Roll again and repeat from the new dot. After a few thousand rolls, the scattered dots don't form a smudge. They form a crisp, infinitely detailed pattern of triangles nested inside triangles.

This is the chaos game, and its result is the Sierpiński triangle, one of the most famous fractals in mathematics. What makes it startling is the mismatch: every individual step is random, yet the final picture is the same every time, down to the smallest visible hole.
