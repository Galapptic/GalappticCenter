Your Brain Knows a Word Is Wrong Even When Played Backwards

When researchers reversed recordings of speech, they discovered the brain still recognizes familiar words—just processed by completely different neural pathways.

Play a word backwards and it becomes acoustic nonsense—a garbled string of sounds stripped of meaning. Or so it seems. When neuroscientists actually tested what happens in the brain during reversed speech, they found something stranger: the mind keeps trying to decode it, and succeeds more often than it should.
