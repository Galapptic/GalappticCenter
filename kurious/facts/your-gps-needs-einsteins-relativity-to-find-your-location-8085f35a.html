Your GPS needs Einstein's relativity to find your location

Without accounting for tiny time distortions predicted by relativity theory, your GPS would fail within minutes.

Every GPS satellite orbiting Earth experiences time slightly differently than you do on the ground. This sounds like science fiction, but it's measurably real. Einstein predicted that clocks run faster when they're farther from a massive object like Earth, and slower when they're moving quickly. GPS satellites are both high up and moving fast, creating competing effects on their onboard clocks. These atomic clocks tick about 38 microseconds faster per day than identical clocks on Earth's surface. That tiny difference would make your GPS coordinates drift by several miles every single day if engineers didn't correct for it.
