Earth's Longest Mountain Range Sits Where No One Can See It

Winding 65,000 kilometers through every ocean on the planet, a mountain chain four times longer than the Andes, Rockies, and Himalayas combined lies almost entirely hidden beneath the waves.

The longest mountain range on Earth doesn't cross a single country. It doesn't appear on most maps. And until the 1950s, scientists didn't even know it existed. The mid-ocean ridge system stretches roughly 65,000 kilometers around the globe, zigzagging through the Atlantic, Pacific, Indian, and Southern Oceans like the seams on a baseball. If you could drain the oceans, you'd see a continuous spine of volcanic rock—taller than the Alps in places—running through the center of nearly every ocean basin.
