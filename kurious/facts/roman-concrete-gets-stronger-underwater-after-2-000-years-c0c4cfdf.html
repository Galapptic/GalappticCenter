Roman concrete gets stronger underwater after 2,000 years

The Pantheon has stood for two millennia while our bridges crumble in decades. What did Rome know?

Roman concrete structures have survived in seawater for over two thousand years, and they're not just surviving. They're actually getting stronger. The massive piers and breakwaters built during the reign of Augustus still stand firm in Mediterranean harbors while modern concrete structures in similar conditions often need replacing within fifty years. Modern concrete exposed to saltwater deteriorates and crumbles. It cracks, corrodes, and eventually fails. Roman concrete does the opposite. Something in their ancient recipe creates a material that becomes more resilient over time, especially when submerged in seawater. Engineers have been scratching their heads over this paradox for decades.
