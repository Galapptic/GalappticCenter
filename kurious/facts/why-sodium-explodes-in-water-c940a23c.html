Why Sodium Explodes in Water

The metal isn't just reactive—it's so desperate to shed a single electron that it will literally tear water molecules apart to do it.

Drop a pea-sized chunk of sodium into a beaker of water and you'll get a fireball. The metal skitters across the surface, hissing and sparking, then detonates. It looks violent because it is: sodium atoms are ripping electrons off themselves and shoving them onto water molecules so fast the reaction generates enough heat to ignite the hydrogen gas it produces.
