Why Your Brain Refuses to Remember Your Worst Moments

The same neural machinery that helps you forget where you parked can also erase the memory of a car crash—and scientists now understand exactly how it decides what stays and what goes.

You remember the smell of your grandmother's kitchen, the exact words your first love used to break up with you, the song playing when you got your driver's license. But somehow, the details of that terrible car accident in 2019, or the night you spent in the emergency room with chest pains, have faded to almost nothing. This isn't a bug in your memory system. It's a feature—one that neuroscientists have spent decades trying to understand.
