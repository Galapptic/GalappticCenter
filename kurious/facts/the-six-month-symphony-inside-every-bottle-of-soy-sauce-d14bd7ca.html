The Six-Month Symphony Inside Every Bottle of Soy Sauce

That dark splash of soy sauce on your sushi spent half a year being slowly digested by fungi and bacteria working in a biochemical relay race.

The bottle sitting in your refrigerator door holds something ancient. Traditional soy sauce—the kind brewed in wooden barrels across Japan, China, and Korea—takes about 180 days to make. During that time, a carefully orchestrated microbial community breaks down soybeans and wheat into hundreds of flavor compounds, including the glutamates responsible for umami, that savory depth that makes food taste complete.
