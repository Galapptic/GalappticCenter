Ancient Romans paid taxes in honey because it never spoiled

When money could rot but honey couldn't, one liquid gold became more valuable than actual gold.

Honey is the only food that literally never goes bad. Archaeologists have found pots of honey in ancient Egyptian tombs, thousands of years old, still perfectly edible. The Romans knew this. They understood that honey was more than just a sweetener. It was a form of wealth that could last forever. So when tax collectors came knocking, citizens across the empire could hand over jars of honey instead of coins. This wasn't some quirky local custom. It was official imperial policy. Honey became currency, accepted by the state treasury alongside grain, wine, and precious metals. The empire ran on sweetness.
