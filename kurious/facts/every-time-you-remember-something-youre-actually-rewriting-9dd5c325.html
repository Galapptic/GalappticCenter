Every time you remember something, you're actually rewriting it

Your most cherished memory might be half fiction, and it's your own brain doing the creative writing.

You probably think of memory like a video camera, faithfully recording your life for later playback. But here's the unsettling truth: every single time you recall a memory, your brain pulls it apart and rebuilds it from scratch. And during that reconstruction, new details slip in. Details that weren't there before. That childhood birthday party you remember so vividly? The blue balloons, your grandmother's smile, the chocolate cake? Some of those details might have come from photographs you saw later, stories others told you, or just your brain filling in blanks with what seems reasonable. You're not remembering the past. You're imagining a version of it.
