Three-Foot-Tall Humans Once Hunted Komodo Dragons in Indonesia

Standing barely waist-high to modern humans, Homo floresiensis successfully hunted the world's largest living lizards for tens of thousands of years.

In 2003, scientists exploring a limestone cave on the Indonesian island of Flores made a stunning discovery: bones of adult humans who stood just over three feet tall. Nicknamed 'hobbits' after Tolkien's fictional race, Homo floresiensis lived between roughly 100,000 and 50,000 years ago. Despite their diminutive size, these ancient humans weren't prey—they were predators. Archaeological evidence shows they crafted stone tools and hunted the island's megafauna, including juvenile Stegodon elephants and, remarkably, Komodo dragons. These massive lizards can grow over ten feet long and weigh 150 pounds, yet floresiensis apparently found ways to successfully hunt them. It's one of evolution's most surprising David-and-Goliath stories.
