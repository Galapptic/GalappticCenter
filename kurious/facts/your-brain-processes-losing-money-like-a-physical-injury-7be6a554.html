Your brain processes losing money like a physical injury

The sting of a bad investment isn't metaphorical. Your brain literally experiences financial loss as bodily harm.

When you lose money, it genuinely hurts. Not in some poetic sense, but in the actual architecture of your brain. Neuroscientists have discovered that financial loss activates the same neural regions that light up when you touch a hot stove or stub your toe. The anterior insular cortex, a patch of brain tissue buried deep in the folds behind your forehead, processes both kinds of pain identically. This isn't your brain being confused. It's treating the loss of resources as a legitimate threat to your survival, just like physical injury. The sharp pang you feel checking your investment account after a market crash? That's real neural suffering.
