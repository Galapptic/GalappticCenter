The Logical Trap That Makes Your Knowledge Impossible to Verify

A simple principle about how knowledge works has haunted philosophers for centuries—and it suggests that if you don't know what you don't know, you might not know anything at all.

You believe that Paris is the capital of France. You also believe that you know it. But here's the problem: to truly know something, you must also know that you know it. And to know that you know it, you must know that you know that you know it. This infinite regress isn't a word game—it's a genuine logical problem called epistemic closure, and it has been quietly undermining our confidence in knowledge since ancient Greece.
