Vinyl Grooves Thinner Than Hair Store Hours of Music

A human hair is roughly 70 micrometers wide, yet vinyl record grooves measuring just 25-50 micrometers can store entire symphonies in their microscopic undulations.

Pick up a vinyl record and run your finger across it. Those grooves feel smooth, almost imperceptible. That's because each groove is incredibly thin—roughly half the width of a human hair. Yet within these microscopic channels lies an entire album's worth of music. The secret is analog encoding: sound waves are literally carved as physical undulations along a continuous spiral groove. A typical LP contains a single groove spiraling inward for about 500 meters, with variations so tiny they're invisible to the naked eye. When a stylus traces this path, it vibrates thousands of times per second, translating those minuscule wiggles back into the music we hear.
