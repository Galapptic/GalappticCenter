Human Cannonballs Experience Forces That Would Kill Most Animals

Performers routinely survive 9 Gs of acceleration—forces that would cause fatal organ damage in horses, elephants, and most mammals.

When a human cannonball launches from their cannon, they experience roughly 9 Gs of acceleration force in under a second. That's nine times their body weight pressing down on every organ, every blood vessel, every cell. Most animals simply couldn't survive this. Their internal organs would tear loose from their moorings, blood vessels would rupture, and consciousness would vanish instantly. Yet trained performers do this multiple times per week, sometimes for decades. The secret lies partly in human anatomy—our relatively small size, upright posture, and compact organ arrangement actually protect us. Performers also master precise body positioning and muscle tensing techniques that help their tissues resist the crushing forces.
