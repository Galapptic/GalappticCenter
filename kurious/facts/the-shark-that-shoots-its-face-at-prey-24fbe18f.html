The Shark That Shoots Its Face at Prey

In the lightless depths where eyes are useless, the goblin shark evolved a spring-loaded jaw and an electrical sixth sense that detects the heartbeat of hidden prey.

The goblin shark looks like a mistake. Its long, paddle-shaped snout juts forward like a blade, its jaws hang loosely beneath, and its body—flabby, pinkish, almost translucent—seems barely fit for swimming. Fishermen who accidentally haul one up often describe it as grotesque, even nightmarish. But every strange feature serves a purpose. This is a predator exquisitely tuned to hunt in a world without light, where the usual rules of ocean predation simply don't apply.
