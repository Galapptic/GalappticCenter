Your Ears Have a Mysterious Dead Zone

Between the slowest vibration you can feel and the lowest note you can hear lies a strange perceptual void where sound simply doesn't exist.

The human ear can detect a single vibration per second—you'll feel it as a pulse, a rhythm, a beat. And it can perceive 20,000 vibrations per second as a continuous tone, the highest pitch most adults can hear. But somewhere between 1 Hz and roughly 20 Hz, something strange happens. The pulses blur together, yet no pitch emerges. You're left with nothing but a vague sense of pressure or flutter.
