Your Pilot's Control Stick Isn't Actually Connected to Anything

When an Airbus pilot pulls back on the sidestick, no cable tugs at the wings—instead, computers vote on what the plane should do, and they can overrule the human entirely.

In 1988, an Airbus A320 glided over a crowd at an airshow in Habsheim, France. The pilot pulled back hard on the sidestick to climb. The plane didn't respond. It had decided, based on its own calculations of altitude and airspeed, that the pilot was wrong. Three seconds later, it clipped the trees at the end of the runway. The crash killed three passengers and sparked a debate that continues today: when computers fly the plane, who's really in charge?
