The Ancient Game With More Possible Moves Than Atoms Exist

A 2,500-year-old board game played with just two rules generates more unique games than there are atoms in the observable universe—and it took until 2016 for a computer to finally beat the best humans.

The rules of Go can be taught in five minutes. Place a stone on any intersection. Surround your opponent's stones to capture them. That's essentially it. Yet from these two principles emerges a game so vast in its possibilities that mathematicians have calculated it contains more legal board positions than the number of atoms in the observable universe. The comparison isn't poetic exaggeration—it's a statement of literal mathematical fact.
