Roundabouts work because everyone yields to geometry, not rules

Traffic circles succeed not because drivers follow instructions, but because the shape itself makes dangerous choices feel impossible.

Traditional intersections ask humans to remember rules under pressure. Stop signs and traffic lights work only if everyone agrees to obey abstract commands at the exact right moment. Roundabouts take a different approach entirely. They embed the rules into physical space. The circular shape forces your car to slow down and curve. You can't barrel straight through even if you wanted to. The geometry creates a flow pattern where yielding becomes the path of least resistance. Your steering wheel turns because the road curves, not because a sign told you to. The design makes safe behavior feel natural instead of requiring constant decision making.
