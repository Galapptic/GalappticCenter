Archerfish Solve Complex Physics Problems to Snipe Their Prey

These small fish can calculate how light bends through water and adjust their aim accordingly, hitting insects up to two meters away with a precision that rivals trained marksmen.

Archerfish have evolved one of nature's most remarkable hunting techniques. They lurk just below the water's surface, scanning for insects perched on overhanging vegetation. When they spot prey, they spit a powerful jet of water that knocks the insect into the water for an easy meal. But here's what makes this truly extraordinary: light bends when passing between air and water, a phenomenon called refraction. This means the insect isn't actually where it appears to be from underwater. Yet archerfish consistently hit their targets, compensating for this optical distortion with uncanny accuracy. They're essentially solving physics problems in real-time, adjusting their angle of attack based on the insect's true position rather than its apparent one.
