Three-Foot Humans Once Hunted Giant Lizards in Indonesia

Standing barely waist-high to modern humans, Homo floresiensis regularly took down Komodo dragons—the world's largest living lizards—on a remote Indonesian island.

On the Indonesian island of Flores, archaeologists discovered something that rewrote human history. Homo floresiensis, nicknamed 'hobbits,' stood just over three feet tall as fully grown adults. But their small size didn't make them pushovers. These diminutive humans hunted Komodo dragons, giant rats, and small elephants called Stegodon. Their bones, found in Liang Bua cave in 2003, date from roughly 100,000 to 50,000 years ago. Stone tools scattered among the remains show sophisticated hunting strategies. Imagine adults smaller than modern five-year-olds coordinating attacks on ten-foot venomous lizards. Their survival for tens of thousands of years proves that in evolution, brain power beats body size.
