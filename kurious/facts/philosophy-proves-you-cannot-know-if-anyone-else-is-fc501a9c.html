Philosophy Proves You Cannot Know If Anyone Else Is Conscious

Every person you've ever loved might be a philosophical zombie—an empty shell acting conscious without any inner experience whatsoever.

Here's a thought that might keep you up tonight: you cannot prove anyone else is actually conscious. Sure, people talk, laugh, and cry—but how do you know there's genuine experience behind those behaviors? This is the Problem of Other Minds, and it's been haunting philosophers for centuries. You have direct access to exactly one mind: your own. Everything else is inference. When your friend says they feel pain, you see their grimace and hear their words, but you never actually experience their pain. For all you can prove, everyone around you could be a sophisticated automaton, perfectly mimicking consciousness without possessing it.
