Archery Arrows Wiggle Sideways and Still Hit Their Target

Every arrow you've ever seen fly straight was actually bending and flexing wildly around the bow handle in a physics phenomenon that shouldn't work but does.

Here's something that should bother you: when an archer releases an arrow, it's pointing slightly away from the target. The bowstring pushes the arrow forward, but the arrow has to somehow get around the bow's handle, which is directly in its path. Logic says the arrow should slam into the riser and deflect wildly off course. Instead, the arrow bends around the obstacle like a snake slithering past a tree trunk, then straightens out and flies true. This counterintuitive behavior is called the Archer's Paradox, and it's been confusing people since archery began. The arrow essentially wiggles its way to accuracy through a series of rapid oscillations that look chaotic but are perfectly predictable.
