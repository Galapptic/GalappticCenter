The Seal Wives Who Could Never Find Their Way Home

In Scottish and Irish folklore, seals were not always seals—they were shapeshifters whose stolen skins trapped them in human marriages for decades.

Along the cold, rocky coasts of Scotland, Ireland, Iceland, and the Faroe Islands, fishermen once told stories of seals that weren't quite seals. These creatures, called selkies, could shed their skins and walk on land as beautiful humans. But the transformation came with a terrible cost: without their sealskin, they could never return to the sea.

The word "selkie" comes from the Scots word for seal, and the stories have been told in the Northern Isles of Scotland—Orkney and Shetland—for centuries. But similar tales appear across the Celtic and Nordic world, wherever grey seals haul themselves onto beaches and watch the shore with dark, almost human eyes.
