Spanish Conquistadors Weaponized an Aztec God's Return Prophecy

When Hernán Cortés arrived in Mexico in 1519, some Aztecs may have wondered if their exiled feathered serpent god had finally come home.

Quetzalcoatl was one of the most important gods in Mesoamerican culture—a feathered serpent deity associated with wind, learning, and creation itself. According to Aztec mythology, Quetzalcoatl had once ruled as a priest-king in the legendary city of Tollan before being tricked into disgrace and exile. As he departed eastward across the sea, he supposedly vowed to return one day to reclaim his throne. When Spanish conquistadors arrived from the east in 1519, wearing strange armor and riding horses the Aztecs had never seen, the timing seemed almost supernatural. This coincidence—or the story of it—would become one of history's most consequential cases of mythology meeting military conquest.
