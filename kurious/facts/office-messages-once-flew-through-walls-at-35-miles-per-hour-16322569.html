Office messages once flew through walls at 35 miles per hour

Before the internet, buildings had secret highways in their walls where capsules shot through tubes like bullets.

Imagine dropping a message into a container on your desk and watching it vanish into a tube built into the wall. Seconds later, it arrives three floors up with a satisfying thunk. This was pneumatic tube technology, and it turned early 20th century buildings into physical internets. Banks, hospitals, department stores, and offices installed miles of tubing through their walls. You'd write a memo, seal it in a cylindrical capsule, and whoosh. Air pressure did the rest. The system was so fast and reliable that some buildings kept using it well into the 2000s, long after email existed.
