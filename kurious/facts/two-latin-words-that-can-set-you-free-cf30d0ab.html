Two Latin Words That Can Set You Free

A legal phrase dating to medieval England still forces governments worldwide to justify every arrest they make.

In 1679, English shoemaker Francis Jenkes gave a speech criticizing King Charles II—and was immediately thrown in prison without charge. His case helped spark the Habeas Corpus Act that same year, enshrining one of history's most powerful legal protections: the government must prove it has lawful reason to hold you, or let you go.
