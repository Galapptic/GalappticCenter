The Tiny Crystal That Killed the Vacuum Tube

In 1947, three physicists replaced a hot, fragile glass bulb with a sliver of germanium no bigger than a pencil eraser—and accidentally made the smartphone possible.

Early computers were monsters. ENIAC, switched on in 1945, filled an entire room, weighed 30 tons, and ran on 18,000 vacuum tubes—glass bulbs that amplified electrical signals but burned out constantly and guzzled power. Engineers at Bell Labs knew there had to be a better way.
