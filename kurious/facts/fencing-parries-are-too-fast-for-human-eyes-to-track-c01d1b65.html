Fencing Parries Are Too Fast for Human Eyes to Track

Elite fencers execute defensive moves in under 200 milliseconds—faster than the blink of an eye and at the very edge of what human reflexes can achieve.

When two Olympic fencers face off, the decisive moments happen in a blur. The flick of a blade, the snap of a parry—these actions occur so quickly that even trained referees can't follow them with the naked eye. Modern fencing relies entirely on electronic scoring systems because human perception simply isn't fast enough.
