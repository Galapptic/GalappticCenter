The Internet Turns Nice People Into Trolls

The same person who holds doors open for strangers will ruthlessly attack them online, and psychologists now understand exactly why.

Something strange happens when people go online. Polite, reasonable individuals suddenly transform into aggressive, hostile versions of themselves. This phenomenon is so widespread that researchers gave it a name: the online disinhibition effect. Behind a screen, stripped of face-to-face consequences, ordinary people say things they'd never dream of uttering in person. Comment sections become battlegrounds. Social media threads devolve into vicious attacks. The transformation isn't limited to a few bad actors—studies show most internet users have engaged in behavior online they wouldn't replicate offline. The anonymity creates a psychological separation between our real identity and our digital one, essentially giving us permission to misbehave.
