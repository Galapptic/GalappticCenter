Ancient Rotting Fish Created the World's First Flavor Enhancer

The same fermentation process that transforms anchovies into pungent fish sauce accidentally discovered umami two thousand years before scientists could explain it.

Fish sauce sounds simple: pack small fish with salt and wait. But those months of fermentation create something magical. Tiny anchovies, layered with sea salt in massive vats, slowly break down as their own enzymes digest the proteins into amino acids. The result is a clear, amber liquid with an intensity that punches far above its weight. A few drops transform bland rice into something deeply savory. This isn't just salty—it's umami, the fifth taste that makes your mouth water and signals "protein ahead" to your brain. From Vietnamese pho to Caesar salad dressing, fermented fish sauce has infiltrated cuisines worldwide, proving that controlled rot can be delicious.
