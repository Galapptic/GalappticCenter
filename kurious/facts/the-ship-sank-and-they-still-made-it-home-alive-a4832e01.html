The Ship Sank and They Still Made It Home Alive

When Ernest Shackleton's ship was crushed by Antarctic ice in 1915, his 27 men were stranded on frozen sea with no radio, no rescue coming, and 800 miles of ocean between them and civilization.

The Endurance had been trapped for ten months, held fast in the Weddell Sea's pack ice. On October 27, 1915, the pressure finally won. The ice crushed the hull like a fist closing around a walnut. Shackleton and his crew grabbed what they could—tents, sledges, lifeboats—and climbed onto the frozen sea to watch their ship die. It sank a month later.
