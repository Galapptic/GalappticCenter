Frida Kahlo Painted Herself 55 Times to Own Her Narrative

After a horrific bus accident left her bedridden for months, an 18-year-old Frida Kahlo began painting self-portraits using a mirror mounted above her bed—transforming personal suffering into revolutionary art.

Frida Kahlo created 55 self-portraits throughout her life, making up about one-third of her entire body of work. This wasn't vanity—it was survival. In 1925, a devastating bus accident left the young Mexican artist with a broken spinal column, collarbone, pelvis, and numerous other injuries. Confined to bed for months, her mother installed a mirror on the canopy above her. Kahlo later explained: 'I paint myself because I am so often alone and because I am the subject I know best.' Each portrait became an unflinching examination of identity, pain, heritage, and womanhood. She didn't beautify or soften—she confronted viewers with her famous unibrow, facial hair, and intense gaze, refusing to conform to conventional beauty standards.
