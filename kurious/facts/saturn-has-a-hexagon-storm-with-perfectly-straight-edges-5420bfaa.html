Saturn has a hexagon storm with perfectly straight edges

A jet stream wider than Earth forms six perfectly straight sides at Saturn's north pole.

Saturn's north pole hosts one of the strangest weather patterns in our solar system. A massive jet stream whips around the pole at over 200 miles per hour, and instead of forming a circle like hurricanes on Earth, it creates a nearly perfect hexagon. Each of the six sides stretches about 9,000 miles long, making the whole structure wider than Earth itself. The Voyager spacecraft first spotted this bizarre shape in 1981, and when Cassini returned decades later, the hexagon was still there, spinning with the same precise geometry. It has persisted for at least forty years, possibly much longer.
