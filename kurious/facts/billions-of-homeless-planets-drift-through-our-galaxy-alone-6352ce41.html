Billions of Homeless Planets Drift Through Our Galaxy Alone

For every star you see in the night sky, there may be dozens of dark, frozen worlds wandering between them—planets forever lost in the cosmic void.

Not all planets orbit stars. Rogue planets drift through the galaxy untethered, moving through interstellar space in eternal darkness. These cosmic wanderers were likely born in normal solar systems but got kicked out through gravitational chaos—close encounters with massive planets or passing stars that sent them flying into the void. Astronomers estimate there could be billions, possibly trillions, of these homeless worlds in our Milky Way alone. Some may outnumber stars themselves. Without a parent star, these planets receive no sunlight and their surfaces are frozen wastelands. Yet they still carry the heat from their formation deep in their cores, slowly cooling over billions of years as they drift silently through space.
