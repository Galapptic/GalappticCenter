Ancient Greek Philosopher Proved You Cannot Actually Move

A philosopher born 2,500 years ago constructed logical arguments so airtight that mathematicians needed two millennia to figure out where he went wrong—if he even did.

Imagine racing a tortoise. You give it a head start. To catch it, you must first reach where it was—but by then, it's moved ahead. You reach that new spot, but it's moved again. This chase continues infinitely, so logically, you can never catch a tortoise. This is just one of Zeno of Elea's famous paradoxes from around 450 BCE. Another argues that to walk across a room, you must first cross half the distance, then half of what remains, then half again—an infinite series of tasks. How can you complete infinite steps in finite time? You walk across rooms daily, yet Zeno's logic seems unbreakable.
