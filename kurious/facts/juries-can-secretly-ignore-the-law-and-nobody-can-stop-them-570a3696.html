Juries Can Secretly Ignore the Law and Nobody Can Stop Them

Even if a defendant is obviously guilty, twelve ordinary citizens can simply refuse to convict—and the judge, prosecutor, and entire legal system are powerless to override them.

Here's a legal secret that courts don't advertise: juries can't be punished for their verdicts. If twelve people decide a law is unjust, they can vote 'not guilty' regardless of the evidence. This is jury nullification—the power to judge both the facts and the law itself. Judges won't tell you this exists. Lawyers can't argue for it. In many jurisdictions, even mentioning nullification during jury selection gets you dismissed. But once those deliberation room doors close, jurors answer to no one. A guilty verdict can be appealed; an acquittal cannot. This means when a jury says 'not guilty,' that's the final word—even if everyone knows the defendant did it.
