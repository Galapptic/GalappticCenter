A Puzzle About Seven Bridges Invented an Entire Branch of Math

When Leonhard Euler proved it was impossible to walk across every bridge in a Prussian city exactly once, he accidentally created graph theory and planted the seeds of topology.

In the 1700s, the Prussian city of Königsberg had a pleasant problem. The Pregel River flowed through town, splitting around an island and creating four distinct land masses. Seven bridges connected them, and the locals had a favorite Sunday puzzle: could you walk through the city crossing each bridge exactly once, never doubling back?

Nobody could do it. But nobody could prove it was impossible either. The puzzle seemed too simple to be interesting mathematics—just a game for bored citizens. Then it reached Leonhard Euler.
