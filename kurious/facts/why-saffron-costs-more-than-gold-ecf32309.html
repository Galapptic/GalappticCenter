Why Saffron Costs More Than Gold

Each pound of this crimson spice requires 75,000 hand-picked flowers—and the harvest window lasts just two weeks.

At current market rates, high-quality saffron sells for roughly $5,000 per pound. Gold hovers around $30,000 per pound, but saffron is measured by the gram in kitchens, and the comparison holds when you weigh what's actually used: a pinch of saffron threads against a flake of gold leaf. By that measure, the spice often wins. A single gram of top-grade Iranian or Kashmiri saffron can cost $10 to $15, while a gram of 24-karat gold runs about $75—but gold leaf is beaten so thin that the same visual impact costs less. The real question isn't whether saffron is expensive. It's why anyone decided it was worth the trouble.
