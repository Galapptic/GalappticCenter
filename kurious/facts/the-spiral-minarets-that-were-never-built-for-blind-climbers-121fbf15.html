The spiral minarets that were never built for blind climbers

A popular story says Iraq's corkscrew minarets were shaped so blind callers to prayer could climb them by feel, but the real link between blindness and minarets is stranger and better documented.

Rising 171 feet (52 m) above the plain of central Iraq, the Malwiya minaret of Samarra looks like a giant seashell set on end. A ramp about 7.5 feet (2.3 m) wide winds counterclockwise around the outside five times, with no railing, until it reaches a small pavilion at the top. The name comes from the Arabic for "twisted" or "coiled."

A tidy explanation often travels with photos of it: the spiral was designed so that blind muezzins, the men who call Muslims to prayer, could find their way up by touch alone. It is an appealing story. No historical source supports it. The truth involves blind muezzins, but for a very different reason.
