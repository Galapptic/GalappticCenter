Saffron Costs More Than Gold by Weight

Each crocus flower produces just three hair-thin threads, making this crimson spice worth up to $10,000 per pound.

Saffron comes from the stigmas of Crocus sativus, a delicate purple flower that blooms for just one week each autumn. Here's the catch: each flower produces only three tiny red threads. To harvest a single pound of dried saffron, workers must hand-pick around 75,000 flowers. That's roughly the size of a football field's worth of crocuses. The harvest window is incredibly narrow—flowers must be picked at dawn before the sun wilts them. Every thread is plucked by hand, dried carefully, and sorted by quality. This labor-intensive process explains why saffron regularly sells for $500 to $5,000 per pound, sometimes exceeding gold's value ounce for ounce.
