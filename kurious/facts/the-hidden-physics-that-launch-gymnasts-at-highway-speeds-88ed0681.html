The Hidden Physics That Launch Gymnasts at Highway Speeds

When Simone Biles leaves the floor, she's traveling faster than Usain Bolt at full sprint—and the science behind it took a century to perfect.

A gymnast performing a tumbling pass doesn't look like a physics experiment. But the moment her feet leave the ground, she becomes a projectile governed by the same equations that describe cannonballs and rockets. Elite gymnasts launch themselves at speeds approaching 30 feet per second—about 20 miles per hour—and reach heights of nearly 10 feet above the floor. The sport has become an arms race between human muscle and Newtonian mechanics, fought on a surface engineered to the millimeter.
