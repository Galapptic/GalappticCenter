A 600 Year Old Book Nobody Can Read Still Baffles Experts

The Voynich Manuscript looks like a textbook from another world, written in a language that might not exist.

Somewhere in Yale's rare book library sits a medieval manuscript that has defeated every attempt to read it for six centuries. The Voynich Manuscript contains about 240 pages of flowing script in an alphabet nobody recognizes, accompanied by bizarre illustrations of unknown plants, naked women in strange plumbing systems, and circular astronomical diagrams. It's written with confidence and consistency, as if the author expected someone to understand it. Carbon dating places the vellum between 1404 and 1438. The ink came shortly after. But what it says remains completely unknown. Cryptographers who cracked enemy codes in World War II tried and failed. Modern computers have tried and failed.
