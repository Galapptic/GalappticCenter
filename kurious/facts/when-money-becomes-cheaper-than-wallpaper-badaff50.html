When Money Becomes Cheaper Than Wallpaper

In 2008, Zimbabwe printed a 100-trillion-dollar bill that couldn't buy a bus ticket, so citizens papered their walls with cash instead.

By November 2008, prices in Zimbabwe were doubling every 24 hours. A loaf of bread that cost 2 million Zimbabwean dollars in the morning might cost 4 million by dinner. Workers rushed to spend their wages within minutes of receiving them. Those who waited even a day watched their money become worthless. The government responded by printing larger and larger denominations—culminating in a 100-trillion-dollar note that remains the highest denomination ever issued by any country.
