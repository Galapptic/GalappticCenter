The Ocean's Cold Fire Burns Without Heat

The blue glow washing onto beaches isn't reflection or pollution—it's half a trillion organisms screaming in light.

When waves break along certain coastlines at night, they shatter into electric blue. The phenomenon looks supernatural, but it's purely biological: single-celled dinoflagellates, each smaller than a grain of sand, responding to disturbance with a flash of cold light. A single liter of seawater during a bloom can contain millions of them.
