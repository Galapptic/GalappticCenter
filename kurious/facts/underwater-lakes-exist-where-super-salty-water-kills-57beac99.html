Underwater Lakes Exist Where Super-Salty Water Kills Everything

At the bottom of the ocean, there are actual lakes with shorelines and waves—filled with water so salty it's lethal to most creatures that accidentally swim into them.

Deep beneath the ocean's surface, you'll find something utterly bizarre: lakes. Not metaphorical ones—actual pools of water with distinct shorelines, sitting on the seafloor. These brine pools form when ancient salt deposits dissolve into seawater, creating fluid so dense it settles into depressions like mercury pooling in a dish. The salt concentration can reach eight times normal seawater levels. When submarines or remotely operated vehicles approach these pools, they encounter a visible boundary where the brine meets regular ocean water. Fish or crabs that accidentally swim into these underwater lakes experience 'brine shock'—the extreme salinity essentially pickles them alive. Their bodies often litter the edges like morbid beach debris.
