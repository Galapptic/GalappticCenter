Friction Can Generate Heat So Intense It Melts Metal

The simple act of rubbing two surfaces together can unleash temperatures exceeding 1,500°C—hot enough to liquefy steel.

Every time you rub your hands together on a cold day, you're witnessing friction converting motion into warmth. But scale this up dramatically, and friction becomes a force capable of melting the toughest metals. When two surfaces slide against each other at high speeds or under extreme pressure, their kinetic energy transforms into thermal energy at the contact points. Train wheels screeching on rails, industrial machinery grinding without lubrication, or spacecraft plunging through atmosphere—all demonstrate friction's terrifying heating potential. At microscopic contact points called asperities, temperatures can spike thousands of degrees in milliseconds, causing metal to glow red, then white, then simply flow like water.
