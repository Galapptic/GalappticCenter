Venice Sinks Eight Millimeters Every Year Into the Sea

The romantic city of canals is slowly drowning, with some scientists warning it could be uninhabitable within a century.

Venice, the iconic Italian city built on 118 small islands, is sinking at roughly eight millimeters per year into the Adriatic Sea. That might sound tiny, but over decades it adds up dramatically. The city has already dropped about 25 centimeters over the last century. Combined with rising sea levels from climate change, Venice faces an existential threat. The famous Piazza San Marco now floods over 100 times per year, compared to just a handful of times a century ago. Venetians have grown grimly accustomed to wooden walkways appearing throughout the city during acqua alta—the high water events that transform streets into canals and ground floors into wading pools.
