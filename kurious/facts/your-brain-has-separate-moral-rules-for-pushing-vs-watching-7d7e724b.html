Your Brain Has Separate Moral Rules for Pushing vs Watching

Most people will flip a switch to kill one person and save five, but almost no one will push someone off a bridge to achieve the exact same outcome.

In 1967, philosopher Philippa Foot posed a question that would become the most famous thought experiment in modern ethics. A runaway trolley is heading toward five workers. You can pull a lever to divert it onto another track, where it will kill one person instead. Most people say yes—pull the lever.

Then came the twist. In 1985, philosopher Judith Jarvis Thomson added a new scenario: you're standing on a footbridge above the tracks. The only way to stop the trolley is to push a large stranger off the bridge and onto the tracks below. His body will halt the trolley. Five saved, one dead—same math as before.
