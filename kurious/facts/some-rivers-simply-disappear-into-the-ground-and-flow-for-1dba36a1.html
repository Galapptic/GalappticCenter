Some rivers simply disappear into the ground and flow for miles

Rivers vanish into stone and travel secretly beneath your feet, sometimes reappearing miles away as entirely different rivers.

In certain landscapes, a perfectly normal river just stops. Not gradually, not by drying up, but by plunging straight into the earth and disappearing completely. These vanishing rivers flow into caves and cracks in limestone bedrock, continuing their journey through pitch black underground channels. The Lost River in Indiana does exactly this, sinking into the ground and traveling beneath the surface for miles. Above ground, the riverbed sits empty and dry. Below, the water rushes through a hidden world of tunnels that no sunlight ever touches. Eventually, many of these rivers emerge again, sometimes joining other waterways or creating entirely new springs miles from where they vanished.
