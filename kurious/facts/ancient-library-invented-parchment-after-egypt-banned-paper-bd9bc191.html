Ancient Library Invented Parchment After Egypt Banned Paper Exports

When Egypt cut off the papyrus supply to sabotage a rival library, Pergamum's scribes turned animal skins into the writing material that would outlast Rome itself.

In the second century BCE, the city of Pergamum in western Anatolia built a library so magnificent that Egypt's Ptolemaic rulers saw it as a threat. The Library of Alexandria was the ancient world's greatest repository of knowledge, and the Ptolemies intended to keep it that way. So they banned papyrus exports to Pergamum, hoping to starve the upstart collection of writing material.
