The Letter J Is Just a Fancy I That Got Out of Hand

Medieval scribes accidentally invented an entire letter while trying to make their handwriting look prettier.

The letter J didn't exist in ancient alphabets. Romans used I for both the vowel sound in 'it' and the consonant sound in 'yes.' Medieval scribes, copying manuscripts by candlelight, started adding decorative tails to the letter I when it appeared at the beginning of words or as a numeral. This flourish helped readers distinguish between uses. What started as mere ornamentation gradually became recognized as something different entirely. The curvy-tailed I slowly developed its own identity, eventually earning its own spot in the alphabet. It wasn't formally recognized as a separate letter until the 16th century, making J one of the youngest letters in the English alphabet.
