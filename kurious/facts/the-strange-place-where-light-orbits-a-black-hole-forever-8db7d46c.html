The Strange Place Where Light Orbits a Black Hole Forever

At exactly the right distance from a black hole, a beam of light can circle endlessly like a car stuck on a cosmic roundabout, forming a perfect ring that Einstein's equations predicted but seemed too strange to be real.

In 1915, Albert Einstein finished his general theory of relativity and made a prediction that troubled even him. Massive objects, he calculated, don't just pull on other objects—they warp the very fabric of space and time. Light, despite having no mass, must follow these curves. A beam passing near the Sun would bend slightly. A beam passing near something far more massive would bend dramatically. And at a certain distance from the most extreme objects in the universe, light would bend so severely it would loop back on itself, tracing a perfect circle that could persist for eternity.
