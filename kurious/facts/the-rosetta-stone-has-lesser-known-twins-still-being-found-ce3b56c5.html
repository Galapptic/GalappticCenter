The Rosetta Stone Has Lesser-Known Twins Still Being Found

The famous Rosetta Stone wasn't unique—ancient Egyptian priests created multiple copies of the same decree, and archaeologists keep discovering its forgotten siblings.

When the Rosetta Stone was discovered in 1799, scholars assumed it was a one-of-a-kind treasure. But it was actually mass media, ancient Egyptian style. The stone records the Decree of Memphis from 196 BCE, issued by priests honoring Pharaoh Ptolemy V. This decree was carved onto multiple stelae and distributed across Egypt's major temples. The Rosetta Stone just happened to be the one that survived intact enough to crack the hieroglyphic code. Other fragments of identical or similar decrees have surfaced over the centuries, some spending thousands of years buried in temple ruins, forgotten storage rooms, or beneath desert sands—waiting to rejoin their famous sibling in history.
