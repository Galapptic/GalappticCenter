Nature Counts Using the Same Numbers as Spiral Galaxies

From the petals on a daisy to the arms of the Milky Way, the universe keeps repeating the same mysterious number sequence.

Count the petals on flowers and you'll notice something strange. Lilies have 3, buttercups have 5, delphiniums have 8, marigolds have 13, and daisies often have 21, 34, or 55. These aren't random numbers—they're all part of the Fibonacci sequence, where each number equals the sum of the two before it: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, and so on. Look at a pine cone's scales or a sunflower's seeds, and you'll find them arranged in spirals. Count those spirals in each direction, and you'll get consecutive Fibonacci numbers. Even spiral galaxies follow similar mathematical patterns. Somehow, nature discovered this sequence billions of years before mathematicians.
