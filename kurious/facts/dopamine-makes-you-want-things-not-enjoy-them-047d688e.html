Dopamine Makes You Want Things Not Enjoy Them

That rush you feel isn't happiness—it's your brain screaming at you to chase something you might never actually enjoy getting.

For decades, we called dopamine the 'pleasure chemical,' assuming it flooded our brains when we felt good. But neuroscientists discovered something stranger: dopamine surges before you get the reward, not after. It's the chemical of anticipation, craving, and motivation—not satisfaction. When you scroll endlessly through social media, dopamine isn't making you happy. It's making you want to keep scrolling, convinced the next post might be amazing. This distinction matters enormously. Dopamine creates the itch, not the scratch. The actual pleasure you feel when eating chocolate or hearing good news involves different neurotransmitters entirely, like endorphins and serotonin. Dopamine just made sure you pursued it in the first place.
