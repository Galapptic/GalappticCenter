Japanese carpenters build furniture that locks together without a single nail

Wooden temples in Japan survive earthquakes for centuries because their beams twist together like three-dimensional brain teasers.

Japanese carpenters have been creating joints so intricate they look like wooden magic tricks for over a thousand years. These connections use no nails, no screws, no glue. Just wood fitting into wood with such precision that the pieces lock together permanently. Some joints involve twenty or thirty different cuts, each one angled perfectly so the pieces can only go together one way. Once assembled, they're stronger than if you'd used metal fasteners. The Hōryū-ji temple near Nara has stood since 607 AD, held together entirely by these impossible joints.
