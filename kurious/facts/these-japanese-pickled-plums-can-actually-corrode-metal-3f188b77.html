These Japanese Pickled Plums Can Actually Corrode Metal

Umeboshi plums are so intensely acidic that leaving them in contact with aluminum or iron will cause visible corrosion over time.

Umeboshi are Japanese pickled plums with a reputation that precedes them. One bite delivers an explosion of sourness so intense it makes lemons seem mild. These wrinkled, ruby-red fruits have been a staple of Japanese cuisine for over a thousand years, but they hide a surprising secret. Leave an umeboshi touching aluminum foil or sitting in an iron container, and you'll eventually notice corrosion forming. The extreme acidity, combined with high salt content from the pickling process, creates a surprisingly aggressive chemical environment. Traditional Japanese households know never to store umeboshi in metal containers—only glass or ceramic will do. This humble pickled fruit packs enough punch to literally eat through cookware.
