Neanderthals Were Strategic Hunters Who Took Down Giant Prey

Far from brutish loners, Neanderthals used sophisticated wooden spears and coordinated group tactics to kill animals ten times their size.

For decades, scientists assumed Neanderthals were scavengers, picking at carcasses left by better predators. Then came the evidence: healed wounds on mammoth bones that matched spear thrusts, butchery marks on elephant remains at kill sites across Europe, and eight wooden spears found preserved in a German coal mine. Neanderthals weren't scavenging. They were hunting—and hunting big.
