The Tiles That Mathematicians Said Were Impossible

In 1974, a physicist proved you could cover an infinite floor with just two shapes and never once create a repeating pattern.

For centuries, tilemakers knew a simple truth: if you wanted to cover a floor without gaps, your pattern would eventually repeat. Squares, triangles, hexagons—they all lock into grids that cycle endlessly. Then Roger Penrose, an Oxford mathematician and physicist, found something that shouldn't exist: a set of tiles that cover an infinite plane yet never, ever repeat.
