Your Mechanical Watch Contains a Spring Beating 28,800 Times Per Hour

The hairspring in a quality mechanical watch is thinner than a human hair yet can oscillate billions of times over decades without wearing out.

Inside every mechanical watch lives a component so delicate it's nearly invisible: the hairspring. This impossibly thin spiral of metal—typically just 0.05mm wide—coils and uncoils up to 28,800 times per hour, creating the steady rhythm that drives your watch's hands. Unlike batteries that drain or circuits that fail, this purely mechanical component relies on physics alone. The hairspring works with the balance wheel in a dance of stored and released energy, converting the power from your wound mainspring into precise, measurable intervals. What's remarkable is that this continuous oscillation causes virtually no wear. The spring flexes within its elastic limit, meaning it returns to its original shape billions of times without degrading.
