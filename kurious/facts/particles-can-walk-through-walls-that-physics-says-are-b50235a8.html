Particles Can Walk Through Walls That Physics Says Are Impossible

Subatomic particles routinely escape prisons that classical physics declares absolutely inescapable, passing through solid barriers as if they were ghosts.

Imagine throwing a ball at a wall and watching it appear on the other side without breaking through. Absurd for baseballs, but routine for electrons. Quantum tunneling allows particles to penetrate barriers they shouldn't have enough energy to overcome. In classical physics, if you lack the energy to climb a hill, you're stuck. But quantum mechanics plays by different rules. Particles aren't just tiny balls—they're probability waves spread across space. When that wave encounters a barrier, most of it bounces back, but a portion leaks through to the other side. The thinner the barrier, the more likely the particle tunnels through. It's not magic—it's the fundamental weirdness of reality at its smallest scales.
