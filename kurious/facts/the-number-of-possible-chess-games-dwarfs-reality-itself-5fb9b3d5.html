The Number of Possible Chess Games Dwarfs Reality Itself

There are more ways a chess game can unfold than there are atoms in the observable universe—and it's not even close.

The observable universe contains roughly 10^80 atoms. That's a 1 followed by 80 zeros—a number so large it loses all meaning. Every star, planet, grain of sand, and molecule of water. Every atom in every cell of every living thing that has ever existed. Add them all together, and you get that impossibly vast figure.

Now consider chess. Two players, 32 pieces, 64 squares, and a set of rules unchanged since the 15th century. The number of possible games? At least 10^120. That's not just bigger than the number of atoms—it's a trillion trillion trillion trillion times bigger. The comparison breaks mathematics itself.
