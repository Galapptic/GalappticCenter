The Border Drawn in Five Weeks That Split Millions

A British lawyer who had never been to India was given just 36 days to draw a line that would divide 88 million people.

On August 17, 1947, two days after independence, India and Pakistan finally learned where they ended and the other began. The border had been kept secret to prevent mass migration before the handover. It didn't work. Within months, roughly 15 million people crossed the new line in both directions—the largest mass migration in human history.
