A Giant Cannon Ended a Thousand Years of Roman History

A Hungarian engineer built a 27-foot bronze monster that fired half-ton stone balls, demolishing walls that had repelled invaders for a millennium.

In 1453, Sultan Mehmed II faced a seemingly impossible challenge: Constantinople's legendary Theodosian Walls had protected the Byzantine capital for over a thousand years. Previous sieges had failed spectacularly. Then a Hungarian engineer named Orban offered his services. He constructed an enormous bronze cannon, roughly 27 feet long, capable of hurling stone projectiles weighing up to 600 pounds. The weapon was so massive it required sixty oxen and hundreds of men to transport. After weeks of bombardment, the walls that had defied countless armies finally crumbled. On May 29, 1453, Ottoman forces poured through the breaches, ending the Byzantine Empire and transforming world history forever.
