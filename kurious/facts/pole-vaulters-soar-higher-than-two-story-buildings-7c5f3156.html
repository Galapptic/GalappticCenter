Pole Vaulters Soar Higher Than Two-Story Buildings

Athletes routinely launch themselves over 20 feet into the air using nothing but a bendy stick and physics.

When Armand Duplantis cleared 6.24 meters in 2024, he sailed higher than most two-story buildings—roughly equivalent to a giraffe standing on another giraffe. The secret weapon? A flexible fiberglass pole that transforms running speed into vertical flight. Vaulters sprint down the runway at nearly 22 miles per hour, plant their pole, and convert all that horizontal momentum into upward launch. The pole bends dramatically under their weight, storing energy like a massive spring, then releases it explosively as they shoot skyward. It's essentially a human catapult system that took decades of engineering and athletic evolution to perfect.
