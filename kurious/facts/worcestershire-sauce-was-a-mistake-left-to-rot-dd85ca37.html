Worcestershire Sauce Was a Mistake Left to Rot

The world's most famous fermented condiment was considered a complete failure and nearly thrown away before becoming a global sensation.

In 1835, two English chemists named John Lea and William Perrins were commissioned to recreate a sauce recipe that a nobleman had brought back from Bengal. They mixed anchovies, tamarind, vinegar, molasses, and various spices, but the result was absolutely vile—harsh, pungent, and inedible. Disgusted, they shoved the barrel into their cellar and forgot about it. Two years later, while cleaning out the basement, they rediscovered the barrel and decided to taste it before dumping it out. To their shock, the fermentation process had transformed the offensive mixture into something extraordinary: a complex, savory elixir that would become Worcestershire sauce.
