The Ancient Word Trick That Reads the Same Both Ways

A single word carved into a Roman fountain nearly 2,000 years ago still works as a puzzle today—and mathematicians now use the same principle to detect errors in DNA sequences.

In 79 CE, the eruption of Mount Vesuvius buried Pompeii under volcanic ash. When archaeologists began excavating the city centuries later, they found something strange scratched into a column: SATOR AREPO TENET OPERA ROTAS. The five words formed a perfect square, readable left to right, right to left, top to bottom, and bottom to top. The Romans had discovered palindromic magic—and they weren't the first.
