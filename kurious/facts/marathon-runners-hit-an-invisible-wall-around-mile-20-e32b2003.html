Marathon Runners Hit an Invisible Wall Around Mile 20

The fastest marathoners in the world actually run their slowest miles at the end, not because they're tired, but because their muscles have literally run out of fuel.

Watch any marathon closely and you'll notice something strange: even elite runners slow dramatically in the final six miles. This isn't simply fatigue or mental weakness. Around mile 18-20, runners encounter what athletes call 'hitting the wall' or 'bonking.' Their muscles have exhausted their primary fuel source—glycogen, the stored form of glucose. The human body can store roughly 2,000 calories of glycogen in muscles and liver, but a marathon burns approximately 2,600 calories. When those reserves empty, your body must switch to burning fat, a much slower and less efficient process. Suddenly, legs feel like concrete, pace drops dramatically, and the final miles become a survival test.
