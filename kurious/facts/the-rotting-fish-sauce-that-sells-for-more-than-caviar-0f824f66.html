The Rotting Fish Sauce That Sells for More Than Caviar

A condiment that smells like decomposing flesh has become one of the world's most expensive ingredients, with aged bottles fetching hundreds of dollars from chefs who swear nothing else comes close.

The first thing visitors notice at a traditional fish sauce factory is the smell. It hits you from a hundred meters away—a pungent, almost violent aroma of decay that triggers every instinct to turn back. Inside wooden warehouses, thousands of clay urns and massive vats contain layers of small fish and sea salt, slowly liquefying under the tropical sun. This process, unchanged for over two thousand years, produces one of the world's most important seasonings. And in certain markets, the finest examples now cost more per ounce than Beluga caviar.
