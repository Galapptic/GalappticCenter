Persian Artists Painted Details Too Small for the Naked Eye

Medieval Persian painters created images so intricate that scholars today need magnifying glasses to see what they actually depicted.

Between the 13th and 17th centuries, artists in Persia developed a painting tradition unlike anything in Europe. Working on manuscripts for royal courts, they created scenes where a single figure's robe might contain dozens of individual flowers, each with its own shading and petals—rendered at a scale smaller than a grain of rice.
