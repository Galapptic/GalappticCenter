The Bayeux Tapestry Tells Its Epic Story in 230 Feet of Thread

Nearly a millennium before graphic novels existed, medieval embroiderers created one of history's longest visual narratives—and it survived wars, revolutions, and a plan by the Nazis to steal it.

Stretched end to end, the Bayeux Tapestry would span most of a football field. Its 230 feet of linen chronicle one of the most consequential events in European history: the Norman Conquest of England in 1066. But calling it a tapestry is technically wrong. The images weren't woven into the fabric—they were stitched onto it, scene by scene, by hands whose identities remain unknown.
