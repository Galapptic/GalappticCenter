Brown Dwarfs Are Cosmic Failures That Glow Invisibly

Too massive to be planets but too small to ignite nuclear fusion, brown dwarfs spend eternity glowing in light human eyes can never see.

Brown dwarfs are the universe's cosmic underachievers. They form exactly like stars do, collapsing from clouds of gas and dust, but they never accumulate enough mass to ignite hydrogen fusion in their cores. Stars need about 80 times Jupiter's mass to sustain fusion. Brown dwarfs fall short, ranging from roughly 13 to 80 Jupiter masses. Without fusion's fierce energy, they can't produce visible light like our Sun. Instead, they glow dimly in infrared, radiating heat leftover from their formation and slow gravitational contraction. They're warm objects cooling in the cosmic darkness, visible only to infrared telescopes. Scientists call them 'failed stars,' though they're spectacular in their own right.
