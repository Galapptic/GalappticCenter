How Texting Rewired the Teenage Brain for Friendship

Before 2005, most teenagers had never maintained a friendship in real-time while physically alone—a concept that now defines adolescent social life.

In 1999, the average American teenager sent zero text messages. By 2011, they sent and received 3,417 per month—roughly one every six minutes of their waking lives. This wasn't just a new way to say hello. It was a fundamental restructuring of how young people understood, maintained, and experienced friendship itself. The shift happened so fast that researchers are still mapping its consequences.
