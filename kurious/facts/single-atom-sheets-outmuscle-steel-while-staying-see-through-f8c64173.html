Single Atom Sheets Outmuscle Steel While Staying See-Through

A material so thin it's literally two-dimensional can support an elephant balanced on a pencil point without breaking.

Graphene is essentially a single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a honeycomb pattern—and when we say single layer, we mean it's literally one atom thick. That's about a million times thinner than a human hair. Yet this impossibly thin material is roughly 200 times stronger than steel by weight. You could stretch a graphene sheet across a coffee cup, and it would support a car before tearing. The mind-bending part? Despite this incredible strength, graphene is nearly transparent, absorbing only about 2.3% of light that passes through it. It's like having an invisible suit of armor made from pencil lead's molecular cousin.
