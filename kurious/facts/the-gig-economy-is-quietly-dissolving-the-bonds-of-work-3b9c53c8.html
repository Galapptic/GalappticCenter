The Gig Economy Is Quietly Dissolving the Bonds of Work

For the first time in a century, millions of workers have no boss, no benefits, and no guarantee they'll earn anything tomorrow.

In 2023, roughly 36 percent of American workers—about 59 million people—performed some form of gig work. They drove for Uber, delivered groceries through Instacart, coded websites on Upwork, or walked dogs via Rover. Most had no employment contract. No health insurance from their platform. No paid sick days. No pension contributions. They were entrepreneurs, legally speaking, even if they earned $12 an hour delivering burritos in the rain.

This isn't a glitch in modern capitalism. It's a feature. The gig economy represents the most significant restructuring of work since the factory system emerged in 19th-century Manchester. And its consequences are only beginning to unfold.
