Philosophy Proves You Cannot Know the Sun Will Rise Tomorrow

Every sunrise you've ever witnessed is logically worthless as evidence for tomorrow's dawn, according to one of philosophy's most devastating arguments.

Every morning, you assume the sun will rise. It always has, so it always will, right? Not so fast. The problem of induction reveals that no amount of past experience can logically guarantee future events. Just because something happened a billion times doesn't mean it must happen again. You're making a leap of faith every single day. The argument isn't that the sun won't rise—it probably will. The problem is that you cannot prove it will using logic alone. Your entire reasoning rests on assuming that the future will resemble the past. But why should it? That assumption itself requires proof, which requires another assumption, creating an infinite regress.
