Your Brain Literally Cannot See Objects Moving at Extreme Speed

A bullet fired at you would be completely invisible to your eyes until the moment it stopped—your brain simply can't process motion that fast.

Your eyes aren't cameras recording everything in real-time. They have a biological refresh rate, and your brain needs time to process what you're seeing. When objects move faster than roughly 550 miles per hour, they exceed what neuroscientists call your "temporal resolution threshold." Your retinal cells simply can't capture enough light from the object, and your brain can't stitch together a coherent image. This is why you never actually see a bullet in flight, why hummingbird wings blur into ghostly halos, and why a fastball from a major league pitcher seems to vanish mid-throw. Your visual system literally gives up trying to track it.
