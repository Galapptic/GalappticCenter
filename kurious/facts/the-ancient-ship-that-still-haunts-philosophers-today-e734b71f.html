The Ancient Ship That Still Haunts Philosophers Today

Your body replaces nearly every atom you're made of within a decade, which raises an uncomfortable question: are you the same person who started reading this sentence?

In ancient Athens, citizens preserved a wooden ship they believed had carried the hero Theseus back from Crete after slaying the Minotaur. For centuries, as planks rotted, Athenian craftsmen replaced them one by one. Eventually, not a single original piece remained. The Greek historian Plutarch, writing around 75 CE, posed what seemed like a simple question: was it still the same ship?

Twenty centuries later, philosophers still can't agree on the answer. And the question has grown teeth, because what's true of wooden ships turns out to be true of you.
