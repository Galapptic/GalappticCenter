Lenticular clouds hover motionless while wind screams through them

Mountains create clouds that don't move an inch even as air blasts through them at 100 mph.

On certain days near mountain ranges, you can spot clouds that look like stacked dinner plates or flying saucers hovering in the sky. These are lenticular clouds, and they have a strange superpower. While wind tears through them at breathtaking speeds, sometimes exceeding 100 miles per hour, the clouds themselves don't budge. They stay locked in place like painted scenery. It's as if nature installed invisible anchors in the sky. The wind passes through, the moisture constantly cycles, but the cloud shape remains eerily stationary. It's one of the atmosphere's most mesmerizing optical tricks.
