Lake Baikal Freezes So Clear You Can See 130 Feet Down

Each winter the world's deepest lake turns into a sheet of glass so transparent that people walk across it and see the lake bed dozens of feet below their boots.

In February, Lake Baikal in southern Siberia stops looking like a lake. Its surface hardens into ice up to 5 feet (1.5 m) thick, and in many places that ice is nearly flawless: dark, glassy and full of trapped bubbles that hang like frozen coins. Look down through it in early spring and you can sometimes see as far as 130 feet (40 m) into the water, one of the greatest depths of visibility measured in any freshwater lake.
