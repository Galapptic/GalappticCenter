This Metal Spoon Melts in Your Tea Then Reforms

Gallium spoons dissolve into silvery puddles in hot beverages, only to solidify again when the liquid cools—a party trick that breaks every expectation about metal.

Gallium looks like aluminum but harbors a secret: it melts at just 29.76°C (85.6°F). That's slightly below body temperature and well under the temperature of hot tea. Hand someone a gallium spoon, watch them stir their drink, and witness their confusion as the utensil liquefies into a shimmering metallic blob. The tea doesn't need to boil—anything above a warm bath will do. When the liquid cools or you fish out the gallium and let it sit, it resolidifies into a usable spoon again. Unlike ice melting into water that disperses, liquid gallium stays together as a dense, mirror-like puddle, making this reversible trick endlessly repeatable.
