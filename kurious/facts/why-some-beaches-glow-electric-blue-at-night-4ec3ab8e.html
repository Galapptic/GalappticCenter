Why Some Beaches Glow Electric Blue at Night

The eerie blue light washing ashore isn't pollution or a prank—it's billions of single-celled organisms lighting up when waves disturb them.

On certain summer nights, waves along the California coast, the Maldives, and Puerto Rico's bioluminescent bays crash in neon blue. The glow comes from dinoflagellates—tiny plankton, each smaller than a grain of sand. When water movement stresses their cell membranes, a chemical reaction fires off: the enzyme luciferase oxidizes a molecule called luciferin, releasing photons. One organism is invisible. A few million per liter turn the surf into liquid starlight.
