Chinese Painters Mastered Perspective a Millennium Before Leonardo

While European art historians long credited Renaissance masters with inventing visual depth, scroll painters in Song Dynasty China were already guiding viewers through mountains that receded convincingly into mist—using techniques the West wouldn't discover for another thousand years.

In 1435, the Italian architect Leon Battista Alberti published a treatise that supposedly changed art forever. His mathematical system for linear perspective—using a single vanishing point to create the illusion of depth—became the foundation of Western painting. Art history textbooks still treat it as a watershed moment, the birth of realistic space in two dimensions. But a thousand years earlier, Chinese landscape painters had already solved the problem of depth. They just did it differently.
