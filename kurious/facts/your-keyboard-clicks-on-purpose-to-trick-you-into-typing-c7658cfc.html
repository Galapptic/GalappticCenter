Your keyboard clicks on purpose to trick you into typing faster

The sound your keyboard makes isn't an accident. It's a carefully engineered psychological trick.

Modern keyboards don't need to make any sound at all. The electronics inside register your keypress long before anything physically clicks. Yet engineers spend millions designing the perfect clicking sound for each key. They hire acoustic engineers and run countless tests. Why? Because that click isn't about the keyboard. It's about your brain. The sound creates something called haptic feedback, a sensory confirmation that tricks your mind into thinking you're typing faster and more accurately than you actually are. Without it, typing feels mushy and uncertain, even when the keyboard works perfectly.
