Giant Methane Bubbles Can Swallow Ships Without Warning

Massive underwater eruptions of methane gas can reduce water density so dramatically that vessels plunge straight to the ocean floor in seconds.

Imagine sailing calm waters when suddenly your ship drops like a stone. This nightmare scenario can actually happen thanks to methane hydrates—frozen methane trapped in ocean sediments. When these deposits destabilize, they release enormous plumes of gas bubbles. Here's the terrifying physics: water filled with bubbles has drastically lower density than normal seawater. Ships float because they displace water heavier than themselves. But when the ocean becomes a frothy mixture of water and gas, that buoyancy vanishes. The vessel simply falls through the aerated water, potentially sinking so fast that no distress signal can be sent. These eruptions happen without warning over known methane deposits worldwide.
