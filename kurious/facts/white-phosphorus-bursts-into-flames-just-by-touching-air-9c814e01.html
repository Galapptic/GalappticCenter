White Phosphorus Bursts Into Flames Just by Touching Air

This waxy element is so reactive it must be stored underwater because mere contact with oxygen causes it to spontaneously ignite and produce eerie, glowing smoke.

White phosphorus is one of chemistry's most dramatic elements. This pale, waxy solid doesn't need a spark, match, or any ignition source—it catches fire all by itself when exposed to air. The moment oxygen touches its surface, a violent chemical reaction begins. Heat builds rapidly until flames erupt, producing dense white smoke that seems to glow with an otherworldly light. This ghostly luminescence is why phosphorus got its name, from the Greek word meaning 'light-bearer.' The element burns at around 30°C, barely above room temperature, making it extraordinarily dangerous to handle. Scientists must store it completely submerged in water to prevent catastrophic fires.
