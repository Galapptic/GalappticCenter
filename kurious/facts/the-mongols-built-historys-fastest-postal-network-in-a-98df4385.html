The Mongols Built History's Fastest Postal Network in a Decade

While medieval European kings still relied on slow foot messengers, Genghis Khan's empire created a 50,000-mile relay system that could move a letter across Asia in just two weeks.

In the 1230s, the Mongol Empire accomplished something no government had done before: a continental postal system that stretched from Korea to Eastern Europe. They called it the Yam, and it made the empire's vast territory governable. A message that would take months by normal travel could cross the entire network in roughly two weeks.
