Medieval monks wrote entire bibles using ink made from wasps' nests

The most sacred texts in Christianity were written with a substance that began as insect architecture.

Open a medieval bible and you're looking at words written in iron gall ink, the standard writing fluid for over a thousand years. Monks didn't buy this ink from a store. They made it themselves using a recipe that required collecting strange brown balls from oak trees. These weren't fruit. They were galls, abnormal growths that trees produce when certain wasps lay their eggs inside them. The wasp larvae secrete chemicals that make the tree tissue swell into a protective chamber. Monks harvested these galls, crushed them up, and transformed them into the very ink that would preserve scripture.
