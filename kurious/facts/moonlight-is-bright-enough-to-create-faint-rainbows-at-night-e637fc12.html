Moonlight is bright enough to create faint rainbows at night

On certain foggy nights, the Moon paints ghostly rainbows that your eyes can barely see.

Rainbows don't need sunlight. They just need light, any light, splitting through water droplets in the air. On nights when the Moon is nearly full and shining brightly, it can create its own rainbows called moonbows or lunar rainbows. These ghostly arcs appear most often near waterfalls or after rain showers, just like their daytime cousins. The catch? They're so faint that most people never notice them. Your eyes struggle to detect colors in dim light, so moonbows usually look white or pale gray. But they're there, tracing the same colorful spectrum across the night sky that sunshine creates by day.
