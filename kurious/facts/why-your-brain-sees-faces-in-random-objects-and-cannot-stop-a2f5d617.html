Why Your Brain Sees Faces in Random Objects and Cannot Stop

That electrical outlet isn't staring at you, but your brain has already decided it has eyes, a nose, and possibly an attitude.

The Virgin Mary appears in a grilled cheese sandwich. A house on your street looks perpetually surprised. The front of your car seems to be grinning. You know none of these things actually have faces—and yet you see them anyway, instantly and automatically, before your conscious mind can intervene. This isn't a glitch in human perception. It's a feature, and understanding why it exists reveals something profound about how your brain constructs reality from chaos.
