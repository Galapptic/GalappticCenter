Every shuffle you make is probably a universe first

The number of possible card arrangements exceeds the atoms in our galaxy, making your last shuffle almost certainly unique in history.

Pick up a standard 52-card deck and shuffle it thoroughly. Congratulations—you've almost certainly created an arrangement that has never existed before in human history and likely never will again. This isn't exaggeration; it's mathematics. The number of possible orderings is 52 factorial (written 52!), meaning 52 × 51 × 50 × ... × 2 × 1. This equals roughly 8 × 10^67—an 8 followed by 67 zeros. To put this in perspective, there are only about 10^80 atoms in the observable universe. Even if every person who ever lived shuffled a deck once per second since the Big Bang, we'd barely scratch the surface of possible arrangements.
