Basilisk Lizards Sprint Across Water Like Tiny Dinosaurs

These reptiles can run up to 15 feet across water at nearly 5 miles per hour, earning them the nickname 'Jesus Christ lizard.'

The common basilisk lizard of Central and South America has mastered something that seems impossible: running across water without sinking. When threatened by predators, these green-brown reptiles rear up on their hind legs and sprint across ponds and streams at remarkable speeds. They can cover distances up to 15 feet before their momentum fails and they have to swim. Young basilisks are especially good at this trick because they're lighter, sometimes running 30 feet or more. This escape strategy is so effective that locals call them 'Jesus Christ lizards' after the biblical miracle. The lizards use this ability to flee from snakes, birds, and other predators lurking in the rainforest.
