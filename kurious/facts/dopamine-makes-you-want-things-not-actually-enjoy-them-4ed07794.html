Dopamine Makes You Want Things Not Actually Enjoy Them

That rush you feel anticipating a purchase brings more dopamine than owning it ever will.

You've probably heard dopamine called the 'pleasure chemical,' but neuroscience tells a different story. Dopamine actually drives wanting, seeking, and craving—not the enjoyment itself. When you're scrolling endlessly, refreshing your email, or anticipating that package delivery, that's dopamine at work. The actual pleasure you feel when something good happens? That's largely driven by different brain chemicals like endorphins and endocannabinoids. This explains a frustrating human experience: why the anticipation often feels more exciting than the reality. Your brain evolved to make you pursue rewards relentlessly, not to make you satisfied once you get them. Dopamine is your brain's motivational engine, keeping you hungry and hunting.
