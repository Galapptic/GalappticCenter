How Submarines See Everything Using Nothing But Sound

In the pitch-black depths where light cannot reach, submarines navigate, hunt, and hide by listening to echoes that travel farther than any radar could ever reach.

Radar revolutionized warfare above the waves, but it's useless underwater. Radio waves die within meters of the surface. Light fades to nothing by 1,000 meters down. Sound, however, travels beautifully through water—about 4.3 times faster than through air, and across distances that can span entire ocean basins. This simple physics gave birth to sonar, the technology that lets submarines perceive their environment in total darkness.
