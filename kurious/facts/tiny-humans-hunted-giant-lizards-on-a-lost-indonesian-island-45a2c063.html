Tiny Humans Hunted Giant Lizards on a Lost Indonesian Island

Standing just over three feet tall, Homo floresiensis stalked and killed Komodo dragons—the world's largest living lizards—turning the expected predator-prey relationship completely upside down.

In 2003, a team of Australian and Indonesian archaeologists broke through the floor of a limestone cave on the remote Indonesian island of Flores. What they found would rewrite human history: the bones of an entirely new species of hominin, one that had survived until remarkably recent times. The researchers named it Homo floresiensis, but the world quickly gave it another name—the Hobbit.

These diminutive humans stood about 106 centimeters tall, roughly the height of a modern four-year-old, with brains the size of a chimpanzee's. Yet the archaeological evidence surrounding their bones told a story that seemed almost impossible: these tiny hominins had hunted, butchered, and eaten Komodo dragons, the fearsome apex predators that still roam Flores today.
