A Botched Assassination Created the Insanity Defense

In 1843, a Scottish woodturner tried to kill the British Prime Minister, shot his secretary instead, and accidentally gave us one of law's most controversial protections.

Daniel M'Naghten was convinced the Tories were persecuting him. The Scottish woodturner had traveled to London with one target: Prime Minister Robert Peel. On January 20, 1843, he spotted a man leaving Peel's residence at 10 Downing Street and fired a pistol into his back. But M'Naghten had made a fatal error. The man bleeding on the pavement wasn't Peel—it was Edward Drummond, the Prime Minister's private secretary. Drummond died five days later.
