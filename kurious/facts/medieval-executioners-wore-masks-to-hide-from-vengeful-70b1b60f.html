Medieval executioners wore masks to hide from vengeful ghosts

The hood wasn't about mystery. It was protection from something far more terrifying than angry crowds.

We imagine medieval executioners in hoods and masks, sinister figures at the scaffold. But here's the twist: most executioners didn't wear masks at all. The iconic hood is largely a myth, popularized by later fiction and film. When masks were worn, the reasons had nothing to do with ghosts. In most medieval towns, everyone knew exactly who the executioner was. He lived among them, often for generations. The job ran in families. Anonymity wasn't just impossible, it was never the point. So where did this haunting image come from, and what were executioners really afraid of?
