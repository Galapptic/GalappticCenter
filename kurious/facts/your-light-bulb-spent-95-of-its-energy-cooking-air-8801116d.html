Your light bulb spent 95% of its energy cooking air

Every time you flipped a switch, your bulb was secretly a tiny heater that happened to glow.

Thomas Edison's light bulb was a miracle that banished darkness from homes worldwide. But here's the catch: incandescent bulbs are spectacularly inefficient at their actual job. For every 100 watts of electricity you pump into one, only about 5 watts comes out as visible light. The other 95 watts becomes heat, radiating into your room as invisible infrared radiation. You were essentially paying to run a small space heater that produced a bit of light as a side effect. This is why touching a lit bulb burns your fingers, and why rooms with many incandescent bulbs feel stuffy and warm.
