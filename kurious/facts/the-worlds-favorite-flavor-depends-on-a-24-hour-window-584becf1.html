The World's Favorite Flavor Depends on a 24-Hour Window

Every vanilla bean in your ice cream exists because someone hand-pollinated an orchid flower before it wilted forever.

Vanilla comes from the seed pods of a climbing orchid native to Mexico. Here's the catch: each flower opens just once, for roughly twelve to twenty-four hours. Miss that window, and you miss your chance entirely. In the wild, specific Mexican bees called Melipona handle pollination during this brief opening. But vanilla grown anywhere else lacks these native pollinators. That means every commercial vanilla bean—whether grown in Madagascar, Tahiti, or Indonesia—requires human intervention. Workers walk the orchid vines daily, watching for newly opened blooms, then carefully transfer pollen by hand using tiny sticks or thorns. It's painstaking work that makes real vanilla one of the world's most labor-intensive crops.
