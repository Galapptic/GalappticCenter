Caves Breathe Like Living Creatures Through Giant Air Currents

Deep underground, massive caves inhale and exhale thousands of cubic meters of air daily, creating winds strong enough to extinguish torches.

Caves aren't stagnant holes in the ground—they're dynamic breathing systems. Temperature differences between cave interiors and the outside world drive powerful air currents that flow through underground passages like breath through lungs. In winter, warm cave air rises and escapes through upper entrances while cold air rushes in below. Summer reverses this pattern completely. Some caves generate winds exceeding 40 kilometers per hour at narrow passages. Explorers have long noticed these currents, using candle flames to navigate toward hidden exits. The phenomenon is so reliable that cavers call entrance passages 'blowholes' when they feel that telltale rush of subterranean air.
