Vermeer May Have Traced His Masterpieces Using Hidden Optics

The photographic precision that made Johannes Vermeer famous might have been literally projected onto his canvases using mirrors and lenses, a secret he took to his grave.

Johannes Vermeer's paintings possess an almost supernatural quality—soft focus effects, precise perspective, and light that behaves exactly like photography. For centuries, art historians wondered how a 17th-century Dutch painter achieved what seems impossible by eye alone. The answer may lie in a device called the camera obscura, a darkened room or box where light passes through a small hole or lens, projecting an inverted image onto a surface. Evidence suggests Vermeer traced these optical projections directly onto his canvases, essentially painting over photographs before photography existed. His hometown of Delft was a center for lens-grinding, and his neighbor was the pioneering microscopist Antonie van Leeuwenhoek.
