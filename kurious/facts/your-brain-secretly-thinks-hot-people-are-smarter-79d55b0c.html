Your Brain Secretly Thinks Hot People Are Smarter

Without any evidence whatsoever, your mind automatically assigns higher intelligence to people it finds attractive.

Here's an uncomfortable truth: when you meet someone attractive, your brain quietly whispers that they're probably smart too. This cognitive shortcut is called the "halo effect," and it happens completely automatically. Within milliseconds of seeing a face, you've already made judgments about that person's intelligence, trustworthiness, and competence—largely based on how symmetrical and conventionally attractive they appear. Studies consistently show that attractive people are rated as more intelligent, even when their actual abilities are identical to less attractive individuals. Teachers do it with students. Employers do it in interviews. Juries do it with defendants. You're doing it right now without realizing it. Your visual system and your judgment systems are more tangled than you'd ever want to admit.
