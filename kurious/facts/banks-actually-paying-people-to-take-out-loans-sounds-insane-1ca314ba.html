Banks Actually Paying People to Take Out Loans Sounds Insane

In several countries, mortgage holders have received monthly checks from their banks instead of making payments.

Normally, you borrow money and pay interest for the privilege. But starting in 2009, several central banks flipped this logic entirely. They introduced negative interest rates, essentially charging commercial banks to park money rather than lend it out. The goal was forcing banks to push money into the economy. In some cases, this trickled down to consumers in bizarre ways. Danish homeowners with adjustable-rate mortgages actually received quarterly payments from their banks. Instead of owing interest, the bank owed them. It sounds like a glitch in the financial matrix, but it was deliberate monetary policy attempting to stimulate sluggish economies after the 2008 financial crisis and again during the pandemic.
