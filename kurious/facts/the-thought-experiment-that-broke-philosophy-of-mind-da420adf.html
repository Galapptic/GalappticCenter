The Thought Experiment That Broke Philosophy of Mind

In 1980, a philosopher locked himself in an imaginary room and accidentally proved that we can't explain understanding without already knowing what it is.

John Searle was 48 when he published "Minds, Brains, and Programs" in Behavioral and Brain Sciences. His Chinese Room thought experiment became one of the most cited—and most attacked—arguments in cognitive science. It wasn't meant to prove machines can't think. It was meant to show something stranger: that we don't actually know what we mean by "understanding."
