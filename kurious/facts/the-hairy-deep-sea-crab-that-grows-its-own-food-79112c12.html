The Hairy Deep-Sea Crab That Grows Its Own Food

Near scalding vents on the Pacific seafloor, a crab survives by waving its arms to fertilize the bacterial garden growing in its own fur.

In 2005, scientists diving in the submersible Alvin on the Pacific-Antarctic Ridge, about 900 miles (1,500 km) south of Easter Island, found a pale, blind crab with arms covered in silky blond bristles. They named it Kiwa hirsuta, after a Polynesian sea goddess, and the press quickly called it the yeti crab.
