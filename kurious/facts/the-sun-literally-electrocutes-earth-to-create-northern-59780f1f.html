The Sun Literally Electrocutes Earth to Create Northern Lights

Every aurora is essentially our planet getting zapped by a hundred million billion watts of electrical current from space.

The aurora borealis isn't just pretty lights—it's Earth's atmosphere getting electrocuted by the Sun. Our star constantly blasts charged particles called solar wind into space at over a million miles per hour. When this electrified stream slams into Earth's magnetic field, it doesn't just bounce off. Instead, it generates massive electrical currents that flow along magnetic field lines toward the poles. These currents carry around 100 million billion watts of power—roughly equivalent to 50,000 power plants firing simultaneously. When that electricity hits the upper atmosphere, it excites oxygen and nitrogen atoms, which release energy as the ghostly green, red, and purple curtains we call the Northern Lights.
