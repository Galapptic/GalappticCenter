A Teaspoon of Neutron Star Would Weigh Six Billion Tons

The most extreme objects in the visible universe pack the mass of our entire Sun into a sphere roughly 12 miles across.

When massive stars die, they don't go quietly. After exhausting their nuclear fuel, stars between 8 and 25 times our Sun's mass collapse catastrophically. The core implodes in milliseconds, crushing protons and electrons together to form neutrons. What remains is a neutron star—a sphere containing more mass than our Sun squeezed into roughly the diameter of a city like Manhattan. The density defies imagination: a sugar-cube-sized chunk would weigh about as much as Mount Everest. Gravity at the surface is roughly 200 billion times stronger than Earth's. If you somehow stood on one, you'd be flattened to a film of atoms instantly.
