Why Yellowstone's Old Faithful Keeps Its Appointments

The world's most punctual geyser hasn't missed a scheduled eruption in over a century, and the secret lies in a hidden plumbing system that works like a giant pressure cooker.

Every 90 minutes or so, a crowd gathers on the wooden benches at Yellowstone's Upper Geyser Basin. They check their watches. Rangers have posted a prediction time, accurate to within ten minutes. And then, right on cue, a column of boiling water rockets 130 feet into the Wyoming sky. Old Faithful has been doing this since at least 1870, when the Washburn Expedition gave it its name. It has never stopped.

But geysers are not supposed to be predictable. They're violent, chaotic features—underground explosions of steam and superheated water. Most of the thousand-odd geysers on Earth erupt at random intervals, or go dormant for decades, or simply stop forever. So why does Old Faithful, and a handful of others at Yellowstone, run like clockwork? The answer lies in an invisible network of underground pipes, chambers, and constrictions that regulate pressure with remarkable precision.
