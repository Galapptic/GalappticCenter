Vinyl records wobble on purpose so the needle stays in

That wobble you see when a record spins isn't a flaw—it's actually keeping the music playing.

Watch a vinyl record spin and you'll often see it wobbling up and down, like a gentle wave moving around the platter. Your first instinct might be to flatten it out or worry something's wrong. But here's the thing. That wobble isn't always a defect. Engineers discovered that a slight vertical movement actually helps the stylus track the groove more reliably. The needle needs to maintain contact with both walls of the groove as it spirals inward, and a bit of controlled flex in the system prevents the stylus from bouncing out when it hits imperfections. It's a counterintuitive truth. Perfect rigidity can actually sound worse.
