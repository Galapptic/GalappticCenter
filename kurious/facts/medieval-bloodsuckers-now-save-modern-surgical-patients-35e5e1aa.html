Medieval Bloodsuckers Now Save Modern Surgical Patients

After being dismissed as quackery for over a century, leeches received FDA approval in 2004 as legitimate medical devices.

For thousands of years, doctors applied leeches to patients for nearly every ailment imaginable. By the late 1800s, medicine finally recognized bloodletting as mostly useless, and leeches became symbols of medical ignorance. But here's the twist: modern surgeons have brought them back. Today, medicinal leeches work in hospitals worldwide, particularly after reconstructive surgeries involving reattached fingers, ears, and skin flaps. When veins are too damaged to drain blood properly, tissues become dangerously congested. Leeches solve this by doing what they've always done—feeding. Their gentle suction removes pooled blood while their saliva prevents clotting, keeping tissue alive until veins naturally regenerate.
