Invisible Money Now Moves Billions Every Single Day

A mysterious pseudonymous programmer created a currency that exists only as math, and it's now worth more than most countries' entire economies.

In 2009, someone calling themselves Satoshi Nakamoto released Bitcoin into the world and then vanished. No government backs it. No bank controls it. It exists purely as encrypted data spread across millions of computers worldwide. Yet today, hundreds of billions of dollars worth of Bitcoin changes hands daily, invisible transactions racing through the internet. The technology underneath, called blockchain, creates an unbreakable chain of records that everyone can verify but nobody can alter. It's money reduced to pure mathematics—cryptographic puzzles that prove ownership without revealing identity. What started as an experiment among cypherpunks became a financial force that central banks now scramble to understand and regulate.
