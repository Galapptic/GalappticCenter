The Liquid That Crawls Out of Its Own Container

Below −271°C, helium stops being a liquid in any normal sense—it becomes a quantum fluid that defies gravity, flows without friction, and escapes through gaps no ordinary substance could penetrate.

In 1938, Soviet physicist Pyotr Kapitsa and, independently, John Allen and Don Misener in Cambridge made a discovery that seemed to break physics. They cooled helium-4 below 2.17 Kelvin (−270.98°C) and watched it transform into something unprecedented: a superfluid with zero viscosity. Pour it into a cup, and it climbs the walls, flows over the rim, and drips off the bottom until the container is empty.
