Ancient Highways Sparked History's First Global Trade Wars

Before stock markets existed, merchants waged economic warfare across 4,000 miles of desert, mountains, and steppe, creating the world's first truly connected economy.

The Silk Road wasn't a single road at all—it was a vast network of trade routes stretching from China to the Mediterranean, connecting empires that barely knew each other existed. Starting around 130 BCE, merchants transported silk, spices, precious metals, and gems across treacherous terrain. But they carried something even more valuable: ideas. Buddhism traveled from India to China along these paths. Papermaking and gunpowder moved westward. Diseases spread too, including plagues that would reshape civilizations. The routes passed through some of Earth's most forbidding landscapes—the Gobi Desert, the Pamir Mountains, and the Iranian Plateau. Yet for over 1,500 years, traders risked everything for the enormous profits waiting at the journey's end.
