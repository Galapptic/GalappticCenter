The Poison-Wrapped Nut That Takes 15 Steps to Eat

The cashew you snack on came from a fruit that looks like a bell pepper, wrapped in a shell containing the same chemical found in poison ivy—and someone had to roast it by hand.

Every cashew you've ever eaten arrived at your mouth through a gauntlet of fire, acid, and careful human hands. Unlike almonds or walnuts, which can be cracked and consumed almost immediately, cashews must be coaxed from shells that would blister your skin on contact. The process is so labor-intensive that for centuries, cashews remained a regional curiosity in their native Brazil. Today they're a $7 billion global industry—but the journey from tree to trail mix still involves techniques that would look familiar to sixteenth-century Portuguese colonizers.
