Jupiter's auroras shine 100 times brighter than Earth's northern lights

The same phenomenon that paints Earth's skies green becomes an ultraviolet monster at Jupiter.

Jupiter has auroras just like Earth does. Both planets create shimmering curtains of light near their poles when charged particles collide with their atmospheres. But Jupiter's version makes our northern lights look like a flickering candle next to a spotlight. These Jovian auroras glow about 100 times more intensely than anything we see from Alaska or Norway. They also operate on a completely different scale. Earth's auroras might stretch across a few hundred miles. Jupiter's auroral ovals are each larger than our entire planet. And they never turn off. While Earth's light shows come and go with solar activity, Jupiter's auroras rage continuously, day and night.
