Would You Plug Into a Machine That Guarantees Perfect Bliss

Most people refuse simulated paradise, revealing that happiness isn't actually what we want most.

Philosopher Robert Nozick proposed a devastating thought experiment in 1974. Imagine a machine that could give you any experience you desired—winning Nobel Prizes, finding true love, achieving enlightenment. Your brain would be stimulated to feel genuine pleasure, indistinguishable from reality. You'd never know you were plugged in. The catch? None of it would be real. You'd float in a tank while supercomputers fed your neurons perfect bliss. Nozick asked a simple question: would you plug in for life? Most people instinctively say no. This gut reaction reveals something profound about human values. We don't just want to feel happy—we want our happiness to mean something.
