Your Brain Manufactures Memories of Events That Never Happened

Scientists can implant entirely fictional childhood memories in adults with alarming ease, and your brain will defend them as absolutely real.

Here's an unsettling truth: many of your cherished memories might be completely fabricated. Your brain doesn't store memories like a video recorder. Instead, it reconstructs them each time you remember, filling gaps with assumptions, suggestions, and pure imagination. The terrifying part? You can't tell the difference. False memories feel identical to real ones—complete with vivid details, strong emotions, and absolute certainty. Your brain doesn't flag them as fake. In studies, people confidently recall childhood events that researchers completely made up, describing elaborate details of things that never occurred. Your memory isn't a reliable witness; it's a creative storyteller that believes its own fiction.
