The Gutenberg Press Sparked Europe's First Information War

A single invention caused more political chaos in 50 years than centuries of warfare, turning ink into the most dangerous weapon in Europe.

Around 1440, Johannes Gutenberg perfected movable type printing in Mainz, Germany. What seemed like a simple improvement in book production triggered an unprecedented explosion of information. Before Gutenberg, a single Bible took scribes years to copy by hand. His press could produce hundreds of copies in weeks. Within decades, printing shops spread across Europe, churning out not just religious texts but political pamphlets, scientific treatises, and scandalous critiques of authority. For the first time in history, ideas could spread faster than authorities could suppress them. The printing press didn't just share knowledge—it weaponized it, launching what historians now recognize as Europe's first true information war.
