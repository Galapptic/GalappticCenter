Medieval spiral staircases twisted clockwise to trap right-handed attackers

The direction of a staircase twist could mean the difference between life and death in castle warfare.

Walk up the stone stairs of almost any medieval castle and you'll notice something odd. The spiral always twists clockwise as you ascend. This wasn't an accident or aesthetic choice. It was a calculated defensive weapon. Castle architects designed these staircases to give defenders above a crucial advantage during combat. An attacker climbing upward would find their sword arm pinned against the central stone column, unable to swing freely. Meanwhile, the defender fighting downward had their right arm on the open side of the curve, with full range of motion to strike.
