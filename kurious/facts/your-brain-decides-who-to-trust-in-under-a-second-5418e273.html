Your Brain Decides Who to Trust in Under a Second

Before you've even processed someone's name, your brain has already judged their trustworthiness based purely on their eye-to-chin ratio.

In roughly the time it takes to blink, your brain has already decided whether a stranger seems trustworthy. Research shows faces with wider eyes and smaller, narrower chins trigger automatic trust responses. This happens so fast it's essentially unconscious—about 100 milliseconds, far quicker than deliberate thought. The effect is remarkably consistent across different people and cultures. We don't choose to make these judgments; they happen to us. Scientists believe this rapid assessment system evolved when quick decisions about friend or foe meant survival. Today, these snap judgments influence everything from who we befriend to who gets elected, often without our awareness.
